## Introduction

Rapamycin, a macrolide compound, extends lifespan in multiple model organisms by inhibiting the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway, a central regulator of growth and metabolism [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor]. This intervention is one of the most robust pharmacological lifespan extenders discovered to date [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin], making it a cornerstone of geroscience research. Understanding the consistency and determinants of its efficacy is critical for translational aging biology and for identifying the key nodes of longevity regulation.

Prior quantitative syntheses have established the foundational evidence. A seminal meta-analysis by Harrison et al. (2009) aggregated data from multiple mouse strains and demonstrated a significant mean lifespan extension [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin]. Subsequent work, such as that by Swindell (2017), expanded the scope to include additional species and strains, confirming a positive overall effect while highlighting substantial between-study heterogeneity [CIT:swindell-2017-rapamycin]. These syntheses provided essential estimates of the average effect size [EFFECT=...] and its variability [CI=...].

Despite these advances, specific moderators of rapamycin's effect remain insufficiently resolved. Prior reviews have noted potential variations by sex, genetic background, and dosing protocol [CIT:swindell-2017-rapamycin], but the statistical power to formally test these interactions was often limited by the number of included studies [K_STUDIES]. Furthermore, the field has seen a steady accrual of new primary studies since the last major synthesis, incorporating novel strains, refined delivery methods, and lifespan data from alternative models [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor]. This expanding evidence base creates an opportunity to test prior conclusions with greater precision and to explore additional covariate dimensions, such as the timing of treatment onset.

The present systematic synthesis and meta-analysis is designed to extend and refine this prior landscape. It does not seek to establish the mere existence of a rapamycin effect, which is well-documented [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin]. Instead, it aims to rigorously stress-test the consistency of that effect across a pre-specified set of biological and methodological moderators. The analysis will use a larger, updated corpus and employ contemporary multi-level models to better account for clustering effects within studies (e.g., multiple treatment arms from a single publication).

This work is explicitly scoped to quantify effect modification; it does not aim to adjudicate among the myriad molecular mechanisms downstream of mTOR inhibition. Its non-goals include evaluating rapamycin's effects on age-related diseases in isolation from lifespan or synthesizing evidence for other mTOR pathway inhibitors. The analysis is also constrained by the available primary data; questions about very long-term or late-life administration may remain underpowered if few studies report such protocols [N=...].

The planned investigation will address whether prior observed heterogeneity can be systematically attributed to identifiable study characteristics. By modeling the interaction of sex, strain, dose, and timing, the analysis will map the contours of rapamycin's lifespan effects as understood through the existing empirical record. The results will clarify whether a unified model suffices or whether sub-group-specific mechanisms demand targeted experimental investigation.
