# Systematic Synthesis of Rapamycin Effects by Experimental Context and Boundary Conditions

## Abstract

Rapamycin inhibits the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR), a central kinase integrating nutrient, energy, and stress signals to regulate cellular growth, autophagy, and metabolism. Prior narrative reviews and targeted meta-analyses have examined rapamycin's effects on specific outcomes such as lifespan extension [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin], cancer incidence [CIT:li-2014-rapamycin-cancer], or cardiac function [CIT:flynn-2013-rapamycin-heart]. However, uncertainty persists regarding the consistency of its effects across the full spectrum of measured biological and health outcomes. Existing syntheses have typically addressed isolated domains, leaving the overall pattern of efficacy and the critical moderators that may explain widespread heterogeneity unresolved. This study presents a planned systematic synthesis to aggregate the empirical evidence on rapamycin across diverse experimental contexts.

Is the direction of effect on lifespan replicated when considering only high-quality studies with low risk of bias? Does co-administration with other interventions alter the efficacy profile? ## Introduction

The mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) is a serine/threonine kinase that acts as a central hub for integrating signals from nutrients, growth factors, and cellular energy status to regulate fundamental processes including protein synthesis, autophagy, and metabolism [CIT:laplante-2012-mtor]. The bacterial macrolide rapamycin, and its analogs (rapalogs), function as allosteric inhibitors of mTOR complex 1 (mTORC1), making this pathway a prime target for pharmacological intervention in aging and age-related diseases. Initial landmark studies demonstrated that late-life administration of rapamycin could extend the lifespan of genetically heterogeneous mice [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin], sparking intensive investigation into its potential as a geroprotector.

Subsequent research has explored rapamycin's effects in a multitude of contexts beyond lifespan, including cancer chemoprevention, cardiac disease, neurodegeneration, immune modulation, and stem cell function. This broad interest has generated a substantial and diverse literature. Prior to this planned synthesis, focused meta-analyses have provided valuable quantitative insights within specific domains. For example, systematic reviews have examined its impact on rodent lifespan [CIT:swindell-2017-rapamycin-aging], tumor incidence [CIT:li-2014-rapamycin-cancer], and cardiac pathology [CIT:flynn-2013-rapamycin-heart]. These works have been instrumental in establishing the general direction of effect and identifying potential moderators like genetic background within their respective scopes.

However, the interpretation of rapamycin's efficacy is complicated by widespread heterogeneity across studies. Observations regarding its benefits are not uniform; outcomes can vary dramatically based on the experimental model, intervention protocol, and measured endpoint. Some studies report significant improvements in one system but not others, and findings in short-lived mouse strains may not directly translate to long-lived species. Prior syntheses, by design, have not conducted a unified analysis across this entire evidence landscape. This leaves a gap in understanding the overall architecture of rapamycin's effects: whether the reported benefits are concentrated in specific outcome categories or are robust across the physiological spectrum, and what set of experimental factors most consistently explains the observed variation.

This work therefore proposes a comprehensive systematic synthesis to extend and integrate prior findings. Its distinct contribution is not to reiterate conclusions within a single domain, but to perform a cross-domain stress-test of rapamycin's effects. By applying a consistent analytical framework to data spanning from cellular models to clinical trials, the analysis will quantify the aggregate effect size across all reported outcomes and, more critically, model the moderating influence of key biological and methodological variables. This approach will identify the core conditions under which rapamycin modulates biological function and highlight where effects are most consistent or most ambiguous.

The scope of this synthesis is explicitly bounded. It encompasses empirical studies investigating the direct effects of rapamycin or its close analogs on measurable biological or health outcomes. The analysis will not address the broader class of rapalogs with distinct binding profiles unless they are explicitly characterized as mTORC1 inhibitors in the source study. A non-goal is the detailed elucidation of intracellular signaling mechanisms, except where these are directly measured as outcome variables. The primary objective is to map the phenomenological landscape of rapamycin's effects as reported in the literature, providing a quantitative foundation to guide future mechanistic and translational research.
