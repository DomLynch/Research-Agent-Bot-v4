## The lens

The evidence pool is confined to environmental heat exposure and mortality, with no direct data on sauna heat therapy, making any publishable thesis on therapeutic mechanisms or outcomes unsupported by the provided facts.

## Already known — do not publish

- Heat exposure increases core temperature and poses health risks, especially in vulnerable populations.
- Saunas induce sweating and temporary physiological changes.

## Tensions / contradictions

- Environmental heat-related mortality data (e.g., 56% more deaths in women) contrasts with unverified claims of sauna health benefits, highlighting a evidence gap between harm and therapy.

## Evidence gaps

- Specific sauna parameters (temperature, duration, frequency) for clinical outcomes lack empirical validation.
- Biomarkers of heat acclimation (e.g., heat shock proteins) in therapeutic sauna use are understudied compared to environmental heat stress.

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- No direct evidence from sauna trials is cited, rendering the thesis speculative and not publishable.
- Reliance on environmental heat studies may not generalize to controlled sauna environments due to differences in exposure duration and intensity.

## Suggested next extractions

- Randomized controlled trials of sauna therapy on cardiovascular biomarkers (e.g., blood pressure, heart rate variability).
- Dose-response studies comparing infrared vs. traditional saunas on heat shock protein expression.
- Epidemiological data on long-term sauna use and mortality from cohort studies in Finland or Japan.
