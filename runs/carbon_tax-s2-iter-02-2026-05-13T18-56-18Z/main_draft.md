## Methods

The retrieval sweep was executed across [PLACEHOLDER:databases] with a date range of [PLACEHOLDER:date-range]. The query architecture combined controlled vocabulary and free-text terms structured around carbon pricing, carbon tax, emissions pricing, and their lexical variants, cross-referenced with outcome terms for emissions, economic performance, employment, energy demand, and innovation [PLACEHOLDER:query-terms]. Deduplication proceeded by DOI and PMID matching. The search is reported here as executed rather than planned; retrieval receipts are filed in the pipeline's provenance log. Reporting follows the PRISMA 2020 framework for transparent documentation of identification, screening, and inclusion steps [CIT:page2021prisma|reporting-framework].



Eligibility criteria are specified in population-exposure-comparator-outcome terms appropriate to carbon-pricing policy research. The population comprises jurisdictions, sectors, or household cohorts subject to a defined carbon tax instrument. The exposure is the implementation or marginal increase of an explicit carbon tax (price per unit CO₂-equivalent). Comparators include pre-implementation baselines, untreated jurisdictions in quasi-experimental designs, or alternative policy counterfactuals. Outcomes span direct emissions, GDP or output effects, employment reallocation, energy-price pass-through, clean-innovation indicators, and household welfare measures. Adjudication is performed by a single LLM judge operating against the universal evidence contract, preceded by a deterministic rule-triage pre-pass that filters on parsed-text adequacy, character-count floor [PLACEHOLDER:char-count-threshold], and presence of at least two non-title evidence quotes. There is no second human reviewer and no third-reviewer adjudicator; decisions are recorded in eligibility_receipts.json. The primary pooled corpus is restricted to explicit carbon tax interventions [PLACEHOLDER:primary_interventions]; studies examining only cap-and-trade or implicit carbon-pricing mechanisms [PLACEHOLDER:translational_only_interventions] are retained in a translational sensitivity layer but excluded from the primary effect pool. This exclusion is documented in primary_effect_input_set_strict.json and reflects the pipeline's strict A-core gate, which additionally requires per-quote evidence affirming that the current experiment used the declared study system, primary intervention, comparator, and endpoint [CIT:higgins2019cochrane|method-reference].



Data extraction captures effect-size estimates (point estimates, standard errors, confidence intervals, or t-statistics converted to standardized scales), sample sizes, outcome metric and units, intervention dose (tax rate in local currency per tonne CO₂-equivalent), comparator description, study design classification, geographic jurisdiction, and sector coverage — all in their original reported units. Each extraction includes at least two verbatim evidence quotes linking the reported numeric to its textual source. The extraction is performed by a single automated LLM pass per included paper; every receipt — comprising numerics, evidence quotes, model identifier, and timestamps — is filed in effect_extractions.json. Dual independent extraction and third-party adjudication are deferred to the pre-publication step; the present pipeline iteration does not execute multi-reviewer reconciliation [CIT:page2021prisma|reporting-framework]. A Researka Tier-2 canonical-fact cross-check overlay runs on every extraction containing percent-change numerics, returning verdicts of matched, discrepant, no_canonical_fact, or no_receipt_numerics, filed in extraction_crosscheck.json [PLACEHOLDER:crosscheck-verdict-distribution]. Where sentinel papers cannot be reached by automated retrieval, manual full-text injection is SHA-256-hashed and recorded as a provenance side-car.



Pre-specified moderator variables are drawn from the topic-pack scope and coded at the study level. [MODERATOR_P:geographic_region] captures the implementing jurisdiction grouped into OECD, non-OECD high-income, and low- and middle-income categories, reflecting heterogeneous institutional capacity. [MODERATOR_P:tax_rate_real] records the initial or average effective tax rate in 2020-adjusted USD per tonne CO₂-eq, extracted in original currency and converted using market exchange rates; no arbitrary numeric thresholds partition this continuous moderator. [MODERATOR_P:revenue_recycling] classifies the dominant revenue-use mechanism (lump-sum rebate, labor-tax reduction, deficit reduction, green investment) to test whether recycling design moderates economic outcomes [CIT:metcalf2009carbon-pricing|theory]. [MODERATOR_P:sector_coverage] distinguishes economy-wide from sector-specific implementations (power, industry, transport). [MODERATOR_P:study_design] classifies each study as difference-in-differences, synthetic control, structural model, computable general equilibrium, or other quasi-experimental design [CIT:wagner2021methods-policy-eval|method-reference]. Original-unit extraction is followed by post-hoc category sensitivity to avoid information loss from premature discretization.



The automated quality screen implemented by the universal evidence contract evaluates five pass-fail criteria: (1) parsed-text adequacy, confirming that the source text was machine-readable and structurally intact; (2) character-count floor [PLACEHOLDER:char-count-threshold], filtering abstracts-only or severely truncated records; (3) at least two non-title evidence quotes substantiating the extracted numerics; (4) endpoint coverage, confirming the study reports on at least one of the topic pack's declared endpoints; (5) intervention-control coverage, confirming both the carbon-tax exposure and a comparator are described in the evidence quotes. Records failing any criterion are demoted to unclear status. The topic pack's study designs — predominantly quasi-experimental policy evaluations — correspond to risk-of-bias assessment frameworks appropriate for observational causal inference [CIT:sterne2016robins-i|quality-framework]. Per-domain expert adjudication using that framework is deferred to the pre-publication step; the present iteration relies on the contract-based automated screen described above.



The pipeline executes an inverse-variance random-effects pool over contract-passing effect extractions within a single metric family drawn from [PLACEHOLDER:preferred_metric_families]. The pool is reported only at k ≥ 2 contract-passing effects per family; at k = 1 the pipeline reports a narrative synthesis. The point estimate uses DerSimonian-Laird between-study variance estimation [CIT:dersimonian1986meta-analysis|synthesis-method]. The full multi-level meta-regression apparatus — including random intercepts for study ID and outcome-within-study, restricted maximum-likelihood (REML) between-study variance, and Hartung-Knapp confidence-interval adjustment [CIT:intHout2014hartung-knapp|synthesis-method] — is a designed-for capability of the pipeline, deferred until the corpus accrues k ≥ 5–10 contract-passing effects per metric family. This deferral ensures that within-family parameter estimation has sufficient degrees of freedom for stable variance-component identification [CIT:riley2011multivariate-meta|synthesis-method]. Execution of dedicated meta-analysis statistical software is deferred accordingly; the present iteration implements the inverse-variance pool in the pipeline's built-in computation layer.



Leave-one-out influence diagnostics, Cook's-distance-based leverage assessment, funnel-plot asymmetry inspection, Egger's regression for small-study bias [CIT:egger1997bias|sensitivity-method], and trim-and-fill adjustment [CIT:duval2000trimfill|sensitivity-method] are all deferred until k ≥ 10 contract-passing effects per metric family. Heterogeneity quantification — including I², Cochran's Q, prediction intervals, and τ² — is reported descriptively at k ≥ 2 but interpretation is reserved for k ≥ 5 when between-study variance estimates stabilize [CIT:higgins2003inconsistency|sensitivity-method]. If the corpus includes studies of translational-only interventions [PLACEHOLDER:translational_only_interventions], these are mapped in a separate analog-evidence layer without pooling into the primary effect corpus, preserving the causal specificity of the primary synthesis [PLACEHOLDER:translational-sensitivity-count]. This translational sensitivity layer is designed to inform generalizability discussions rather than to contribute to the pooled point estimate.



The tension matrix is a designed-for capability of the pipeline in which moderator combinations — for instance, geographic region × revenue-recycling mechanism × study design — define a multi-dimensional contingency grid. Each cell would contain the pooled effect estimate (if k ≥ 2 within-cell) or a sparse-cell flag. The present iteration does not render the tension matrix because the corpus has not accrued sufficient contract-passing effects across the requisite moderator-stratum cells [PLACEHOLDER:current-k-primary]. The pipeline is configured to auto-generate the matrix when k ≥ 5 contract-passing effects accumulate across at least four populated moderator cells, at which point the grid would visualise effect heterogeneity by moderator combination and identify strata warranting focused investigation [CIT:thompson1999exploring-heterogeneity|synthesis-method]. Until that threshold is reached, moderator influence is assessed through the narrative comparison of extracted effect magnitudes across pre-specified moderator levels.
