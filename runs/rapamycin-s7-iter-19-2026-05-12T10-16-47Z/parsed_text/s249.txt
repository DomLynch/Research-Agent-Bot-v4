States Preventative Services Task Force (USPSTF) was established in 1984 to provide evidence-based guidelines for delivery of preventative health care services. The USPSTF goal has been to improve clinical outcomes by leveraging the advantages of preventative care, with emphasis on preventing disease rather than merely treating it. At the time USPSTF was established, a vigorous effort was underway to identify aging biomarkers [\u003ca href=\"#r1\" class=\"anchor \"\u003e1\u003c/a\u003e], which would in principle lay groundwork for future studies into basic aging mechanisms and, potentially, assist with the identification of interventions influencing these mechanisms. In many ways, these long-term goals of basic aging research were complementary and consistent with those of USPSTF, since the concept of “prevention” is fundamental to the rationale underlying development of drugs targeting basic aging mechanisms. Although \u003ci\u003ebona fide\u003c/i\u003e “anti-aging” drugs have not yet achieved clinical validation in humans, important steps forward have been made, with one human trial now underway to evaluate efficacy of metformin in healthy glucose-tolerant subjects [\u003ca href=\"#r2\" class=\"anchor \"\u003e2\u003c/a\u003e].21:T505,Pre-clinical evidence supporting beneficial effects of rapamycin on lifespan has been buoyed by mouse survivorship studies [\u003ca href=\"#r3\" class=\"anchor \"\u003e3\u003c/a\u003e], which may be concerning since prior work has shown that results from such studies can vary among laboratories and be influenced by strain or other factors [\u003ca href=\"#r5\" class=\"anchor \"\u003e5\u003c/a\u003e]. This complicates their interpretation, since for example it is unclear which mouse strains provide the best models for translation to human populations [\u003ca href=\"#r5\" class=\"anchor \"\u003e5\u003c/a\u003e]. To explore the role of strain and other factors, a meta-analysis of 29 mouse survivorship studies was recently carried out [\u003ca href=\"#r6\" class=\"anchor \"\u003e6\u003c/a\u003e]. Findings demonstrated significant heterogeneity among studies, but overall meta-estimates were consistent with a 13% lifespan increase in rapamycin-treated mice (95% CI: 11.5% - 14.6%), with stronger increases in females (15.1%) compared to males (9.4%) and a trend towards weaker survivorship increases in pure inbred strains such as C57BL/6 [\u003ca href=\"#r6\" class=\"anchor \"\u003e6\u003c/a\u003e]. These results confirm generally favorable effects of rapamycin treatment on mouse lifespan, but also identify sex- and strain-dependent effects influencing the treatment response magnitude.22:T79d,"]) self.__next_f.push([1,"Rapamycin increased mouse lifespan by 13% on average, but this \u003ci\u003erelative\u003c/i\u003e measure of treatment effect does not provide the complete story and alternative metrics may be more informative from a preventative medicine standpoint [\u003ca href=\"#r6\" class=\"anchor \"\u003e6\u003c/a\u003e]. In particular, the age-specific \u003ci\u003eabsolute\u003c/i\u003e survivorship increase may be a more informative measure of treatment effect, since this value is reciprocally related to the number of individuals requiring treatment to yield one additional survivor at a given age (i.e., number needed to treat, NNT). The above-mentioned meta-analysis of 29 experiments estimated a minimal NNT value of 3.10 (95% CI: 2.75–3.55), with the minimum NNT meta-estimate occurring at the 67th percentile survival time and NNT increasing precipitously at later ages (\u003ca href=\"#f1\" class=\"anchor xref_figure xref_figure-f1\"\u003eFigure 1A\u003c/a\u003e).
