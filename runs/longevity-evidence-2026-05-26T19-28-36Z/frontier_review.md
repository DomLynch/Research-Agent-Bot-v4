# Frontier review — longevity

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-26T19-28-36Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

Longevity is not a linear genetic preset but a systems-level outcome where conserved pathways intersect with organ-specific aging states and acute environmental stressors, creating non-intuitive thresholds; the 25% heritability ceiling (fact 161784) is challenged by proteomic biomarkers with stronger hazard ratios (fact 157873), while ecological extremes like insecticide exposure (fact 163217) reveal species-specific vulnerabilities absent in human nutrition data.

## Already known — do not publish

- Genetic factors account for about 25% of human longevity variation.
- Mediterranean diet adherence is associated with better health outcomes in older adults.
- Environmental pollutants reduce lifespan in insects.
- Youthful brain and immune systems correlate with longevity in humans.

## Tensions / contradictions

- The modest OR of 1.10 for rs2149954 on chromosome 5q33.3 in European-descent humans (fact 145166) contrasts with the profound HR of 0.44 for youthful brain-immune systems (fact 157873), suggesting proteomic biomarkers may outweigh specific genetic variants in predictive power.
- A 70% longevity decrease in solitary bees from combined warm winters and sulfoxaflor insecticides (fact 163217) versus a 15.5% prevalence in high Mediterranean diet adherence among older adults (fact 179394) highlights disparity between acute toxicological effects and chronic dietary benefits, questioning cross-species extrapolation.

## Evidence gaps

- Interaction between Mediterranean diet adherence (Medi-Lite score) and genetic loci like rs2149954 in modulating sarcopenia risk or longevity in nonagenarians, as current evidence is siloed.
- Dose-response relationships for environmental stressors (e.g., sulfoxaflor at 11.64 ng a.i./bee) on human neural or immune aging biomarkers, given the lack of human data paralleling the bee study.

- novelty 85 / evidence_strength 30 / reviewer_risk 70
- **Why publishable:** By juxtaposing the dramatic 70% longevity reduction in Osmia cornuta from sulfoxaflor (fact 163217) with human dietary intervention data (facts 179394, 179393), this paper highlights the need for cross-disciplinary models to study how modern chemical exposures might accelerate aging in humans.


## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- For the multi-organ aging clocks thesis: The review is overly speculative without empirical validation in longitudinal cohorts; proposed biomarkers lack tested interventions and may not generalize beyond UK Biobank participants.
- For the ecological toxicology thesis: Extrapolation from insects to humans is unjustified due to fundamental differences in lifespan, physiology, and stress responses; the bee study's dose (11.64 ng a.i./bee) may not translate to human exposure scenarios.
- The evidence pool is narrow with only six facts spanning genetics, proteomics, and nutrition, limiting robustness for meta-analyses and increasing risk of overfitting narratives.
