## The lens

The evidence pool contains no A/B-bound facts, precluding the formulation of specific, citable theses for a publishable paper. The ALPHA HINTS suggest directions such as investigating the paradox of obesity in sarcopenia assessment or sex-specific treatment effects, but these require primary data extraction to become evidence-based.

## Already known — do not publish

- Grip strength is inversely associated with mortality risk
- Low handgrip strength is a biomarker for frailty and poor health outcomes

## Tensions / contradictions

- Obesity shows protective associations for probable sarcopenia when using grip strength alone (hint), but may conflict with other sarcopenia measures like muscle mass
- Sex differences modulate handgrip strength response to interventions (hint), yet evidence on longevity impacts is sparse

## Evidence gaps

- Dose-response relationships between grip strength improvements and longevity markers in randomized trials, particularly with whey supplementation
- Longitudinal studies on grip strength and functional decline (e.g., ADL, dysphagia) in underrepresented groups like dialysis patients

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- Lack of direct, citable evidence from controlled studies limits meta-analytic potential
- Heterogeneity in grip strength protocols (e.g., measurement devices, cut-offs) across observational studies introduces bias
