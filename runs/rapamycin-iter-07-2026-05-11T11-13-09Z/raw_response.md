===TITLE===
Heterogeneity in Rapamycin's Effects on Lifespan Across Experimental Contexts

===ABSTRACT===
To what extent does rapamycin consistently extend lifespan across varying experimental designs, and which methodological or biological factors moderate this effect? Existing meta-analyses have identified a general longevity benefit [CIT:swindell-2017-meta] but have noted substantial heterogeneity that limits precise prediction of effect magnitude across contexts [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin]. This leaves the precise boundaries of its efficacy and the relative importance of potential moderators unresolved.

This planned systematic synthesis will screen all available studies reporting rapamycin lifespan interventions in mammalian models. The corpus will be defined by predetermined inclusion criteria, with screening counts reported as [N_SCREENED], [N_ACCEPTED], and [K_STUDIES]. The analysis will employ a multilevel mixed-effects model framework to partition total variance in log-transformed lifespan ratio between study-level random effects and pre-specified moderators. A tension matrix approach will be used to quantify consistency of effects across key design dimensions.

The primary analysis is structured to interrogate heterogeneity through pre-specified interaction terms and subgroup models. The planned boundary conditions for moderator tests include: biological sex, genetic background, treatment initiation timing, dose, and route of administration. The analysis will test whether the pooled effect size [EFFECT] differs significantly [MODERATOR_P] across these strata. The goal is to quantify the proportion of variance attributable to each factor, not to assert their presence or absence a priori.

The findings from this planned analysis will clarify whether observed heterogeneity stems from identifiable methodological factors or suggests more fundamental biological variability. This specification will generate prioritized hypotheses for future primary studies, particularly those designed to test high-leverage moderators like sex-specific responses or late-life intervention efficacy, thereby strengthening the evidence base for translational applications.

===INTRODUCTION===
The mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway is a conserved regulator of growth and metabolism, and its inhibition by rapamycin has become a central intervention in aging research [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor]. The prospect of a pharmacological modulator that consistently extends lifespan across diverse species has significant implications for understanding fundamental biology and for translational geroscience [CIT:kennedy-2014-metformin]. However, translating findings from model organisms to human health requires a precise understanding of an intervention's effect boundaries and the conditions under which it operates most effectively. The central research question is therefore not whether rapamycin can extend lifespan, but to what degree its effect is robust versus sensitive to variation in experimental design and biological context.

Prior quantitative syntheses have laid critical groundwork for answering this question. The seminal meta-analysis by Harrison et al. (2009) established the foundational finding of a significant, pooled lifespan extension in mice [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin]. Subsequent work has expanded on this, with Swindell (2017) providing a comprehensive meta-analysis that confirmed the effect and began to investigate heterogeneity, noting substantial between-study variance [CIT:swindell-2017-meta]. More recent focused reviews have examined specific facets, such as the influence of sex [CIT:mannick-2018-sex] or the potential for late-life initiation [CIT:bitto-2016-latelife]. These collective works establish rapamycin as a robust life-extending agent but simultaneously highlight significant unexplained heterogeneity. The gap lies in synthesizing these disparate threads within a single, unified analytical framework that can disentangle the relative and interactive contributions of multiple moderators.

This paper aims to extend and refine prior syntheses by moving beyond confirming the main effect to rigorously stress-testing its consistency. The contribution is a planned multi-level meta-regression that explicitly models methodological and biological covariates as potential sources of effect-size heterogeneity. Where prior work has reported on individual moderators, this analysis is designed to evaluate them concurrently, assessing their independent and interactive explanatory power in a pre-specified model. This approach allows for the quantification of heterogeneity attributable to specific factors versus residual, unexplained variation.

The scope of this synthesis is intentionally bounded. It is limited to mammalian models, given their direct relevance to human aging biology. The analysis will not encompass related interventions such as caloric restriction or other mTOR inhibitors unless they are directly compared within a rapamycin-focused study. A primary non-goal is the generation of new primary data; this is a statistical synthesis and hypothesis-generation exercise based on the existing literature. Furthermore, the analysis will not attempt to derive a single, universal "dose-response" curve, as the diversity of treatment protocols across studies precludes such a direct aggregation without advanced, and potentially tenuous, modeling assumptions.

The planned analysis is structured around a core tension: does the weight of evidence support a generalized effect of rapamycin on lifespan, or does it reveal a landscape of conditional effects? The models will be specified to test this directly. For instance, a significant random effect for study ID in the base model would indicate substantial unexplained heterogeneity. The subsequent moderator analysis will then test whether variables like sex (Male vs. Female vs. Both), genetic background (specific inbred strains vs. outbred), and treatment timing (early-life, adult, late-life) can explain a meaningful portion of this variance. The analysis will further explore potential interactions, such as whether the dose-response relationship differs between sexes or initiation periods. This design does not pre-suppose the outcome but provides a rigorous, pre-registered framework for evaluating the consistency of the intervention's effect.

In summary, this work positions itself within a lineage of quantitative syntheses on rapamycin and aging [CIT:arriola-apelo-2016-review]. Its distinct contribution is the planned application of a contemporary multi-level meta-analytic framework to a consolidated dataset to decompose effect heterogeneity. By pre-specifying the models and moderators, the analysis aims to provide a clearer, more nuanced map of rapamycin's efficacy, identifying contexts of maximal effect and those where the effect is attenuated or absent. The results will generate specific, testable hypotheses for future primary research, such as the need for dedicated studies in female animals across multiple genetic backgrounds or trials designed to optimize late-life dosing regimens. This step is essential for moving from establishing a phenomenon to understanding its governing principles.
