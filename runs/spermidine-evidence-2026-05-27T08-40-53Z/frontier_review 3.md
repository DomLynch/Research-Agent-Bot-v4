# Frontier review — spermidine

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-27T08-40-53Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The evidence pool for spermidine is narrowly anchored to a single 2017 murine longevity study, lacking mechanistic, dose-response, or human data, which prevents a publishable thesis without extrapolating beyond the available facts.

## Already known — do not publish

- Spermidine administration extends mouse lifespan by up to 25% when given orally throughout life, as shown in Cancer Research 2017.

## Tensions / contradictions

- In vitro spermidine inhibits SARS-CoV-2 at IC50 136.7 μM, but no in vivo data links this antiviral effect to the longevity benefits observed in mice.
- MAP1S-deficient mice have reduced median survival, suggesting autophagy involvement, yet the direct role of spermidine in modulating autophagy proteins like LC3, Beclin 1, and p62 in vivo is not corroborated by the provided evidence.

## Evidence gaps

- Human clinical trials evaluating spermidine's impact on lifespan or age-related diseases.
- Dose-titration studies for spermidine (e.g., at 5 mg/kg·d) in non-rodent species or varied genetic backgrounds.
- Tissue-specific analysis of autophagy regulation by spermidine beyond whole-organism lifespan metrics.

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- The thesis would over-rely on a single study from 2017, ignoring potential publication bias or lack of replication.
- Absence of data on spermidine's safety, optimal dosing, or mechanisms in humans weakens translational relevance.

## Suggested next extractions

- AMPK phosphorylation and autophagy protein dynamics (LC3, Beclin 1, p62) in response to spermidine in mammalian tissues.
- SARS-CoV-2 infection models in animals to test spermidine's in vivo antiviral efficacy.
- Longitudinal studies of spermidine at varied doses (e.g., 5 mg/kg·d) on healthspan in outbred or genetically diverse rodent strains.
