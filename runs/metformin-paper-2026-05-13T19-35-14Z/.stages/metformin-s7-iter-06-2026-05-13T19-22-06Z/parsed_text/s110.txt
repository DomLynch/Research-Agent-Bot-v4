China Abstract Osteoporosis is a widespread metabolic bone disorder characterized by a reduction in bone density and structural deterioration, leading to an increased susceptibility to fractures. This study investigates the role of metformin (Met) in promoting the osteogenic differentiation of bone marrow stromal cells (BMSCs) through the activation of AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) and explores its potential application in the treatment of osteoporotic fractures. We conducted a series of in vivo and in vitro experiments to elucidate the mechanisms underlying the effects of metformin. In a murine model of osteoporosis, metformin treatment significantly enhanced tibia fracture healing, as evidenced by increased bone mineral density (BMD), bone volume fraction (BV/TV), trabecular number (Tb.N), and decreased trabecular separation (Tb.Sp), as observed through micro-computed tomography (Micro-CT) and histological analyses. Immunoblot and real-time PCR demonstrated that metformin upregulated collagen type I (Col-I), a key osteogenic marker, and osteoprotegerin (OPG), an inhibitor of osteoclast differentiation that contributes to bone homeostasis via activated the AMPK signaling pathway. In vitro, metformin enhanced the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs, as indicated by elevated alkaline phosphatase (ALP) activity. Western blot and PCR analyses further revealed that metformin increased the expression of AMPK, phosphorylated AMPK (p-AMPK), mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR), phosphorylated mTOR (p-mTOR), Beclin-1, and LC3-II/LC3-I, suggesting enhanced autophagy. The application of the AMPK inhibitor Compound C attenuated these effects, confirming the role of AMPK-mediated autophagy in metformin-induced osteogenesis. These findings suggest that metformin, through the activation of AMPK and subsequent enhancement of autophagy, promotes the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs and accelerates the healing of osteoporotic fractures. Future research should focus on optimizing metformin dosage and administration routes and evaluating the long-term safety and efficacy of this therapeutic approach for patients with osteoporotic fractures. Journal of Orthopaedic Surgery and Research (2025) 20:661 Introduction Osteoporosis is a common metabolic bone disorder char­ acterized by reduced bone density and deterioration of bone microarchitecture, resulting in increased bone fra­ gility and a heightened risk of fractures [1]. Osteoporotic fractures, particularly prevalent among the elderly and postmenopausal women, present significant clinical chal­ lenges due to their profound impact on patients’ quality of life and the complexity of treatment and repair [2]. Current therapeutic strategies for osteoporotic fractures often prove insufficient [3], thereby necessitating the exploration of novel and more effective treatment modal­ ities. Given the limitations of existing treatments, there is a growing interest in repurposing established drugs with known safety profiles, such as metformin, to address the complications associated with osteoporosis. In light of the need for innovative osteoporosis treat­ ments, metformin, an antidiabetic drug, has emerged as a candidate due to its multiple effects beyond glucose regulation, particularly its impact on bone metabo­ lism [4]. Studies have demonstrated that metformin can modulate bone cell function through various pathways, promoting bone formation and inhibiting bone resorp­ tion [5]. Among these pathways, the AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) signaling pathway plays a pivotal role in mediating metformin’s effects on bone metabo­ lism. The AMPK signaling pathway is integral to cellu­ lar energy regulation, comprising a catalytic α subunit and regulatory β and γ subunits [6]. AMPK activation occurs in response to energy stress, promoting ATP pro­ duction and inhibiting ATP consumption to maintain cellular energy balance [7]. In bone metabolism, AMPK activation has been shown to promote osteoblast dif­ ferentiation through various mechanisms, including the phosphorylation of Smad1/5/9 proteins, which enhances the expression of bone formation-related genes [8]. Fur­ thermore, AMPK also regulates autophagy, a cellular degradation process that maintains cellular homeostasis by clearing damaged organelles and proteins. Autophagy activation by AMPK provides the necessary energy and metabolites for osteoblast differentiation. Furthermore, AMPK can upregulate the expression of Runx2, a key transcription factor for osteoblast differentiation, and enhance the activity of the Wnt/β-catenin signaling path­ way, both of which are crucial for osteoblast genesis [9, 10]. One of the key mechanisms through which metfor­ min influences bone health is its activation of the AMP- activated protein kinase (AMPK) signaling pathway, which plays a critical role in cellular energy regulation and osteoblast differentiation. In addition to promoting osteoblast activity, inhibition of osteoclast differentiation is essential for maintain­ ing bone homeostasis. Osteoprotegerin (OPG), a glyco­ protein predominantly secreted by osteoblast-lineage cells, acts as a decoy receptor for receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa-B ligand (RANKL). By binding RANKL with high affinity, OPG prevents its interac­ tion with the RANK receptor on osteoclast precursors, thereby suppressing osteoclastogenesis and subsequent bone resorption. This mechanism establishes OPG as a pivotal regulator in the RANKL/RANK/OPG signaling axis, which orchestrates bone remodeling balance. Dys­ regulation of this axis has been implicated in osteopo­ rosis pathogenesis through enhanced osteoclast activity [11]. Epidemiological investigations have demonstrated that metformin therapy in type 2 diabetic patients corre­ lates with reduced risks of osteoporosis and osteoporotic fractures [12]. Preclinical mechanistic studies further indicate that metformin confers bone-protective effects, at least partially through suppressing excessive osteoclas­ togenesis via transcriptional upregulation of OPG and concomitant downregulation of RANKL in osteoblasts [13]. Therefore, elucidating the functional role of OPG is critical to fully understand metformin’s skeletal benefits. To decode the complex actions of metformin on bone health, network pharmacology-a field that merges sys­ tems biology with pharmacology-offers an invaluable tool for mapping drug interactions at a systemic level [14]. It employs computational and experimental techniques to map out the interactions between drugs, targets, and disease-related pathways. This holistic approach allows for the identification of multi-target drugs and the eluci­ dation of complex mechanisms underlying drug effects, providing a comprehensive understanding of how drugs like metformin exert their therapeutic actions. This paper aims to detect the effects of metformin on the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs through the acti­ vation of the AMPK signaling pathway and its potential application in the treatment of osteoporotic fractures. Materials and methods Reagents Metformin was purchased from Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Antibodies related to autophagy, including Anti-Phos­ pho-mTOR and Anti-mTOR, were procured from Cell Signaling Technology (Boston, MA, USA). Additional antibodies, such as Anti-Phospho-AMPK, Anti-AMPK, Anti-β-actin, Anti-Beclin-1, Anti-LC3B, and Anti-Col­ lagen I et al., were sourced from Affinity Biosciences (Jiangsu, China). Animal model of osteoporotic fracture Healthy adult female C57BL/6 mice, aged 8 weeks, pur­ chased from Charles River Laboratories, housing and care of the animals were in accordance with the NIH Guidelines for the Care and Use of Laboratory Ani­ mals. All mice were randomly divided into three groups, Page 3 of 14 Du et al. Journal of Orthopaedic Surgery and Research (2025) 20:661 each consisting of six mice: sham-operated group, OVX group, and OVX + Metformin group. The selected num­ ber ensures that the study minimizes animal use while achieving scientific validity, in compliance with the “3Rs” principle for ethical animal research. Further justification for the number of animals includes the biological vari­ ability of the chosen model and the expected effect size of the intervention being studied. The mice were fasted for 6 h prior to ovariectomy (OVX). 8 weeks post-surgery, a standardized distal tibia fracture was established in the right tibias of each group as previously described in the study by Hu et al., which were produced using a three-bending device and fixed with a 30 G needle [15]. The OVX + Metformin group received metformin treatment at a dose of 200 mg/kg/ day. After 28 days of intervention, after deep anesthe­ sia with isoflurane, all mice were sacrificed by cervical dislocation, and tissues were collected for subsequent experiments. The Ethics Committee for Animal Experi­ mentation at North China University of Science and Technology approved our experimental protocols, fol­ lowing the Guiding Opinions on the Treatment of Lab­ oratory Animals issued by the Ministry of Science and Technology of the People’s Republic of China and the Laboratory Animal-Guideline for Ethical Review of Animal Welfare issued by the National Standard GB/ T35892-2018 (No. 2024sy3040). The handling and treat­ ment of animals strictly adhered to the NIH’s Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals. Micro-CT analylsis At 28 days post-fracture, the right tibias were carefully dissected free of muscle and stored in 10% neutral forma­ lin for 48 h, then scanned using a high-resolution micro- CT (SkyScan1176 Software: Version1.1 (build 6), Bruker, Kontich, Belgium). The region of interest encompassed the area 2 mm above and below the fracture line. Isolation of primary bone marrow stromal cells (BMSCs) Primary BMSCs were isolated from the left femurs of mice in each group. The femurs were digested with 0.25% trypsin at room temperature for 5 min, followed by 0.1% collagenase digestion in a 37 °C water bath for 2 h. The bone fragments were then cultured in a humidi­ fied incubator (Thermo, USA) at 37 °C with 5% CO2 for 3 days. After changing the medium and removing non­ adherent fragments, primary BMSCs were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) (Gibco) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin/amphotericin (Solarbio, China). Third-passage cells were used for the subsequent experiments. Cell viability assay BMSCs were exposed to various doses of metformin (0, 10, 50, 100, 200, 400, 600, and 1000 µM) in 96-well plates at a density of 3 × 104 cells/well for 24 h. Cell survival was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8; Solarbio, China) as per the manufacturer’s instructions. After add­ ing 10 µL of CCK-8 solution to each well, samples were incubated in the dark for 90 min, and absorbance was measured at 450 nm. ALP staining BMSCs were treated with 50, 100, and 200 µM metfor­ min for 7 days, then washed with PBS twice on a shaker for 3 min, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min, washed again with PBS, and stained using a 5-bromo- 4-chloro-3-indolylphosphate (BCIP)/Nitro Blue Tetra­ zolium (NBT) ALP color development kit (Beyotime, China). After 30 min of incubation at room temperature, protected from light, the stained cells were viewed and photographed under a microscope (Olympus, Japan). Quantitative real-time PCR To investigate the mechanisms by which metformin pro­ motes the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs, cells were treated with either vehicle, metformin (200 µM) alone, or a combination of metformin and the AMPK inhibitor Compound C. Osteogenic markers, key molecules of the AMPK pathway, and autophagy-related genes were ana­ lyzed by using real-time PCR. Total RNA was extracted from both BMSCs and frac­ ture callus tissue using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). Fracture callus tissue were ground in liquid nitrogen, homogenized in TRIzol, and subjected to phase separa­ tion and precipitation following standard protocols. For cells, BMSCs were lysed directly in TRIzol after PBS washing. RNA was quantified using a Nanodrop 2000 (Thermo, USA), and reverse-transcribed using a one- step RT-PCR kit with SYBR Green (Takara, Japan). RT- qPCR was performed using SYBR qPCR Mix (Servicebio, China), with β-actin as the internal control. Molecular dynamics simulation analysis To further investigate the interactions and stability of the complex, molecular dynamics (MD) simulations were performed using the GROMACS software to simulate the protein-ligand complex for 100 ns. The AMBER14SB. ff force field was selected to generate the topology file for the protein, while the ACPYPE software and the GAFF force field were used to generate the topology file for the small-molecule ligand. A TIP3P water box was con­ structed to include the entire docking system, and Na/ Cl ions were added to neutralize the system’s charge. The energy minimization of the system was carried out using 2500 steps of the steepest descent method followed by 2500 steps of the conjugate gradient method. Subse­ quently, the system underwent a 100 ps NVT ensemble simulation and a 100ps NPT equilibration simulation at a temperature of 298.15 K. Finally, a 100ns production MD simulation was performed under periodic boundary conditions. The particle-mesh Ewald (PME) method was applied for long-range electrostatic interactions, with a non-bonded cutoff distance set to 1.2 nm, a collision fre­ quency of 2ps, a system pressure of 1 bar, and an integra­ tion step size of 2 fs. Trajectories were saved every 100ps. Molecular dynamics simulation was carried out in trip­ licates to assess the flexibility and stability of Metformin with AMPK. Statistical analysis All experiments were performed in triplicate. Subsequently, these intersecting molecules were subjected to a PPI network analysis using the STRING database, which identified significant interactions among the molecules (Fig. 1B). GO analysis showed that these molecules are primarily involved in biological processes such as the positive regu­ lation of the MAPK cascade, regulation of inflammatory response, positive regulation of DNA-binding transcrip­ tion factor activity, epithelial cell proliferation, cellular response to peptides, and regulation of smooth muscle cell proliferation (Fig. 1C). Molecular docking analysis was performed to evaluate the binding affinity between metformin and AMPK, a key regulator in energy metabolism and osteoblast differen­ tiation. The docking model demonstrated a good bind­ ing affinity with a score of 3.4353, suggesting a potential interaction between metformin and AMPK (Fig. 1E). Meanwhile, as is shown in the Fig. 1F, the RMSD (Root Mean Square Deviation) of the protein-ligand complex over a 100 ns molecular dynamics simulation. Initially, the RMSD increases during the equilibration phase (0-20ns), then stabilizes around 0.25–0.30 nm with minor fluctuations, indicating that the complex reaches equilibrium and maintains structural stability. The low and stable RMSD values (< 0.3 nm) suggest that the protein-ligand complex remains stable throughout the simulation, with no significant conformational changes. This supports the stability of the binding mode under the simulation conditions. Also, RMSF results demon­ strated that the AMPK has a stable binding region for metformin, supporting its potential for strong and stable binding, while the observed flexibility in certain regions may enhance the binding process or facilitate conforma­ tional changes necessary for functional activity(Fig. 1G). These results highlight the role of AMPK activation in the mechanism by which metformin may influence bone metabolism and the healing of osteoporosis-induced fractures. The involvement of pathways related to inflam­ mation, cellular proliferation, and metabolic regulation underscores the multifaceted effects of metformin in this context. Binding free energy calculation and decomposition analysis of the metformin-AMPK complex Binding free energy is a critical parameter in the molec­ ular dynamics study of protein-ligand interactions, providing essential insights into the thermodynamic properties of these interactions. A more negative binding free energy (Delta G) indicates stronger binding affinity between the protein and ligand, reflecting a more stable complex. This is because systems naturally tend toward lower free energy states, which correspond to greater stability. To further evaluate the binding stability between met­ formin and AMPK, the binding free energy components were calculated. As shown in Table 1, the van der Waals interaction between metformin and AMPK is -21.44 ± 3.92 kcal/mol, while the electrostatic interac­ tion is -127.53 ± 28.14 kcal/mol, both of which strongly favor the binding. However, the polar solvation energy (103.14 ± 23.99 kcal/mol) offsets much of the favorable electrostatic interaction. Despite this, the total binding free energy (Delta G = -50.60 ± 7.28 kcal/mol) indicates that metformin binds strongly to AMPK, supported by the significant contributions of van der Waals and elec­ trostatic forces. These findings highlight the thermody­ namic stability of the metformin-AMPK complex and its potential biological relevance. Metformin promotes the healing of tibia fractures in osteoporotic mice Micro-CT reconstructions clearly revealed significant callus formation in all groups 28 days post-fracture. The callus in the OVX group was more loosely orga­ nized compared to the sham group, whereas those in the metformin-treated group were denser than those in the OVX group (Fig. 2A). This was reflected in significantly increased bone mineral density (Fig. 2B), bone volume fraction (Fig. 2C), connectivity density of trabeculae (Fig. 2D), number of trabeculae (Fig. 2E), and a signifi­ cant reduction in trabecular separation (Fig. 2F) in the metformin-treated group compared to the other three groups (P < 0.05). Histological examination of the tibia stained with hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) showed that mice in the metformin-treated group had a greater num­ ber and more continuous trabeculae compared to the OVX group (Fig. 2G). The OVX mice showed a mark­ edly lower bony callus ratio in comparison with the Sham and OVX + metformin groups (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2H). These results demonstrate that metformin treatment signifi­ cantly promotes the healing of tibia fractures in osteopo­ rotic mice. Metformin promotes osteogenic differentiation in mice BMSCs The results of CCK-8 analysis indicated that after 72 h of treatment with different concentrations of metfor­ min, there was no significant change in cell proliferation (Fig. 4A). Subsequently, we treated BMSCs with 50, 100, and 200 µM metformin to observe its effects on osteo­ genic differentiation. Metformin affects osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs via AMPK signaling pathway and autophagy To explore the mechanisms by which metformin pro­ motes the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs, we examined the expression of proteins involved in the AMPK/mTOR signaling pathway, autophagy, and osteo­ genic transcriptional regulation by using Western blot and real-time PCR. Western blot analysis (Fig. 5A-B) revealed that metformin treatment led to a dose-depen­ dent increase in the phosphorylation of AMPK and a decrease in the phosphorylation of mTOR (Fig. 5C), indi­ cating activation of the AMPK pathway and inhibition of mTOR signaling. Additionally, metformin significantly upregulated the expression of osteogenic transcription factors Runx2 and Osterix, as well as the autophagy- related proteins Beclin-1 and the LC3-II/LC3-I ratio (Fig. 5D-H). Meanwhile, Metformin also increased OPG expression and decreased RANKL levels, suggesting a dual role in promoting osteoblast activity while inhibit­ ing osteoclastogenesis (Fig. 5F-G). Real-time PCR results confirmed these observations at the transcript level. Met­ formin significantly elevated the mRNA expression of AMPK (Fig. 5I), mTOR (Fig. 5J), and Beclin-1 (Fig. 5K) in a concentration-dependent manner. These findings sug­ gest that metformin promotes osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs by activating the AMPK pathway, enhancing autophagy, and upregulating key osteogenic transcrip­ tion factors, thereby coordinating anabolic signaling with bone remodeling regulation. The impact of AMPK inhibitor on metformin-induced autophagy and osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs To assess the role of AMPK in metformin-induced osteo­ genic differentiation, BMSCs were treated with metfor­ min alone or in combination with the AMPK inhibitor Compound C. ALP staining (Fig. 6A) demonstrated that metformin markedly increased ALP activity, while co- treatment with Compound C significantly suppressed this effect, indicating reduced osteogenic differentiation. Western blot analysis revealed that Compound C inhib­ ited the phosphorylation of AMPK and reversed the sup­ pression of p-mTOR induced by metformin (Fig. 6C-D), suggesting inhibition of AMPK signaling. Additionally, the expression levels of osteogenic transcription factors Runx2 and Osterix, as well as autophagy-related proteins were decreased in the presence of Compound C (Fig. 6E- F). Furthermore, Compound C abrogated the metfor­ min-induced upregulation of OPG and suppression of RANKL(Fig. 6G-H). Immunofluorescence staining of LC3B (Fig. 6I) also confirmed the reduction of autopha­ gic activity in the Compound C co-treatment group. These findings suggest that AMPK activation is essen­ tial for metformin-induced osteogenic and autophagy- related responses in BMSCs. Discussion As of 2024, the number of individuals globally affected by osteoporosis is estimated to exceed 500 million, includ­ ing approximately 22% of women and 6% of men aged 50 years and older. The prevalence of osteoporosis continues to rise worldwide, particularly in regions with aging pop­ ulations such as Europe, North America, and Asian [16]. It is anticipated that the incidence of osteoporosis and related fractures will significantly increase as the global population continues to age [17]. Osteoporosis is a prevalent metabolic bone disease characterized by decreased bone density and the dete­ rioration of bone microarchitecture, leading to increased bone fragility and a higher risk of fractures [18]. Osteo­ porotic fractures, particularly common among the elderly and postmenopausal women, pose significant clinical challenges due to their impact on patients’ quality of life and the complexity of treatment [19]. Current therapeu­ tic strategies, including pharmacological interventions, physical therapy, and surgical procedures, often fall short in effectively addressing the multifaceted nature of osteo­ porotic fractures, necessitating the exploration of novel treatment modalities [20]. Network pharmacology has been widely used to predict the hypothesis of the association between drugs and biomolecular networks [23]. This study first adopted network pharmacology to investigate the mechanism of action and molecular tar­ gets of metformin in the treatment of Osteoporosis. In this study, we investigated the potential of metfor­ min to promote the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs through the activation of the AMPK pathway. Our find­ ings demonstrate that metformin significantly enhances BMSCs’ osteogenic differentiation and bone formation, suggesting its potential application in the treatment of osteoporotic fractures. The findings of this study elucidate the molecular mechanisms by which metformin promotes the osteo­ genic differentiation of BMSCs via the activation of the AMPK signaling pathway. The activation of AMPK by metformin enhances autophagy, a cellular degradation process that maintains cellular homeostasis by removing damaged organelles and proteins [30]. This autophagic process is crucial for providing the necessary energy and metabolic substrates for osteogenic differentiation [31]. Our study demonstrated that metformin treatment sig­ nificantly increased the expression of autophagy markers such as Beclin-1 and LC3-II/LC3-I, indicating enhanced autophagic activity. These results suggest that metfor­ min-induced autophagy via AMPK activation, plays a pivotal role in promoting the osteogenic differentiation of BMSCs, thereby offering a potential therapeutic strategy for osteoporosis-related fractures. This molecular insight not only answers the research hypothesis but also pro­ vides a theoretical basis for the application of metformin in bone regenerative medicine. In terms of gene function and cellular behavior, the study’s results highlight the significant impact of met­ formin on the expression of key osteogenic markers and transcription factors. Metformin treatment was shown to upregulate the expression of Col-I, a key osteogenic marker, and OPG, which functions as a decoy recep­ tor for RANKL and inhibits osteoclastogenesis, thereby contributing to bone remodeling and homeostasis. The increase in ALP activity further supports the role of met­ formin in enhancing osteogenic differentiation. These findings reveal that metformin modulates the genetic and cellular landscape of BMSCs, promoting their differen­ tiation into BMSCs. This not only advances our under­ standing of the genetic regulation of osteogenesis but also underscores the potential of metformin as a modula­ tor of stem cell fate. The ability of metformin to influence cellular behavior and gene expression profiles in BMSCs provides new insights into the mechanisms of bone for­ mation and regeneration. The implications of these findings extend to the realm of immunological mechanisms, particularly in the con­ text of bone healing and immune response modulation. The study showed that metformin treatment led to the activation of AMPK and subsequent phosphorylation of downstream targets, which are known to intersect with various immune signaling pathways [32, 33]. Moreover, the study did not investi­ gate the long-term effects of metformin on bone health, nor did it account for potential variability in treatment response among different populations, such as elderly individuals or those with comorbidities. The optimal dos­ age and administration regimen for metformin in the context of bone repair also remain to be established. In addition, while our study employed network pharmacol­ ogy, molecular docking, and molecular dynamics simula­ tions to predict potential binding interactions between metformin and AMPK, these findings were not experi­ mentally validated at the molecular level. Specifically, we did not perform site-directed mutagenesis or use AMPK point mutant models to confirm the functional significance of the predicted binding sites. Future stud­ ies should incorporate such wet-lab approaches to vali­ date the computational predictions and further elucidate the mechanistic basis of metformin’s osteogenic effects. These enhancements will be essential for strengthening the therapeutic rationale and translational value of our findings. Conclusion In conclusion, this study elucidates the mechanism by which metformin promotes the osteogenic differentia­ tion of BMSCs through AMPK-dependent autophagy, thereby accelerating the healing of osteoporotic frac­ tures in mice. These findings suggest that metformin, a widely used antidiabetic drug, holds promise as a thera­ peutic agent for enhancing bone regeneration and treat­ ing osteoporotic fractures. However, to translate these preclinical findings into clinical practice, further inves­ tigations are required to determine the optimal dosage, administration route, and long-term safety of metformin in diverse patient populations. Additionally, exploring the synergistic effects of metformin with other osteogenic or antiresorptive agents could provide a more comprehen­ sive approach to managing osteoporotic fractures.
