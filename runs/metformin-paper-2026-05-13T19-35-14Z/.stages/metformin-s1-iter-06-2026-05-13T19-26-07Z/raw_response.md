=== TITLE ===
Systematic Scoping Review of Metformin Effects on Murine Lifespan via Automated Evidence-Contract Pipeline

=== ABSTRACT ===
To what extent does metformin consistently extend lifespan across murine models, and how do pre-specified moderators including strain, sex, dosage, and treatment initiation timing shape any observed heterogeneity? Prior narrative and quantitative syntheses have summarized the literature [CIT:barzilai-tame-2016-review|narrative-review] [CIT:chen-2022-metformin-meta|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:ivimey-cook-tyshkovskiy-2025-meta|prior-meta-analysis], yet uncertainty persists regarding the consistency and boundary conditions of the intervention's effect. This review implements a reproducible evidence-contract pipeline designed to map this landscape. The pipeline performs a multi-source retrieval sweep, followed by eligibility adjudication by a single LLM judge against a deterministic rule-triage pre-pass. A universal evidence-contract gate then applies parsed-text adequacy criteria, and a strict per-quote audit demotes records whose evidence quotes do not affirm the murine system, metformin intervention, comparator, and lifespan endpoint. The corpus shape is described by placeholders: [N_SCREENED] records identified, [N_ACCEPTED] after evidence-contract application, yielding [K_STUDIES] studies for quantitative summary. The planned analysis involves inverse-variance random-effects pooling of contract-passing effect estimates within a single metric family at k >= 2. Mixed-effects models are specified to test for heterogeneity and to examine the pre-specified moderator strata of murine strain, sex, metformin dose, and timing of treatment onset. A tension matrix rendering is a designed-for capability of the pipeline; the present iteration defers it until the corpus accrues sufficient moderator-stratum cells. The primary research questions are whether the pooled effect estimate is directionally positive and statistically distinguishable from zero, and whether the pre-specified moderators partition a meaningful portion of observed variance. A key implication for future work is that consistent null findings under this automated synthesis would argue against further investment in single-factor mouse studies and redirect focus toward mechanistic or combinatorial interventions [CIT:foretz-viollet-2014-mechanism|mechanism-review].

=== INTRODUCTION ===
The investigation of pharmacological lifespan extension in murine models represents a critical translational bridge between invertebrate genetic studies and human gerontology [CIT:barzilai-tame-2016-review|narrative-review]. Metformin, a first-line antidiabetic agent, has emerged as a leading candidate for such study due to its epidemiological associations with reduced all-cause mortality and its modulation of evolutionarily conserved nutrient-sensing pathways [CIT:madiraju-2014-metformin-mechanism|mechanism-review]. The precise research question driving this synthesis is: to what extent and under what specific boundary conditions does metformin consistently extend lifespan in murine models? This question remains open because the existing primary study corpus is characterized by substantial heterogeneity in experimental design [CIT:martin-montalvo-2013-metformin|primary-study] [CIT:anisimov-2011-metformin|primary-study] [CIT:strong-2016-itp-acarbose-met|primary-study], and prior systematic efforts have not provided a fully powered, pre-specified joint moderator framework that can quantitatively map this variability.

The prior synthesis landscape includes both narrative and quantitative reviews. Narrative reviews have adeptly summarized the mechanistic rationale and historical trajectory of metformin in aging research [CIT:barzilai-tame-2016-review|narrative-review], while more recent meta-analytic work has sought to pool effect estimates [CIT:chen-2022-metformin-meta|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:ivimey-cook-tyshkovskiy-2025-meta|prior-meta-analysis]. These prior meta-analyses have provided valuable initial aggregations, demonstrating the feasibility of quantitative synthesis in this domain. However, they have been constrained by the methodological tools and corpus size available at their time of conduct, leaving room for extension. For example, one synthesis may have focused primarily on a single outcome metric or aggregated across diverse experimental contexts without a formally specified, multi-moderator mixed-effects model [CIT:chen-2022-metformin-meta|prior-meta-analysis]. Another may have employed a conventional meta-analytic framework without a transparent, automated evidence-contract gate to adjudicate study inclusion based on the direct affirmation of core endpoints and interventions in the source text [CIT:ivimey-cook-tyshkovskiy-2025-meta|prior-meta-analysis]. The present work therefore extends these prior syntheses by implementing a rigorous, automated pipeline to stress-test the robustness of the aggregated evidence and to refine the understanding of effect moderation.

This paper's contribution is threefold. First, it operationalizes a reproducible, single-LLM-judge pipeline with a deterministic rule-triage and evidence-contract audit to ensure that every included record's primary text explicitly affirms the murine system, metformin as the intervention, a relevant comparator, and lifespan as the endpoint. Second, it applies a uniform inverse-variance random-effects model to the contract-passing effect estimates, allowing for the formal estimation of between-study heterogeneity. Third, it specifies a priori the exploration of four key moderators: murine strain, sex, metformin dosage, and the timing of treatment initiation (e.g., early life versus late life). The pipeline is designed to estimate the pooled effect of metformin on murine lifespan and to quantify the relative contribution of these moderators. Tension-matrix rendering, which would visualize effect estimates across cross-classified moderator strata, is a designed-for capability of the pipeline; the present iteration defers this analysis until the corpus accrues k >= 2 contract-passing effects per metric family within each stratum cell.

The scope of this synthesis is deliberately focused on the primary intervention of metformin in murine models, with the primary endpoint of lifespan. Translational-only interventions, such as other biguanides like buformin and phenformin, are excluded from the primary pooled analysis but are retained for a translational sensitivity layer in the discussion. Explicit non-goals include a formal risk-of-bias assessment of the primary studies using a domain-specific framework; the pipeline's evidence-contract and per-quote audit steps serve as a proxy for methodological adequacy in the context of an automated synthesis, but a comprehensive assessment of bias domains would require per-domain expert adjudication, a capability that is deferred. Furthermore, this work does not aim to synthesize all proposed mechanisms of metformin action but instead focuses on the lifespan outcome, referencing mechanistic reviews only to contextualize potential sources of heterogeneity [CIT:foretz-viollet-2014-mechanism|mechanism-review] [CIT:madiraju-2014-metformin-mechanism|mechanism-review]. The pipeline does not perform external registry registration of its protocol, nor does it execute dedicated meta-analysis statistical software for advanced diagnostics such as leave-one-out analysis, publication-bias regression, or trim-and-fill; these are designed-for capabilities deferred until the corpus is of sufficient size. By defining this scope and these boundary conditions, the analysis is designed to provide a clear, automated, and replicable answer to the core question of metformin's effect on murine lifespan, while explicitly acknowledging the heterogeneity that prior primary studies and syntheses have identified [CIT:alfaras-2017-metformin|primary-study] [CIT:martin-montalvo-2013-metformin|primary-study].
