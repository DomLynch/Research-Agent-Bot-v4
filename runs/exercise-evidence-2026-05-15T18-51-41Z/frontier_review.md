# Frontier review — exercise

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-15T18-51-41Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The differential modulation of autophagy (ULK1 phosphorylation at Ser555) and systemic inflammation (hs-CRP, sTNFαR2) by exercise presents a non-obvious mechanistic link that could reconcile its pleiotropic effects in cancer and geriatric settings, but only if dose-response and combined therapy protocols are critically examined across murine and human models.

## Already known — do not publish

- Exercise improves physical function in elderly
- Exercise reduces tumor growth in mice
- Exercise decreases inflammatory biomarkers in cancer survivors

## Tensions / contradictions

- Exercise alone reduced hs-CRP by 30.2% in cancer survivors (fact 23), while exercise combined with metformin reduced it further but with wider confidence intervals (fact 21), suggesting interaction effects that are not fully characterized
- Voluntary running wheel exercise reduced MDA-MB-231 tumor growth by 66% in mice (fact 18), but human studies on vascular function show smaller effect sizes (SMD 0.65 in fact 10), indicating possible species-specific or outcome-specific efficacy gaps
- Autophagy markers (ULK1 phosphorylation) were upregulated by acute cycling in healthy humans (fact 14), yet chronic exercise interventions in cancer survivors focus on inflammatory endpoints (facts 19-24), leaving a disconnect between acute molecular responses and long-term clinical outcomes

## Evidence gaps

- The role of exercise-induced autophagy in cancer survivors, particularly in breast and colorectal cancer, as existing studies (e.g., fact 14) are limited to healthy humans
- Dose-response relationships for telerehabilitation calisthenic programs in elderly (fact 5-7), as the 4-week intervention lacks long-term data on frailty or mortality
- Effects of blood flow restriction (BFR) exercise (facts 11-12) on muscle protein synthesis in older adults or cancer patients, beyond healthy young men

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- Heterogeneity in exercise interventions (voluntary running vs. telerehabilitation vs. BFR) complicates pooled analysis
- Mechanistic studies (e.g., fact 14) use small samples of healthy young men, limiting generalizability to cancer or geriatric populations
- Lack of standardized outcomes across cancer types (e.g., breast vs. colorectal in fact 21) may confound inflammatory marker interpretations
- Potential publication bias toward positive results in cancer exercise studies (e.g., facts 18-24)

## Suggested next extractions

- Studies on autophagy markers (e.g., LC3B lipidation) in cancer patients undergoing exercise interventions
- Dose-response data for metformin-exercise combinations on inflammatory endpoints like sTNFαR2 across different cancer stages
- Long-term follow-up of telerehabilitation programs in elderly beyond 4 weeks, focusing on frailty indices and mortality
- Comparative analyses of BFR exercise versus aerobic exercise in older adults with sarcopenia or cancer cachexia
