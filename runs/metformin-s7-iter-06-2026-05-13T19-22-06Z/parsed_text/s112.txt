Genome Medicine Developing a novel aging assessment model to uncover heterogeneity in organ aging and screening of aging‑related drugs Yingqi Xu1†, Maohao Li1†, Congxue Hu1†, Yawen Luo1, Xing Gao1, Xinyu Li1, Xia Li1* and Yunpeng Zhang1* Abstract Background The decline in organ function due to aging significantly impacts the health and quality of life of the elderly. Assessing and delaying aging has become a major societal concern. Previous studies have largely focused on differences between young and old individuals, often overlooking the complexity and gradual nature of aging. Methods In this study, we constructed a comprehensive multi-organ aging atlas in mice and systematically ana- lyzed the aging trajectories of 16 organs to elucidate their functional specificity and identify organ-specific aging trend genes. Cross-organ association analysis was employed to identify global aging regulatory genes, leading to the development of a multi-organ aging assessment model, hereafter referred to as the 2A model. The model’s validity was confirmed using single-cell RNA sequencing data from aging mouse lungs, cross-species gene expression profiles, and pharmacogenomic data. Furthermore, a random walk algorithm and a weighted integration approach combining gene set enrichment analysis were implemented to systematically screen potential drugs for mitigating multi-organ aging. Results The 2A model effectively assessed aging states in both human and mouse tissues and demonstrated predic- tive capability for senescent cell clearance rates. Compared to the sc-ImmuAging and SCALE clocks, the 2A model exhibited superior predictive accuracy at the single-cell level. Organ-specific analyses identified the lungs and kidneys as particularly susceptible to aging, with immune dysfunction and programmed cell death emerging as key contribu- tors. Notably, single-cell data confirmed that plasma cell accumulation and naive-like cell reduction showed linear changes during organ aging. Aging trend genes identified in each organ were significantly enriched in aging-related functional pathways, enabling precise assessment of the aging process and determination of organ-specific aging milestones. Additionally, drug screening identified Fostamatinib, Ranolazine, and Metformin as potential modulators of multi-organ aging, with mechanisms involving key pathways such as longevity regulation and circadian rhythm. Conclusions The 2A model represents a significant advancement in aging assessment by integrating multi-dimen- sional validation strategies, enhancing its accuracy and applicability. The identification of organ-specific aging path- ways and candidate pharmacological interventions provides a theoretical foundation and translational framework for precision anti-aging therapies. Genome Medicine (2025) 17:83 Background Aging manifests itself as progressive changes in the structure and function of different organ systems, mak- ing it a key risk factor for a variety of human diseases [1, 2]. Recent studies have identified twelve major hallmarks of aging, including genome instability, telomere attri- tion, epigenetic alterations, loss of proteostasis, disabled macroautophagy, deregulated nutrient-sensing, mito- chondrial dysfunction, cellular senescence, stem cell exhaustion, altered intercellular communication, chronic inflammation, and dysbiosis [3]. However, the exact ori- gins of aging remain unclear, and the underlying molecu- lar mechanisms are not fully understood. With the global aging population, elucidating the molecular mechanisms of organ aging has emerged as a critical priority in gerontology and precision medicine [4]. The human genome is a vast repository of informa- tion, and understanding how genes change over time is critical to understanding the mechanisms of aging [5]. While many earlier studies have focused on discrete com- parisons between younger adults (aged 20–40) and older individuals (typically over 50 or 60 years of age), they often fail to capture the continuous and dynamic nature of aging as a time-dependent biological process [6, 7]. A recent article reported RNA-seq expression data col- lected from multiple organs across multiple time points. However, the potential of this time-resolved data has not been fully exploited when identifying differentially expressed genes [8]. This limitation is also evident in research on transcriptomic aging clocks, where existing models predominantly rely on gene expression profiles from readily accessible samples, such as peripheral blood or skin [9, 10]. Although these models offer insights into overall biological aging, they fail to capture the hierarchi- cal and organ-specific aspects of the aging process. In this study, we developed a novel aging assessment model by integrating time-series transcriptomic data from mice, systematically deciphering organ-specific aging molecular signatures. Unlike traditional studies that primarily focus on global aging processes, our approach emphasizes the dynamic evolutionary patterns of gene expression during organ aging. We defined two key molecular features: aging trajectory clusters, which cap- ture temporal variation patterns of organ-specific gene expression, and aging trend gene clusters, which con- sist of gene sets exhibiting significant linear correlations with age. Genes within aging trend gene clusters, termed aging trend genes, offer new insights into the heterogene- ity of organ aging. Our analysis of 16 organs revealed that each organ exhibits its own unique aging trajectory clus- ter. Upon functionally annotating these aging trajectory clusters, aging trend gene clusters showed higher enrich- ment of aging-related functions. This finding not only reveals differences in aging rates among organs, but also helps explain why certain organs are more susceptible to degeneration and dysfunction during aging. Notably, our research goes beyond descriptive observation by incor- porating immune correlation analysis, thereby uncover- ing the complex relationship between aging trend genes and immune cells. The organ-specific assessment model constructed in this study, based on trend gene clusters across multiple organs, significantly improves the accu- racy of organ-specific aging prediction compared to existing models, providing a foundational framework for developing precise anti-aging intervention strategies. To systematically analyze the functional properties of aging trajectory clusters, we focused on eight core aging- related biological processes based on consensus from existing studies. These processes include cell death regu- lation, proliferation and development, immune response, inflammatory response, hypoxic stress, DNA repair, and damage response. The selection of these processes was supported by extensive literature: the pro-survival effects of the hypoxia response can delay aging [11], the tem- poral expression of organ growth factors regulates the aging rate [12], and unrepaired DNA damage mediates cell cycle arrest through chromosome bridges [13]. Nota- bly, the decline in immune surveillance, accompanied by chronic inflammation, has been recognized as a common feature of aging across multiple organs [14, 15]. In con- trast, although biological processes such as metabolic reprogramming and neural plasticity play significant roles in the aging of specific organs [16, 17], their cross- organ universality is insufficient, and thus, they were not included in our analytical framework. By constructing a regulatory network of these eight functional modules and aging trend genes, this study establishes a multidimen- sional analytical framework for organ aging, providing a systematic research platform for elucidating the molecu- lar interactions underlying the aging process. The aging assessment model developed in this study demonstrates significant potential for predicting the effi- cacy of anti-aging drugs. Notably, the core global aging up-trend genes identified in our study exhibit strong associations with immune regulation and inflamma- tory response pathways, underscoring their mechanis- tic relevance to contemporary anti-aging drug targets. Previous studies have shown that targeting the inflam- matory microenvironment and redox homeostasis can effectively extend lifespan in model organisms [18, 19]. For instance, metformin activates the AMPK pathway to regulate mitochondrial metabolic reprogramming and concurrently inhibits NF-ƙB-mediated immune- inflammatory responses [20]. Rapamycin mitigates aging-related immune cell phenotypes by blocking the mTORC1 signaling axis, whose dysregulation is closely Page 3 of 21 Xu et al. Genome Medicine (2025) 17:83 associated with organ damage and myeloid inflamma- tion progression [21]. Additionally, the senolytic agent fisetin selectively eliminates senescent cells through the p53/p21-mitochondrial autophagy axis, significantly reducing organ inflammation levels [22]. These findings not only validate the biological significance of the model- identified screening genes but also highlight the potential for multi-target synergistic interventions in anti-aging therapies. Our study provides a theoretical foundation for developing novel intervention strategies aimed at mit- igating age-related deterioration. Building on these findings, we introduce an innovative drug screening model designed to identify customized therapeutics tailored to the unique aging character- istics of each organ. This model not only deepens our understanding of potential therapeutic targets but also advances the development of precision medicine in aging-related health interventions. Our aim is to estab- lish targeted interventions and create a foundation for achieving the therapeutic goal of “lightening the body and prolonging life.” This research not only provides valuable insights for the application of therapeutics in anti-aging efforts but also presents novel directions and possibilities for future research in drug development and the progression of the anti-aging field. Methods Data collection Transcriptomic data used in this study were sourced from two publicly available repositories: Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) (https://​www.​ncbi.​nlm.​nih.​gov/​geo/) [23] and Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) (http://​ commo​nfund.​nih.​gov/​GTEx/) [24]. The primary dataset analyzed was GSE132040 [8], which offers a compre- hensive spatiotemporal transcriptomic atlas of 16 mouse organs across the lifespan, ranging from 1 to 27 months of age (n = 891). This dataset served as the basis for iden- tifying organ-specific aging trend genes. To rigorously evaluate the robustness and generalizabil- ity of the proposed model, we implemented a hierarchical validation strategy incorporating multiple independent datasets. The GSE34378 [25] dataset (five mouse organs, aged 13 to 130 weeks, n = 90) was employed to assess age-associated dynamics in immune cell composition. Datasets GSE12480 (young vs. old mouse hearts, n = 20) and GSE199493 [26] (young vs. old mouse bone, n = 25) were used to validate the applicability of the 2A model across different organ systems and its capacity to distin- guish aging scores. The GSE141252 dataset, comprising transcriptomic profiles from 17 mouse organs across ages 4 to 24 months (n = 518), was applied to delineate organ- specific versus systemic aging patterns. Additionally, GSE178115 [27], which includes gene expression profiles of human fibroblasts treated with KU60019, was utilized to explore the association between the 2A score and the efficiency of senescent cell clearance. For cross-species evaluation, transcriptomic data from the GTEx project were incorporated, encompassing adipose, kidney, pitui- tary, and brain tissues from a total of 2448 human sam- ples stratified into young (20–39 years) and old (60–79 years) age groups [24]. This enabled evaluation of the cross-species generalizability of the 2A model. At the single-cell level, several datasets were integrated to investigate cell type-specific aging mechanisms. These included GSE145562 (mouse spleen and lung tissues, n = 12) [28], GSE241718 (mouse subcutaneous adipose tissue, n = 4) [29], and GSE215001 (human mesenteric adipose tissue, n = 2). Furthermore, the GSE247719 data- set [30], comprising single-cell transcriptome profiles from mouse lung tissue aged 3 to 23 months (n = 47), was leveraged to benchmark the performance of the 2A model against existing single-cell aging clocks. Detailed metadata for each dataset—including sample size, sequencing platform, and original references–are pro- vided in Additional file 2: Table S1. Bulk RNA‑seq data preprocessing All raw count matrices from bulk RNA-seq datasets underwent a two-step quality control and normaliza- tion process. Initially, genes exhibiting low expression— defined as those detected in less than 20% of samples within each organ—were excluded to minimize noise from non-informative features. Subsequently, normali- zation was performed using the edgeR package, applying the counts per million (CPM) transformation followed by log2 scaling (log2-CPM). This approach accounts for variations in library size and sequencing depth, facili- tating accurate comparisons across samples. For the GSE132040 dataset, principal component analysis (PCA) was conducted using the prcomp function in R (version 4.4.2) to systematically assess the spatial distribution of transcriptomic profiles across different organs. This dimensionality reduction technique enabled visualization of global expression patterns and identification of organ- specific transcriptomic signatures. Aging trend genes We utilized bulk RNA-seq data (GSE132040) across multiple time points to construct a model for identify- ing aging trend genes. Initially, we employed the Impul- seDE2 [31] R package to pinpoint genes exhibiting significant differences across growth and development stages. Genes with adjusted p-values less than 0.01 were included as subjects for subsequent analysis, with bio- logical replicates considered as technical confounders. Subsequently, to unveil common expression patterns Page 4 of 21 Xu et al. Genome Medicine (2025) 17:83 among these genes, we applied unsupervised clustering using the Mfuzz R package and determined the optimal cluster number, k, based on appropriate criteria [32]. During functional annotation, we focused on eight major categories strongly associated with aging from the Gene Ontology (GO) database [33] and utilized KEGG path- ways [34] for further functional annotation. Finally, the Mann–Kendall nonparametric test [19] was employed to systematically identify monotonic temporal trends in gene expression. This rank-based method does not rely on data distribution assumptions, making it robust against heterogeneity and outliers in transcriptomic data. It is particularly well-suited for analyzing multi-time point aging trajectories. By quantifying the aging trajec- tory trends across 16 mouse organs, we identified genes that exhibited consistent unidirectional changes (con- tinuous increase or decrease) across multiple time points as aging trend genes, using a Mann–Kendall test signifi- cance threshold of p < 0.05. Functional annotation of aging trajectories To investigate the biological mechanisms underlying aging, we focused on eight core hallmarks: cell death, cell division, development, immunity, inflammation, hypoxia, DNA repair, and DNA damage. A novel functional anno- tation system was developed, supported by a multi-level analytical framework, to elucidate the biological signifi- cance of aging trajectories across organ systems. First, the hierarchical structure of the GO ontology database was used to extract subcategory terms for each feature, and semantic similarity analysis was used to screen semanti- cally related extended terms. Next, a cross-organ hyper- geometric test (FDR < 0.05) was performed to identify shared and organ-specific pathways, which were further visualized using heat maps. For KEGG pathway enrich- ment analysis, a hypergeometric distribution model was applied to identify significant pathways (FDR < 0.05) with the aging trajectory of each organ as the background. Manual verification was performed to ensure that the pathways were accurately mapped to the aging trajectory. Analysis of aging stages based on aging trend genes Utilizing organ-specific aging trend genes and organ expression data, we applied the K-means method from the “ConsensusClusterPlus” R package for unsupervised clustering analysis of the samples. This approach enabled us to investigate the contribution of aging trend genes to defining aging stages. The optimal clustering of the organ aging stages was determined using cumulative distribu- tion function curves. To ensure the stability of the clus- tering, this analysis was iterated 1000 times. Additionally, principal component analysis (PCA) was performed on different aging stages to identify differences between clusters. CIBERSORT analysis We conducted immune infiltration analysis on organ aging expression data using the 22 immune cell signa- ture gene sets (LM22) to explore the effects of aging on the immune system at different stages. The analysis was performed with the CIBERSORT R package, employ- ing parameters set to 1000 permutations and quantile normalization. Additionally, we applied Min–Max nor- malization to the cell proportions of each sample, scaling the values between 0 and 1, ensuring they sum to 1. This approach allowed us to derive the relative score for each cell type [34]. ssGSEA enrichment analysis We conducted single-sample gene set enrichment analy- sis (ssGSEA) using the R package GSVA to quantify the expression levels of 12 programmed cell death pathways across various organs. Subsequently, we assessed the cor- relations between these pathways based on their scores in each organ. Single‑cell quality control and pretreatment analysis This study integrated three single-cell transcriptome datasets to construct a multi-tissue immune landscape, enabling validation of aging trend gene expression at the single-cell level. Spleen and lung tissue data were obtained from GSE145562 [28], mesenteric adipose tissue from GSE215001, and subcutaneous adipose tissue from GSE241718 [29]. Each dataset included control samples from both young and aged groups. For data analysis, we utilized the R package Seurat [35] and applied the follow- ing criteria to identify and filter low-quality cells: (1) cells with a feature count less than 200 or greater than 3000, and (2) cells with a proportion of mitochondrial genes exceeding 6%. After applying these criteria, we retained high-quality cells for subsequent analysis. We utilized the R package “Harmony” to mitigate batch effects from the data and subsequently conducted data standardization, clustering, and visualization [36]. By referencing reported cell markers and cellmarker2.0 databases, we manually annotated distinct cell clusters [37]. Quantification of expression of aging trend genes In the single-cell transcriptomic datasets, we utilized the AUcell [38] tool to calculate aging trend gene scores for each cell, based on organ-specific aging trend genes. This analysis produced two scores for each cell: the Rising gene score and the Declining gene score. Cells with the top 10% scores in each gene set were then classified into corresponding trend cell clusters. Page 5 of 21 Xu et al. Our inputs included annotated single-cell data and bulk RNA-seq data, with the latter requiring prediction of cell proportions. The deconvolution module utilized cell type-specific expression profiles from single-cell data to jointly estimate the cell type composition of bulk RNA- seq expression and the posterior distribution of cell type-specific gene expression. Calculations of cell type proportions were performed using default parameters. Effectiveness of the 2A model in predicting senescent cell clearance To assess the effectiveness of the 2A model in predict- ing senescent cell clearance, we analyzed the GSE178215 dataset, which includes transcriptomic profiles of human dermal fibroblasts treated with KU60019 and untreated controls at days 3 and 8. Finally, bar plots were used to visualize the distribution of senes- cence scores across conditions, thereby validating the utility of the 2A model in predicting the effectiveness of senescent cell clearance interventions. Comparative validation of 2A model with existing aging clocks In the GSE247719 dataset, we first performed data qual- ity control and cell type annotation following standard procedures. We then applied the 2A model developed in this study, alongside two published aging clocks—sc- ImmuAging and SCALE—to assess aging at the single- cell level. The sc-ImmuAging model utilizes LASSO regression based on age-related immunosenescence marker genes in T cells, B cells, and monocytes to predict the biological age of immune cells, making it particularly suitable for evaluating immune cell aging. The SCALE model, by contrast, employs elastic net regression to inte- grate tissue-specific aging-related genes and assess both the degree of aging and intercellular heterogeneity based on gene expression profiles. It is widely used in tissue- level aging research. Our 2A model is a cross-organ aging scoring system constructed from time-series multi-organ RNA-seq data, and it demonstrates strong generalizabil- ity across tissues. To comprehensively compare model performance, we calculated the Pearson correlation coef- ficient between model-derived aging scores and chrono- logical age at both the organ level and corresponding cell lineage level. Additionally, to further assess the resolution of the 2A model at the single-cell level, we evaluated the correlation between its aging scores and chronological age within each annotated cell subset. Results Identifying and functionally analyzing genes with aging trends in various organs Aging is a complex, multistage process characterized by the progressive decline of organ functionality. Based on cross-organ aging research data published by Stephen R Quake’s team [8], this study systematically analyzed high-throughput RNA sequencing data from 16 mouse organs across 10 time points. Principal component analy- sis (PCA) revealed significant spatial clustering differ- ences in transcriptomic profiles among different organs (Additional file 1: Fig. S1a), suggesting that each organ may regulate the temporal progression of aging through distinct molecular pathways. To identify key regulatory genes involved in aging, we applied the ImpulseDE2 model to detect differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in each organ (Additional file 1: Fig. S1b-c) and classi- fied them into dynamically similar gene clusters using Mfuzz clustering. These gene clusters were systemati- cally annotated based on eight core aging-related biologi- cal processes: cell death, cell division, cell development, immunity, inflammation, hypoxia, DNA repair, and DNA damage. Notably, most organ-specific expression clus- ters exhibited specific enrichment in these aging-related features, with the lung and kidney showing the strongest associations (Fig. 1a, Additional file 1: Fig. S1d). Among all aging features, cell death and immune function dem- onstrated the highest levels of association (Additional file 1: Fig. S1e). Different organs exhibited distinct sen- sitivities to these aging processes. For example, bone tissue clusters were primarily enriched in cell death, cell development, and inflammatory pathways, whereas kidney clusters were predominantly associated with cell death and immune responses. This organ-specific aging signature was further evident at the intra-cluster level. Within the kidney, for instance, Clusters 1 and 4 were specifically enriched in immune and inflammatory path- ways, while Cluster 6 was primarily involved in cell divi- sion regulation (Fig. 1a, Additional file 1: Fig. S3). These findings indicate that distinct gene modules within the same organ may collaboratively drive aging through functional specialization. Further analysis of dynamic gene cluster patterns revealed a spatiotemporal coupling between gene expres- sion trends and organ physiology. In upward-trending clusters, immune and inflammatory pathways were pre- dominant. For example, Cluster 6 in the heart displayed sustained activation of Th1/Th2 cell differentiation and antigen presentation pathways, indicating an age-related imbalance in T-cell homeostasis [44]. Similarly, Clusters 1 and 4 in the kidney were enriched in chemokine signaling and Th17 cell differentiation pathways, potentially con- tributing to the development of a chronic inflammatory microenvironment [45]. In contrast, downward-trending clusters were predominantly associated with basal met- abolic functions. The progressive silencing of protein digestion and absorption pathways in Cluster 9 of the bone likely reflects a decline in anabolic capacity. Certain clusters exhibited fluctuating expression patterns, such as the intermittent activation of TCA cycle-related genes in the kidney, suggesting transient metabolic remodeling in response to aging-associated stress [46]. Notably, stage- specific high expression in certain clusters correlated with key functional transitions during aging. For exam- ple, Cluster 6 in the lung exhibited specific activation of the AMPK/PPAR signaling pathways during late-stage aging, promoting lipid metabolic reprogramming and myofibroblast differentiation [47], thereby exacerbating fibrotic pathology (Fig. 1a,b, Additional file 1: Fig. S4). Cross-organ comparisons revealed that gene clusters with linear temporal changes exhibited conserved aging- related functions, whereas fluctuating clusters were more likely to drive organ-specific processes, whose dynamic features remain challenging to capture. In contrast, resilient organs, including the skin and bone marrow, retained metabolic activity into old age, exhibiting a relatively slower aging rate (Fig. 1d, Additional file 1: Fig. S2a-e). This differen- tial aging pattern aligns with recent findings on entropy accumulation, where organs such as the spleen and liver show greater entropy increases, suggesting heightened susceptibility to aging-related degenerative changes [49]. Functional annotation analysis further substantiated the molecular basis of organ-specific aging heterogeneity (Fig. 1e). The youthful state exhibited significant enrich- ment in pathways related to cell cycle regulation and tissue development, whereas the aged state was predomi- nantly characterized by the activation of innate immune responses and inflammatory regulatory pathways. This cross-organ temporal aging pattern unveils a fundamen- tal biological principle: mammalian aging is character- ized by a systematic transition from an anabolism-driven developmental program to a survival strategy dominated by inflammatory defense. This discovery not only eluci- dates the dynamic characteristics of organ-specific aging but, more importantly, establishes a theoretical frame- work for precisely regulated spatiotemporal aging inter- vention strategies. Through an analysis of age-associated co-expression patterns of organ-specific aging trend gene sets, we identified significant positive correlations among all organs except bone marrow. The temporal expression similarity of aging trend gene sets across different organs revealed a significant degree of overlap (Fig. 3a). During the identification of conserved cross-organ aging markers, we determined that while organs retained tissue specificity, they also shared core regulatory modules (Fig. 3a). This led us to define 114 global aging trend genes (GATGs), which display consist- ent trend alterations across at least four organs (Fig. 3b, Additional file 1: S11a). Among these, immune response genes like Ighm, Rbm3, and Igkc increased in 8 organs, while Col3a1, related to collagen synthesis, decreased in 7 organs. Additionally, the GATGs include several known aging marker genes, such as Ccl8 and Tnfrsf1b, and some immune cell markers like Jchain and Cd22 (Fig. 3b, Addi- tional file 2: Table S3). Notably, the kidney, liver, lung, and small intestine accounted for the majority of GATGs, primarily comprising antigen presentation-related genes (Cd74, H2-DMa) and immunoglobulin complex genes (Igkc). The observed inter-organ molecular coordina- tion may originate from shared transcriptional regulatory networks established during embryonic development. Functional enrichment analysis revealed that upregulated genes were predominantly associated with immune- related pathways, including lymphocyte-mediated immune responses, whereas downregulated genes were primarily enriched in protein synthesis pathways, such as collagen fibril organization (Fig. 3c). This finding aligns closely with the classical theory of chronic inflamma- tory activation and anabolic decline during aging, further reinforcing the hypothesis of coordinated multi-organ aging [45, 53]. Subsequently, we established a novel multi-organ aging assessment model (2A Model) using global aging trend genes to characterize the overall aging status of each organ (see Methods for details). We calculated the 2A Model score for each organ and conducted a classification evaluation. The results showed that 2A Model scores were consistently higher in aging stages compared to young stages, with scores increasing progressively over time (Fig. 3d, Additional file 1: S9, S10). Sensitivity to the 2A Model score varied among organs: white blood cells (WBC), spleen, and bone marrow exhibited higher scores, consistent with their roles as primary sources of systemic inflammation. In contrast, the brain and lungs had lower 2A Model scores, potentially due to the pro- tective effect of the blood–brain barrier and the special- ized regulatory mechanisms of alveolar macrophages. The 2A Model offers a valuable method for tracking the overall aging process and indirectly reflecting age-related changes across different organs. Analysis revealed that, compared to the young stage, both organs exhibited upregulation of certain aging markers, such as Ccl8 and Ighm, during aging. How- ever, the classical aging marker Cdkn2a did not exhibit the anticipated upregulation with aging, likely due to its low expression level in bulk RNA-seq (Fig. 3g). The 2A model reveals multi‑tissue robustness and single‑cell heterogeneity in aging across species To assess the multi-tissue applicability of global aging trend genes, this study utilized human mRNA-seq data from adipose tissue, kidney, pituitary gland, and brain, obtained from the GTEx database. Samples were cat- egorized into young (20–39 years old) and old (60–79 years old) groups based on age, and the recognition effi- ciency of global aging trend genes in the aging process was analyzed (Fig. 4a–d). The results demonstrated that global aging trend genes, along with the SenMayo gene sets, were significantly enriched across all four tissues (p < 0.001). In contrast, GeneAge exhibited significant enrichment in adipose tissue, kidney and pituitary gland (p < 0.001, 0.05, < 0.001, respectively) but did not reach statistical significance in the brain (p = 0.95). Similarly, Aging Atlas showed significant enrichment in adipose tissue and pituitary gland (p < 0.001) but did not achieve statistical significance in kidney and brain (p = 0.37, 0.94) (Fig. 4a–d, Additional file 1: S11e-h). In further analy- sis, we calculated the aging scores every 10 years based on the 2A scoring model. The results indicated that the aging scores of these organs showed an upward trend with age, particularly in the kidneys (Fig. 4e–h). By studying a human cohort [27], we further vali- dated the effectiveness of the 2A scoring model in predicting senescent cell clearance. Neonatal human dermal fibroblasts were cultured to senescence and treated with the anti-aging ATM inhibitor KU-60019. Gene expression changes were measured on days 3 and 8 in both the drug-treated and control groups, each with 3 samples (Fig. 4i). Results showed a significant increase in the 2A model score over time (P = 0.029), and a significant decrease after anti-aging treatment (P = 0.00075) (Fig. 4j). In contrast, GSEA enrichment analysis using individual global aging trend genes failed to effectively identify aging status (Additional file 1: Fig. S11i-j). These findings demonstrate that the 2A scoring model not only assesses overall tissue senescence but also shows significant reductions following senescent cell clearance. To comprehensively evaluate the performance of the 2A scoring model in assessing aging status at single-cell resolution, we integrated and analyzed a recently pub- lished single-cell transcriptomic dataset of mouse lung tissue across multiple time points [55]. Preliminary find- ings revealed that, with increasing age, globally upregu- lated aging-associated genes exhibited a marked increase in expression across both tissues and their corresponding cell types, whereas downregulated genes demonstrated consistent attenuation patterns (Additional file 1: Fig. S12). Notably, at the cell subpopulation level, the 2A model effectively captured differential aging trajectories across diverse cell lineages, underscoring its unique advantage and methodological advancement in uncovering cellular heterogeneity during the aging process. Our study identified significant time-dependent characteristics in aging trend genes, indicating that anti- aging drugs targeting these genes might produce endur- ing effects. To capitalize on this, we developed a drug screening model integrating the restart random walk algorithm with iGSEA, tailored to aging trend genes. We used organ-specific and global aging trend genes as inputs, aligning them with corresponding drug targets to construct unique drug-target networks for each organ. By employing adjacency matrices and the random walk algorithm, we calculated drug scores for each organ. Additionally, we sourced gene expression data from drug-treated human cell lines via the Connectivity Map (CMAP) database to derive drug-gene features. Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was then applied using the respective organ-specific aging trend genes to assess the regulatory impact of drugs on aging (see Methods for details) (Fig. 5a, Additional file 2: Table S4). We visualized the top 10 drugs and their target net- works for each organ, revealing unique drug-target pro- files for each organ and highlighting organ-specific aging characteristics (Fig. 5b,c). Notably, we identified potential anti-aging drugs common to multiple organs, including Fostamatinib, Ranolazine, and Metformin. Fostamatinib, associated with aging in 13 organs, is primarily used for chronic immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) and rheuma- toid arthritis (RA) by inhibiting the Syk signaling path- way [58]. In further analysis, we detailed the frequency of drugs across different organs and identified 27 drugs associated with aging in two or more organs, with Fos- tamatinib and Ranolazine at the forefront (Fig. 5d). Enrichment analysis revealed that Fostamatinib pri- marily targets pathways related to longevity regula- tion, circadian rhythm, autophagy, and immunity, while Ranolazine is focused on myocardial contrac- tion, Alzheimer’s disease, and circadian rhythms, all of which are linked to aging (Fig. 5e). We then validated our 2A scoring model using the drug genome, analyz- ing scores for Panobinostat, which promotes aging, and Metformin, known for its anti-aging effects. Results showed that Panobinostat increased aging GATG scores, while Metformin decreased them, confirming the effectiveness of our 2A scoring model (Fig. 5f,g). To validate the accuracy of the drug screening model in this study, we integrated aging trend genes into the CMAP database for small-molecule drug prediction and cross-referenced the multi-organ-associated candi- dates identified by CMAP with our model’s predictions (Additional file 1: S14b). The analysis revealed a strong functional correlation between the CMAP-predicted anti-aging drugs and our model’s screening results. Notably, drugs targeting the mitochondrial respiratory chain complex were identified as potential anti-aging candidates across multiple organs, aligning closely with the mechanisms of metformin and ranolazine, two compounds highlighted by our model [59]. Metformin, for instance, activates the AMPK pathway by inhibit- ing mitochondrial complex I (NADH dehydrogenase), thereby enhancing insulin sensitivity and regulating glucose metabolism [60]. Similarly, celecoxib, a COX-2 inhibitor, mitigates chronic inflammation by suppress- ing IKK kinase activity, a mechanism consistent with the regulatory framework of aging-related inflamma- tory pathways [61]. To further assess the model’s relia- bility, we conducted organ-specific enrichment analyses on genes exhibiting temporal expression differences under the influence of the identified drugs (Additional file 1: S14c). Notably, the organ-specific drugs pre- dicted by our model effectively distinguished the aging status of the corresponding organs while demonstrating the model’s capability to elucidate organ-specific aging regulatory networks at the molecular level. Overall, our study underscores the potential of uti- lizing organ aging trend gene sets to identify and characterize aging-related drug, thereby aiding in the development of novel interventions against aging. This study dem- onstrates the spatiotemporal coupling of gene expres- sion dynamics during aging through the construction of a cross-organ temporal transcriptional map and proposes the theoretical framework of “development- defense conversion.” This framework posits that aging is not merely a process of functional decline but rather a systematic reprogramming, transitioning from a developmentally driven anabolic program to a survival strategy dominated by inflammatory defense. Notably, this conversion exhibits significant organ heterogene- ity. Vulnerable organs, such as the spleen and kidneys, engage immune-inflammatory pathways early, thereby accelerating the aging process [49], whereas the brain may sustain neural plasticity through neuroprotec- tive mechanisms mediated by the blood–brain barrier, delaying degenerative changes [62]. At the molecular level, this heterogeneity is characterized by the spati- otemporal interaction of fluctuating and linear trend gene clusters. For instance, the intermittent activation of TCA cycle-related genes in the kidney suggests that metabolic elasticity serves as a crucial buffering mecha- nism against organ aging, aligning with the recently proposed “metabolic elasticity buffering aging” hypoth- esis [63]. The progressive functional decline of the immune sys- tem is a hallmark of aging and has predominantly been investigated in the context of T cell exhaustion [64] and myeloid inflammatory activation [65]. This study iden- tified a spatiotemporal coupling between plasma cell accumulation and immature lymphocyte exhaustion, establishing a novel research paradigm centered on B cells in immune aging. Pseudo-time trajectory analysis of splenic B cells revealed that aging drives transitional B cells toward terminal plasma cell differentiation. This imbalance in differentiation aggravates immune dys- function through two distinct mechanisms: excessive antibody secretion by plasma cells promotes a chronic inflammatory microenvironment, while the deple- tion of activated B cells compromises antigen-specific immune responses. Importantly, defective autophagy in aging plasma cells results in impaired clearance, per- petuating a vicious cycle of “inflammation-retention” [66]. This “bidirectional imbalance” theory provides an integrative framework for understanding age-related immune deficiencies and addresses the gap in current immune aging models by incorporating the regulatory role of B cells. In the context of aging clock modeling, the organ- specific aging trend gene (GATGs) model proposed in this study demonstrates distinct advantages. Com- pared with the peripheral blood mononuclear cell transcriptome clock developed by Peters et al. [9]. and the dermal fibroblast classification model constructed by Fleischer’s team [10], the 2A model extends aging assessment from static time points to a continuous tem- poral framework by integrating the dynamic expression profiles of multiple organs. Notably, the discrimination efficiency of this model at single-cell resolution sur- passes that of the sc-ImmuAging clock released in 2025 [56], validating its superiority in capturing microscopic aging heterogeneity. Additionally, the temporal cor- relation of scores between lung vascular endothelial cells and type II alveolar epithelial cells is the highest, suggesting that these cell subpopulations may serve as early indicators of organ aging. Notably, the 2A model exhibits dual advantages in predicting the efficacy of anti-aging drugs and assess- ing the clearance of senescent cells. The drug screen- ing framework, developed based on core aging trend genes, effectively elucidates targeted regulatory strate- gies within organ-specific aging networks. Among the candidate compounds, fostamatinib exhibits multidi- mensional anti-aging potential. As a selective spleen tyrosine kinase (SYK) inhibitor, it prevents the terminal differentiation of plasma cells by inhibiting B cell recep- tor signaling, thereby mitigating chronic inflammation [58]. Ranolazine mitigates intracellular calcium overload in cardiomyocytes by inhibiting the late sodium current (INa-L) and concurrently activates the PPAR-δ/CPT1β axis to promote fatty acid oxidation [67]. Metformin, a well-established anti-aging candidate, induces autophagy via the AMPK/mTORC1 axis and reverses renal epi- genetic aging in the cynomolgus monkey model [68]. Despite their demonstrated efficacy in model organisms, the clinical translation of candidate drugs necessitates a systematic evaluation of organ-specific risks and benefits. While fostamatinib demonstrated safety in a phase II trial for rheumatoid arthritis [69], prolonged inhibition of the SYK pathway may disrupt hematopoietic stem cell home- ostasis, necessitating careful monitoring for infection risk in elderly populations. The age-dependent effects of met- formin are particularly pronounced. Recent cohort stud- ies suggest that it may exacerbate metabolic inflexibility in older individuals due to dysbiosis of the gut microbiota [70]. Furthermore, drug repurposing poses challenges due to conflicting indications. For instance, prolonged metformin use in non-diabetic individuals may result in elevated liver enzyme levels and dysregulated lactate metabolism [71]. Our study has certain limitations. This study primar- ily focuses on genes exhibiting continuous trend changes Page 19 of 21 Xu et al. Genome Medicine (2025) 17:83 throughout the aging process, potentially overlooking genes that undergo transient or stage-specific alterations. However, given that aging trend genes are implicated in over 70% of aging-related biological functions, this limi- tation is partially mitigated. The dataset on organ aging trend genes is derived from mouse aging studies, encom- passing only 16 organs. Dependence on a single dataset and the restricted number of organs represent additional limitations. However, validation of the 2A model’s robustness across additional human aging datasets may address this limitation. Furthermore, the absence of organ-level drug expression data poses challenges for studying organ-specific pharmacological effects, and the potential bias introduced by relying on treated cell line expression data as a proxy for organ-level research must be acknowledged. In summary, further prospective stud- ies are required to validate the functional mechanisms underlying organ aging and the predictive accuracy of the 2A model in aging assessment. Conclusions This study presents a comprehensive aging assessment framework that integrates multi-organ transcriptomic trajectories with functional gene signatures to reveal the molecular heterogeneity of aging across tissues. The proposed 2A model demonstrates robust performance in quantifying aging states at both bulk and single-cell levels, outperforming existing models in sensitivity and resolution. By identifying aging trend genes and key func- tional hallmarks—such as immune dysregulation and cell death—we provide mechanistic insights into organ-spe- cific vulnerability to aging. Importantly, we extend the utility of the 2A model to pharmacogenomic contexts, enabling the identification of candidate compounds with potential senolytic or anti-aging properties. Together, our findings offer a scalable and translationally relevant strat- egy for aging assessment and therapeutic screening, with implications for promoting healthy longevity and inform- ing precision interventions for age-related decline.
