# Frontier review — fasting

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-17T05-49-34Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

Fasting is not a uniform intervention but a context-dependent modulator of health, where identical practices yield therapeutic nutritional benefits in cancer cohorts while precipitating acute metabolic crises in non-compliant diabetic populations, revealing a critical tension between patient autonomy and clinical safety.

## Already known — do not publish

- General metabolic effects of fasting
- Ramadan fasting as a religious observance
- Basic glucose regulation changes during fasting

## Tensions / contradictions

- Fact [3] shows higher prealbumin in fasting cancer patients vs non-fasting, while fact [7] indicates lowered prealbumin within the fasting group over time, suggesting possible temporal decay or measurement context differences.
- Facts [1], [2], [3] demonstrate improved nutritional and quality-of-life markers in cancer patients fasting, whereas facts [4], [8] reveal heightened hypoglycemia risk and reduced fasting compliance in high-risk diabetes patients, highlighting disease-specific risk-benefit profiles.
- Fact [5] reports high patient satisfaction with fasting despite fact [4] showing adverse outcomes, indicating a dissonance between subjective well-being and objective safety in diabetes management during Ramadan.

## Evidence gaps

- The dose-response relationship between fasting duration and prealbumin/albumin dynamics in cancer patients with fecal stomas.
- Psychological and behavioral factors driving non-compliance with medical advice in diabetic patients during Ramadan, beyond reported sadness.
- Comparative analysis of fasting effects across different cancer types or stages, as current evidence is limited to fecal stoma populations.

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- The evidence pool is too heterogeneous, with varying populations, interventions, and outcomes, limiting generalizability.
- Sample sizes in cited studies appear small, and confounding factors (e.g., cancer treatment, diabetes management) are not adequately controlled.
- The proposed thesis on compliance paradox relies heavily on Ramadan-specific data, which may not translate to other fasting contexts.

## Suggested next extractions

- Data on fasting effects in cancer patients beyond fecal stoma, including tumor types and treatment modalities.
- Detailed adherence patterns and motivational factors in diabetic patients during intermittent fasting protocols.
- Biomarker panels (e.g., albumin, prealbumin, insulin) measured at multiple time points in fasting cohorts to resolve temporal conflicts.
