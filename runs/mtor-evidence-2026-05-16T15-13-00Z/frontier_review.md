## The lens

The juxtaposition of mTOR modulation effects across species and disease contexts reveals a translational gap: while preclinical models show dramatic tumor burden reduction, clinical outcomes in specific carcinomas remain stagnant, suggesting that pathway inhibition alone is insufficient without contextual stratification. Moreover, the lethal phenotype in hypomorphic mTOR mice highlights mTOR's essential non-oncogenic functions, which may confound therapeutic strategies in cancer.

## Already known — do not publish

- mTOR is a key kinase in cell growth and metabolism
- Rapamycin inhibits mTORC1 signaling
- mTOR inhibitors are used in cancer therapy

## Tensions / contradictions

- The 72% tumor burden reduction in NNK-treated mice with metformin (fact 2848) versus the unchanged 50% 5-year survival in HNSCC patients with mTOR inhibition (fact 11840)
- High mortality (40%) in hypomorphic mTOR mice (fact 15567) contrasts with the therapeutic use of mTOR inhibitors that may suppress essential functions in healthy tissues
- Elevated mTOR phosphorylation in CML patients (fact 64215) with Rapamycin intervention, while mTOR inhibition in HNSCC (fact 11840) shows no survival improvement, indicating tissue-specific pathway dependency

## Evidence gaps

- Comparative efficacy of direct mTOR inhibitors (e.g., rapamycin) versus indirect modulators (e.g., metformin) in the same cancer model
- Lack of data on mTOR phosphorylation levels as a predictive biomarker in solid tumors like HNSCC, beyond hematological malignancies
- Underexplored dose-response relationships and timing of mTOR inhibition in human trials, particularly in head and neck squamous cell carcinomas

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- The studies cited use heterogeneous interventions (metformin, rapamycin, mTOR deficiency) and endpoints (tumor burden, survival, phosphorylation), making direct comparisons potentially misleading without controlled variables.
- The evidence is limited to a small number of studies per context, risking overgeneralization; for instance, the HNSCC survival data (fact 11840) is from a single study without subgroup analysis.
- The thesis may overlook confounding factors such as cancer subtype, patient comorbidities, or concurrent treatments that influence mTOR inhibition efficacy.
