## Methods

This synthesis was implemented using a reproducible evidence-contract pipeline and is reported against PRISMA 2020 where applicable [CIT:page-2020-prisma|method-citation]. The search strategy is designed to query [PLACEHOLDER:databases] for records spanning [PLACEHOLDER:date-range]. The query architecture will combine population terms (murine OR mouse OR mice), intervention terms (metformin), and outcome terms (lifespan OR longevity OR survival OR lifespan extension) using Boolean logic adapted to each database's controlled-vocabulary syntax. The exact query string for each platform is archived at [PLACEHOLDER:query-terms]. Grey-literature sources, including theses repositories and preprint servers, will be searched to mitigate publication bias. Forward and backward citation tracking of included studies and of two prior quantitative syntheses on metformin and murine lifespan [CIT:chen-2022-metformin-meta|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:ivimey-cook-tyshkovskiy-2025-meta|prior-meta-analysis] will supplement database retrieval.

Eligibility criteria follow a PECO framework adapted to preclinical intervention research. The population is restricted to Mus musculus of any genetic background; studies in other species or in vitro systems are excluded. The primary pooled corpus will include only studies administering metformin as the intervention; studies examining buformin, phenformin, or the broader biguanide class are excluded from primary effect-size pooling and are retained solely for a pre-specified translational sensitivity layer that examines analog evidence without combining it with metformin-specific estimates. Eligible comparators include untreated controls, vehicle-treated controls, or diet-matched controls. The primary outcome is median or mean lifespan expressed in days, weeks, or months. Studies must report a measure of central tendency and dispersion, or provide survival curves from which these statistics can be extracted. Exclusion criteria encompass studies that conflate metformin with a co-intervention not administered to control animals, studies that report only maximum lifespan without a group-level summary, and studies for which the full text cannot be retrieved after contacting the corresponding author. No restrictions on publication language or date of publication will be imposed at the screening stage [CIT:page-2020-prisma|method-citation].

Data extraction will capture the following variables from each eligible study: species and strain designation, sex composition, sample size per arm, metformin dose and unit, route of administration, age at treatment initiation, duration of treatment, lifespan outcome metric (median, mean, or both), associated dispersion measure (standard deviation, standard error, or interquartile range), and survival-curve data when group-level summaries are absent. All values will be recorded in their originally reported units; where lifespan is stated in months, the extracted value will remain in months and a post-hoc conversion to days (using a [PLACEHOLDER:conversion-factor] assumption) will be applied only for pooled estimation, with an original-unit sensitivity check to verify that unit conversion does not introduce artificial precision. Extraction will be performed independently by two assessors; disagreements will be resolved through consensus discussion, with a third-party adjudicator available if consensus is not reached [CIT:percie-du-sert-2020-arrive|method-citation]. Study-level identifiers and publication metadata will also be recorded.

Six moderators are pre-specified for meta-regression. [MODERATOR_P:dose] captures metformin dose in its reported unit (mg/kg/day or concentration in diet), extracted as a continuous covariate without arbitrary categorical breakpoints; dose–response linearity will be evaluated post hoc, and any categorization for visualization will be justified by the empirical distribution. [MODERATOR_P:sex] codes the sex composition of each treatment arm (male, female, or mixed), reflecting established sex-dependent variation in pharmacokinetics and lifespan trajectories. [MODERATOR_P:strain] records the mouse genetic background (e.g., C57BL/6, BALB/c, hybrid), which is a known source of between-study heterogeneity in aging interventions. [MODERATOR_P:onset_age] captures the age at which metformin administration commenced, expressed in the original time unit, to test whether early-onset versus late-onset exposure differentially affects lifespan. [MODERATOR_P:route] codes the administration route (dietary admixture, oral gavage, drinking water) because route influences bioavailability and effective exposure. [MODERATOR_P:duration] captures treatment duration in original time units, distinguishing lifetime exposure from fixed-duration protocols. Each moderator is justified by its biological or pharmacological relevance to metformin's mechanism of action, which involves AMPK activation, mitochondrial complex I inhibition, and reduction of reactive oxygen species [CIT:foretz-viollet-2014-mechanism|mechanism-review] [CIT:madiraju-2014-metformin-mechanism|mechanism-review], pathways plausibly sensitive to dose, timing, and route.

Risk of bias will be assessed using the SYRCLE risk-of-bias tool, which is purpose-built for animal intervention studies [CIT:hooijmans-2014-syrcle|method-citation]. The tool evaluates ten domains including sequence generation, baseline characteristics, allocation concealment, random housing, blinding of caregivers and investigators, incomplete outcome data, selective outcome reporting, and other sources of bias. Each domain will be rated as low, unclear, or high risk of bias. Reporting quality will additionally be appraised against the ARRIVE 2.0 guidelines [CIT:percie-du-sert-2020-arrive|method-citation], with item-level compliance recorded as present, absent, or not applicable. Assessments will be performed independently by two reviewers; discordant ratings will be resolved through consensus, with escalation to a third reviewer when agreement is not achieved.

Statistical synthesis will employ a multi-level random-effects meta-regression to account for within-study and between-study heterogeneity. The model specifies study as a random intercept (level 2) and, where a single study contributes multiple treatment comparisons (e.g., distinct doses or sexes), effect size as a nested observation within study (level 3). Restricted maximum-likelihood estimation will be used, implemented in the R package metafor [CIT:viechtbauer-2010-metafor|method-citation]. The Knapp–Hartung adjustment will be applied to confidence intervals and hypothesis tests to correct for small-study degrees of freedom [CIT:hartung-knapp|method-citation]. Heterogeneity will be quantified using the I² statistic and the between-study variance (τ²), reported with their confidence intervals [CIT:higgins-2003-i2|method-citation]. Fixed-effects predictors entering the model are the six pre-specified moderators: dose, sex, strain, onset age, route, and duration. Categorical moderators with fewer than [PLACEHOLDER:min-k-per-level] contributing studies at any level will be collapsed or excluded from the multivariable model to avoid overfitting, with the decision documented in the supplementary protocol. Effect sizes will be computed as the log-transformed ratio of means (ln[mean_treatment / mean_control]) with its associated sampling variance, enabling interpretation as proportional lifespan change [CIT:ivimey-cook-tyshkovskiy-2025-meta|prior-meta-analysis]. Where only survival curves are available, Tierney-estimated summary statistics will be extracted and converted to the same metric.

Sensitivity analyses are designed to evaluate the robustness of pooled estimates. Leave-one-out analysis will sequentially omit each study and re-estimate the overall effect to identify influential observations. Cook's distance and DFBETAS will serve as influence diagnostics, computed at the study level. Publication bias will be assessed visually through contour-enhanced funnel plots and statistically through Egger's regression test adapted for multi-level models [CIT:egger-1997-funnel|method-citation]. Trim-and-fill estimates will be reported as a supplementary check. Heterogeneity decomposition will report the proportion of variance attributable to between-study versus within-study sources, and prediction intervals will be provided alongside confidence intervals to characterise the range of true effects. A translational sensitivity layer will examine studies of buformin, phenformin, and other biguanides at lifespan endpoints, mapped narratively and tabulated without pooling into the primary metformin effect estimate. This translational evidence map is designed to evaluate whether the metformin-specific signal generalises across the biguanide class or is compound-specific [CIT:chen-2022-metformin-meta|prior-meta-analysis].

The tension matrix is designed to visualise moderator combinations that define biologically meaningful cells and to expose regions of the evidence space that are densely versus sparsely populated. Axes will be constructed from the two most informative moderators identified by the multivariable model (e.g., dose and onset age), with cell entries representing pooled effect estimates and their associated uncertainty. Cells containing fewer than [PLACEHOLDER:min-k-cell] studies will be flagged as underpowered and rendered with a distinct visual indicator. The matrix serves a dual interpretive function: it identifies moderator combinations that yield the largest estimated lifespan effects and highlights combinations that lack direct evidence, thereby informing recommendations for future experimental design. Sparse cells in the tension matrix will be cross-referenced with the moderator coefficient table to distinguish genuine moderator effects from artefacts of uneven evidence distribution. This interpretive feedback loop ensures that the synthesis does not conflate absence of evidence with evidence of absence when guiding subsequent investigation [CIT:schunemann-grade|method-citation].
