## The lens

Autophagy presents a therapeutic paradox: its inhibition potentiates cytotoxicity in glioma while autophagy-related lncRNA signatures predict poor prognosis in lung adenocarcinoma and impaired autophagy flux hinders muscle recovery, revealing context-dependent roles that challenge monolithic therapeutic approaches.

## Already known — do not publish

- Autophagy is a fundamental cellular degradation process
- Autophagy generally promotes cell survival under stress

## Tensions / contradictions

- In glioma cells (fact 4), autophagy inhibition increases cytotoxicity of MK-2206 + gefitinib, suggesting a cytoprotective role, whereas in lung adenocarcinoma (facts 1,3), high autophagy-related lncRNA expression correlates with worse survival, indicating a pathogenic role.
- Reduced autophagy flux in injured muscles (fact 2) contrasts with effective ubiquitin-mediated selective autophagy targeting Mtb in macrophages (fact 5), highlighting tissue-specific autophagy responses.

## Evidence gaps

- Dose-response relationships for autophagy inhibitors like 3-MA or beclin 1 silencing across different cancer subtypes and injury models.
- Timing of autophagy modulation in traumatic muscle injury for optimal regeneration.
- Species-specific differences in autophagy pathways between mouse models (fact 2) and human clinical data (facts 1,3).
