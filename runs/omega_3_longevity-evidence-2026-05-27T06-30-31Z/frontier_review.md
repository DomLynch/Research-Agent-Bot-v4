# Frontier review — omega_3_longevity

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-27T06-30-31Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The evidence base presents a paradox where omega-3 shows benefits in subclinical aging markers (physical function, cognition) but hints of harm in mortality quintiles, suggesting the 'longevity' question is misaligned with existing measurements. A publishable synthesis would need to reframe omega-3 not as a universal longevity intervention but as a context-dependent modulator of specific aging trajectories, where effect direction depends on baseline status, dose threshold, and outcome domain.

## Already known — do not publish

- Omega-3 lowers triglycerides in meta-analyses
- Omega-3 shows modest benefits for physical function in older adults
- High-dose omega-3 is used for hypertriglyceridemia

## Tensions / contradictions

- Highest quintiles of omega-3 intake associated with 26% higher total mortality (95% CI 15-38%) and 31% higher CVD mortality (10-55%) vs. lowest quintiles, contradicting RCTs showing functional benefits.
- Dose discrepancy: functional benefits (muscle mass, walking speed) appear at >2 g/day, but potential mortality risks are observed in population quintiles that likely include lower habitual intakes.
- Glycemic conflict: omega-3 reduces HbA1c in some analyses but increases fasting blood glucose (+16.14 mg/dL) in others, suggesting subgroup-specific metabolic effects.

## Evidence gaps

- No direct evidence on omega-3 dose-response for all-cause mortality in prefrail vs. robust older adults.
- Effect of omega-3 on mortality outcomes in participants stratified by baseline FADS genotype or omega-3 index.
- Long-term (>2 years) trials of high-dose (4g/day) omega-3 with mortality and frailty transitions as primary endpoints.

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- Without any aggregated evidence facts, any proposed thesis is entirely speculative and violates the principle of evidence-based synthesis.
- The alpha hints originate from disparate populations (CVD patients, diabetics, MCI adults, prefrail) and cannot be combined to support a unified longevity thesis.
- Proposing a thesis based on hints alone would conflate correlation with causation and ignore critical confounders like baseline health and competing risks.

## Suggested next extractions

- Omega-3 dose-response data (especially 1-4g/day) and all-cause mortality in RCTs with ≥2-year follow-up.
- Subgroup analyses of omega-3 trials by baseline frailty status (prefrail vs. non-frail) with mortality or hospitalization endpoints.
- Omega-3 supplementation trials in populations with elevated inflammatory biomarkers (e.g., IL-6, CRP >3 mg/L) and longevity-related outcomes.
