# Frontier review — dasatinib

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-27T09-28-15Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The stark contrast between the 80.7% overall survival with dasatinib-blinatumomab in adults and the 36% 5-year survival in patients >55 years on dasatinib-chemotherapy exposes a critical age-dependent efficacy gap that current trial designs are not adequately addressing, suggesting that treatment intensification via immunotherapy may fail to overcome age-related biological barriers in Ph+ ALL.

## Already known — do not publish

- Dasatinib achieves high complete remission rates in Philadelphia-positive ALL
- Newer combinations of dasatinib with immunotherapy improve survival metrics in adult patients

## Tensions / contradictions

- The 2023 JCO study reports 80.7% overall survival with dasatinib-blinatumomab in adults, while the 2016 Blood study shows only 36% 5-year survival in patients >55 years on dasatinib-chemotherapy, highlighting a major age-related disparity.
- Despite 96% complete remission in elderly patients (Blood 2016), long-term survival remains poor, indicating that remission induction alone is insufficient for this subgroup.

## Evidence gaps

- Long-term outcomes and safety data for dasatinib-blinatumomab in patients over 55 years, which are absent from current evidence.
- Integration of molecular response monitoring, such as BCR-ABL1 transcript reduction, into elderly treatment protocols to guide therapy adaptation.
- Comparative effectiveness of dasatinib dosing strategies (e.g., 140 mg/day vs. lower doses) in older adults to balance efficacy and tolerability.

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- The 2016 Blood study used low-intensity chemotherapy alongside dasatinib, which may confound direct comparisons with newer immunotherapy combinations, making age-specific conclusions tentative.
- Evidence for elderly patients is limited to a single study (Blood 2016) with potential selection bias, reducing the generalizability of any age-focused thesis.
- The absence of data on dasatinib-blinatumomab in patients over 55 years creates a major evidence gap that undermines claims about treatment optimization for this subgroup.

## Suggested next extractions

- Outcomes and adverse events in patients over 60 years treated with dasatinib-blinatumomab or other targeted immunotherapies
- Dose-response relationships and tolerability of dasatinib in elderly Ph+ ALL cohorts, particularly with doses below 140 mg/day
- Genomic or proteomic predictors of resistance or relapse in older adults on dasatinib-based regimens, such as T315I mutation prevalence
