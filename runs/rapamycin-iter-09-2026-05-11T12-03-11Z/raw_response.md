===TITLE===
Systematic Synthesis of Rapamycin Effects on Murine Lifespan by Experimental Moderator

===ABSTRACT===
To what extent does rapamycin consistently extend lifespan across heterogeneous murine experimental designs? Prior syntheses have identified a general pro-longevity effect but have not fully disentangled the sources of heterogeneity tied to specific experimental parameters [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis]. This registered report describes a pre-specified meta-analysis designed to quantify these sources of variation. The planned corpus is derived from a systematic search, with screening counts to be reported as [N_SCREENED] and [N_ACCEPTED], yielding an expected [K_STUDIES] independent effect estimates. The analysis will employ a multi-level mixed-effects model to partition variance by pre-specified moderators. This framework is designed to test the joint influence of biological variables (mouse sex, genetic background) and methodological factors (rapamycin dose, treatment initiation age, dietary conditions) on the pooled effect size. Tension matrices will map where these factors produce divergent outcomes. The design's boundary conditions interrogate whether the lifespan effect is conditional on the intersection of sex and strain, or on the timing of intervention relative to chronological age. Findings from this pre-specified moderator analysis will pinpoint critical gaps in the existing murine evidence base, guiding the design of future experiments needed to confirm or refute observed conditional effects.

===INTRODUCTION===
The mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway is a central integrator of nutrient sensing, growth, and cellular maintenance, and its inhibition by the macrolide compound rapamycin has emerged as one of the most robust pharmacological interventions to extend lifespan in model organisms [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor|mechanism-review] [CIT:saxton-2017-mtor|mechanism-review]. Initial findings demonstrated that late-life administration of rapamycin could extend the lifespan of genetically heterogeneous mice [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin|primary-study], a result that catalyzed extensive subsequent research. The translational significance of these murine findings is underscored by analogous observations across species [CIT:strong-2008-itp|primary-study] and the initiation of human clinical trials testing rapalogs for immune and aging-related indications [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus|clinical-trial] [CIT:mannick-2018-rtb101|clinical-trial]. Consequently, a precise understanding of the experimental conditions that govern the magnitude and consistency of rapamycin's lifespan effect in mice is critical for prioritizing mechanistic research and informing potential human application.

The existing literature includes several quantitative syntheses that have aggregated findings across murine rapamycin studies. Swindell [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis] performed a meta-analysis focusing on studies published through 2015, confirming a significant overall lifespan extension but noting substantial heterogeneity. More recently, Fok and colleagues [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis] updated this synthesis, incorporating more recent studies and reaffirming the average effect while highlighting that variability across experiments remained poorly explained by single-moderator analyses. These prior meta-analyses have been instrumental in establishing the field's baseline consensus. They have identified key variables of interest, such as sex and initiation age, but their ability to dissect interactive or conditional effects has been constrained by analytical approaches and the evolving size of the primary literature. A focused narrative review has also cataloged the breadth of experimental designs employed [CIT:arriola-apelo-2016-review|narrative-review], implicitly underscoring the methodological diversity that contributes to outcome heterogeneity.

The contribution of the present work is to extend these prior syntheses by applying a pre-specified, joint moderator framework to a contemporary corpus of murine rapamycin lifespan studies. Existing meta-analyses have primarily assessed moderators in isolation; the current analysis is designed to estimate their combined and interactive influence using a multi-level mixed-effects model. This approach directly addresses the gap between recognizing that heterogeneity exists and quantifying its architectural roots within the experimental design space. The goal is not to re-establish the average effect, which is well-documented, but to map the conditional landscape—examining whether the effect size is reliably modulated by the conjunction of, for instance, sex, strain, and the timing of treatment initiation.

The scope of this synthesis is explicitly bounded by the murine rapamycin literature concerning lifespan as a primary endpoint. Non-goals include a comprehensive review of rapamycin's mechanistic actions at the molecular level, which are covered elsewhere [CIT:saxton-2017-mtor|mechanism-review], or an analysis of healthspan metrics not directly tied to mortality. Furthermore, this work does not aim to synthesize the broader literature on other mTOR inhibitors or caloric restriction mimetics, though its methodological framework may be applicable to those domains. By focusing on a single, well-defined intervention and endpoint, the analysis aims to produce maximally precise estimates of conditional effects.

The planned analysis is structured to answer several specific questions: Does the lifespan effect size differ significantly between male and female mice, and does this difference depend on the genetic background used? Is the benefit of rapamycin initiation at a late-life age equivalent to that from a younger starting age, and how does this interact with the dose administered? These questions are critical for interpreting the translational potential of murine findings, as they probe the generalizability of the effect across fundamental biological and experimental axes [CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin|primary-study] [CIT:miller-2011-rapamycin|primary-study] [CIT:bitto-2016-rapamycin|primary-study].

Ultimately, the output of this pre-specified model is designed to quantify the relative contribution of each moderator to the observed variance. The resulting identification of significant interactive effects will generate specific, testable hypotheses for future primary studies. For example, if the analysis estimates that the joint moderator of sex and strain accounts for a substantial portion of heterogeneity, it would argue for the design of subsequent lifespan experiments that explicitly cross these factors. In this way, the synthesis serves as a formal tool for directing the next cycle of empirical investigation in this field.
