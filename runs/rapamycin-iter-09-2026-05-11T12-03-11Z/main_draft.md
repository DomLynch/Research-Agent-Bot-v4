# Rapamycin and Murine Lifespan: A Pre-Specified Meta-Regression of Sex, Strain, Dose, Route, and Treatment Timing

## Abstract

To what extent does rapamycin consistently extend lifespan across heterogeneous murine experimental designs? Two prior quantitative syntheses [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis; CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis] established a positive pooled effect but have not provided a fully powered, pre-specified joint moderator framework capable of estimating the relative contribution of sex, strain, dose, route, and treatment timing within a single analytic specification. A more recent cross-species meta-analysis [CIT:tyshkovskiy-2025-vertebrate-meta|prior-meta-analysis] broadened the comparative frame across species and interventions but did not replace the need for a murine-specific moderator synthesis. This registered report describes a pre-specified meta-regression designed to quantify the conditional structure of the rapamycin lifespan effect in mice. The planned analysis is designed to estimate which moderator combinations may strengthen or attenuate the lifespan effect, directing future primary studies toward the least-resolved cells of the moderator space and informing translational reasoning about human geroscience trials [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus|clinical-trial].

## Introduction

The mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway is a central integrator of nutrient sensing, growth, and cellular maintenance, and its inhibition by the macrolide compound rapamycin has emerged as one of the most robust pharmacological interventions to extend lifespan in model organisms [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor|mechanism-review; CIT:saxton-2017-mtor|mechanism-review]. Initial findings demonstrated that late-life administration of rapamycin could extend the lifespan of genetically heterogeneous mice [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin|primary-study], a result that catalyzed extensive subsequent research. The translational significance of these murine findings is underscored by analogous Interventions Testing Program cohorts [CIT:strong-2008-itp|primary-study] and the initiation of human clinical trials testing rapalogs for immune and aging-related indications [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus|clinical-trial; CIT:mannick-2018-rtb101|clinical-trial]. Consequently, a precise understanding of the experimental conditions that govern the magnitude and consistency of rapamycin's lifespan effect in mice is critical for prioritizing mechanistic research and informing potential human application.

The existing literature includes several quantitative syntheses that have aggregated findings across murine rapamycin studies. Swindell [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis] performed a meta-analysis focusing on studies published through 2015, confirming a positive pooled estimate and identifying sex and genotype as candidate moderators. More recently, Fok and colleagues [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis] updated this synthesis, incorporating more recent studies and reaffirming the average effect while highlighting that variability across experiments remained poorly explained by single-moderator analyses. A more recent cross-species meta-analysis [CIT:tyshkovskiy-2025-vertebrate-meta|prior-meta-analysis] broadened the comparative frame across species and interventions but did not replace the need for a murine-specific moderator synthesis focused on sex, strain, dose, route, and treatment timing. A focused narrative review has also cataloged the breadth of experimental designs employed [CIT:arriola-apelo-2016-review|narrative-review], implicitly underscoring the methodological diversity that contributes to outcome heterogeneity.

The contribution of the present work is to extend these prior syntheses by applying a pre-specified, joint moderator framework to a contemporary corpus of murine rapamycin lifespan studies. The present analysis therefore shifts the question from whether rapamycin extends lifespan on average [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis] to which experimental conditions explain variation around that effect; a multi-level mixed-effects model is designed to estimate the relative contribution of sex, strain, dose, route, and treatment initiation timing within a single analytic specification. This approach directly addresses the gap between recognizing that heterogeneity exists and quantifying its architectural roots within the experimental design space. The goal is not to re-establish the average effect, which is well-documented [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis], but to map the conditional landscape — testing whether the effect is reliably modulated by the conjunction of sex, strain, and treatment timing [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin|primary-study].

The scope of this synthesis is explicitly bounded by the murine rapamycin literature concerning lifespan as a primary endpoint. Non-goals include a comprehensive review of rapamycin's mechanistic actions at the molecular level, which are covered elsewhere [CIT:saxton-2017-mtor|mechanism-review], or an analysis of healthspan metrics not directly tied to mortality. The synthesis also does not adjudicate among the broader family of mTOR inhibitors or caloric restriction mimetics, though its methodological framework may be applicable to those domains. By focusing on a single, well-defined intervention and endpoint, the analysis is designed to produce maximally precise estimates of conditional effects.

The planned analysis interrogates several specific questions [CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin|primary-study]. Whether the lifespan effect differs between male and female mice, and whether any such difference depends on the genetic background used, are pre-specified moderator tests [MODERATOR_P:sex; MODERATOR_P:strain]. Whether late-life rapamycin initiation produces equivalent conditional effects to younger starting ages, and how this interacts with the dose administered, are addressed under the timing-by-dose interaction [MODERATOR_P:timing-by-dose]. These questions probe the generalizability of the effect across fundamental biological and experimental axes [CIT:miller-2011-rapamycin|primary-study; CIT:bitto-2016-rapamycin|primary-study].

The output of this pre-specified model is designed to estimate the relative contribution of each moderator to observed between-study variance. Where interactive moderator effects are estimated to be substantial, the result is intended to generate specific testable hypotheses for future primary studies [CIT:strong-2008-itp|primary-study]. If the joint moderator of sex and strain is estimated to account for a substantial portion of heterogeneity, that result would argue for the design of subsequent lifespan experiments that explicitly cross these factors. In this way, the synthesis serves as a formal tool for directing the next cycle of empirical investigation in this field.
