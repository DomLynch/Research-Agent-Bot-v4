# Frontier review — NMN

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-27T09-55-07Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The complete absence of a curated evidence-fact database for NMN research renders any publishable thesis ungrounded, forcing a focus on the methodological gap in evidence synthesis rather than substantive findings.

## Already known — do not publish

- NMN is a precursor to NAD+ and boosts blood NAD concentrations
- Short-term NMN supplementation appears safe and well-tolerated in humans
- NMN shows dose-dependent effects on NAD+ metabolism and physical performance in trials

## Tensions / contradictions

- Human clinical trials (e.g., GeroScience 2022) report dose-response peaks at 600 mg for efficacy, while animal models (e.g., Scientific Reports 2019) show tissue-specific effects like skeletal muscle but not brain benefits, indicating translational disconnects.
- Mitophagy-inducing effects of NMN are highlighted in cell studies (e.g., Cell Death & Disease 2022), yet comparative efficacy against other agents like urolithin A varies by disease model, such as in prion disease contexts.

## Evidence gaps

- No evidence-fact IDs exist for dose-response thresholds in subgroups like aged humans versus healthy volunteers, particularly for biological age metrics.
- Lack of extracted data on long-term NMN effects beyond 60 days in human trials, and on specific populations such as fertility restoration in women, which is only noted in animal models.
