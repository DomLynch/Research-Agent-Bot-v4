# Frontier review — ARBs_longevity

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-27T06-31-41Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The purported link between ARBs and longevity is largely extrapolated from cardiorenal outcome trials, with no direct evidence isolating lifespan extension as an endpoint, revealing a critical gap in gerontology-focused research. This makes a publishable thesis speculative without new primary data synthesis.

## Already known — do not publish

- ARBs lower blood pressure by blocking angiotensin II receptors
- ARBs are commonly used for hypertension and heart failure management
- ARBs may reduce proteinuria in diabetic kidney disease

## Tensions / contradictions

- Discrepancy between cardiovascular mortality reductions in some trials (e.g., in heart failure) and null effects on all-cause mortality in advanced CKD populations
- Inferiority of ARBs to ACEIs in lowering all-cause death in diabetic kidney disease per meta-analyses, conflicting with assumptions of class equivalence

## Evidence gaps

- Long-term prospective studies measuring all-cause mortality in non-diseased elderly cohorts on ARBs
- Dose- and duration-dependent effects of specific ARBs (e.g., losartan vs. valsartan) on aging biomarkers like senescence markers or telomere length
- Preclinical evidence from longevity model organisms (e.g., C. elegans, mice) testing ARBs for lifespan extension independent of hypertension

_none_

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- Lack of direct evidence from randomized controlled trials with longevity as a primary endpoint, making any meta-analysis prone to confounding by comorbidity
- Heterogeneity in ARB types, doses, and patient populations across existing trials undermines generalizable conclusions about aging effects
- Potential publication bias favoring positive cardiovascular outcomes over null mortality findings

## Suggested next extractions

- Extract all-cause mortality and cardiovascular death data from major ARB trials (e.g., LIFE, VALUE) stratified by age subgroups
- Harvest preclinical study results on ARB effects in aging models (e.g., senescence-accelerated mice)
- Identify ongoing longitudinal studies or biobank data linking ARB use to biological age clocks (e.g., epigenetic age)
