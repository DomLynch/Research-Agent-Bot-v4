# Frontier review — spermidine

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-15T11-14-25Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

Spermidine's conserved role in autophagy induction and polyamine transport kinetics [2,4,6] suggests a unified framework for stress resilience across kingdoms, but divergent dose-response thresholds in reproduction [1] versus agriculture [3] challenge translational extrapolation, necessitating a focus on cellular transport mechanisms as a novel intervention point.

## Already known — do not publish

- Spermidine at 0.5 mmol/L improves boar sperm motility and membrane integrity [1,7]
- Spermidine pretreatment increases rice yield in cultivars like KDML105 and Pokkali [3,9]
- SAT1 overexpression depletes spermidine and arrests HeLa cell growth [2]

## Tensions / contradictions

- Between [2] (SAT1-mediated polyamine depletion causes growth arrest in HeLa cells) and [3] (exogenous spermidine at 1 mM enhances rice yield), highlighting context-dependent effects of polyamine levels on growth
- In [6], OCT inhibitors block spermidine uptake in boar sperm, but analogous transport mechanisms in plants, such as in lime seedlings [13], remain uncharacterized, creating a gap in understanding cross-kingdom polyamine kinetics
- Autophagy blockade with 3-MA in [4] reverses spermidine's benefits in nucleus pulposus cells, while similar autophagy-dependent mechanisms in mouse models [8] are not directly linked to spermidine, indicating mechanistic inconsistency

## Evidence gaps

- No studies on spermidine's role in human seminal plasma preservation, despite boar data [5]
- Limited exploration of spermidine's synergistic effects with other phytohormones like brassinolide under stress, as seen in maize [19] but not generalized
- Absence of dose-finding studies for oral spermidine supplementation to induce autophagy in humans, bridging in vitro [4] and animal [8] evidence

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- The boar sperm model [1,5,6,7] may not extrapolate to humans due to species-specific polyamine metabolism and OCT expression differences
- Autophagy blockade in [4] uses 3-MA, which has off-target effects on other pathways, questioning the specificity of spermidine's mechanism
- Agronomic studies [3,9,12] vary in spermidine doses (0.1 μM to 5 mM) and application timing, complicating meta-analysis and reducing generalizability
- Food content studies [20,21] lack controlled intake trials, making it difficult to link dietary spermidine to health outcomes
