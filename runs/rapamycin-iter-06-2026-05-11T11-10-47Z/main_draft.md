# Systematic Synthesis of Rapamycin Effects on Lifespan Across Species and Treatment Parameters

## Abstract

To what extent does rapamycin consistently extend lifespan across model organisms, and how do effect magnitudes vary by sex, genetic background, dose, and age at treatment initiation? Prior narrative reviews and quantitative syntheses have documented lifespan extension in rodent models [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin], yet heterogeneity across studies and the moderating roles of key experimental parameters remain incompletely characterized [CIT:swindell-2017-meta]. Existing meta-analytic work has focused predominantly on mice [CIT:bitto-2016-itp] and has not systematically incorporated emerging data from non-rodent species or recent dosing-regimen comparisons. A comprehensive assessment of effect-size heterogeneity and its sources is needed to guide translational decision-making.

This project will screen [N_SCREENED] records identified through systematic database searches, applying predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria to yield [N_ACCEPTED] independent effect estimates drawn from [K_STUDIES] published studies. The corpus will span mouse, rat, Drosophila, C. elegans, and any additional species meeting inclusion thresholds. Mixed-effects meta-analytic models will be specified to estimate pooled effects while partitioning between-study variance by prespecified moderator categories. A tension matrix will map the consistency of lifespan effects across the intersection of sex, strain, dose, and treatment-onset timing, identifying cells of convergence and divergence.

Four boundary conditions are interrogated: (1) does effect magnitude differ between males and females [MODERATOR_P:sex]? (2) do inbred and outbred genetic backgrounds produce systematically different responses [MODERATOR_P:strain]? (3) is there a dose–response relationship across the tested range [MODERATOR_P:dose]? (4) does early-onset versus late-onset treatment yield differential effects [MODERATOR_P:timing]? Each question is pre-registered and will be tested irrespective of aggregate directionality.

The planned analysis will identify parameters under which existing evidence is insufficient for inference. These gaps will be formalized as specific experimental recommendations—particularly for species and sex combinations currently underrepresented—to direct next-study designs that can either corroborate or refute the synthesized estimates.

## Introduction

Rapamycin, a macrolide compound originally isolated from *Streptomyces hygroscopicus*, inhibits the mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1) and has become one of the most widely studied pharmacological interventions in the biology of aging [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor]. Interest intensified after the Interventions Testing Program (ITP) demonstrated that rapamycin administered late in life extended median and maximal lifespan in genetically heterogeneous mice [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin]. Since that landmark result, dozens of follow-up studies have examined rapamycin across multiple species, genetic backgrounds, sexes, doses, and treatment-onset windows [CIT:arriola-apelo-2016-review]. The breadth of this experimental landscape has generated a substantial evidence base, yet it has simultaneously created interpretive challenges: effect magnitudes appear inconsistent across conditions, and the determinants of that inconsistency have not been resolved by any single synthesis.

Several quantitative reviews have addressed subsets of this literature. Swindell [CIT:swindell-2017-meta] conducted a meta-analysis of rapamycin lifespan studies in mice, reporting a pooled lifespan extension of approximately [EFFECT=...] [CI=...] across [K_STUDIES=...] experiments. That work identified significant heterogeneity and noted trends toward stronger effects in females and in studies employing lower doses, though statistical power for moderator detection was limited by the number of available studies. Bitto et al. [CIT:bitto-2016-itp] reported ITP results across multiple sites and doses, providing some of the most methodologically rigorous individual data points and highlighting site-by-site reproducibility. Lamming et al. [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor] reviewed the mechanistic rationale linking mTORC1 inhibition to aging pathways, including autophagy, proteostasis, and cellular senescence, establishing a biological framework within which lifespan results can be interpreted. Additional narrative reviews have catalogued effects in *Drosophila melanogaster* [CIT:bjedov-2010-drosophila] and *Caenorhabditis elegans* [CIT:robida-howell-2010-elegans], though these have not been subjected to formal meta-analytic aggregation with standardized effect-size metrics. Collectively, prior work indicates that rapamycin reliably extends lifespan in at least one well-characterized model system [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin, swindell-2017-meta], yet the consistency and boundary conditions of that effect across species, sexes, and experimental designs remain subjects of active debate [CIT:mannick-2018-human].

The translational significance of resolving this heterogeneity is considerable. Rapamycin and rapalog derivatives have entered human clinical trials aimed at immune rejuvenation and healthspan improvement [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus, mannick-2018-human]. If lifespan-extension effects in preclinical models are contingent on narrow parameter combinations—for instance, appearing only in one sex of a single inbred strain at a particular dose—then the evidence base for human translation is weaker than headline results imply. Conversely, if effects prove robust across a broad parameter space, confidence in cross-species generalizability increases. Existing meta-analytic efforts [CIT:swindell-2017-meta] have advanced this question but were constrained by the number of studies available at the time of publication, by a primary focus on murine models, and by limited capacity to test multi-way moderator interactions. The field has since accumulated additional data, including results from non-rodent organisms and from studies directly comparing dosing regimens or treatment-onset timings within a single experimental framework [CIT:bitto-2016-itp, castillo-quan-2019-drosophila].

This synthesis extends prior quantitative reviews in three specific ways. First, it incorporates non-mammalian model organisms—principally *Drosophila* and *C. elegans*—into a unified analytic framework with standardized effect-size metrics, enabling cross-species comparison that prior rodent-focused syntheses did not undertake [CIT:swindell-2017-meta]. Second, it applies a pre-specified moderator structure designed to test two-way and three-way interactions among sex, genetic background, dose, and treatment-onset timing, leveraging the larger pooled corpus to achieve greater statistical power for these tests than was available in earlier work [CIT:arriola-apelo-2016-review]. Third, it constructs a tension matrix—an intersectional grid of moderator combinations—that maps effect consistency at the level of specific parameter cells, thereby identifying not only which moderators matter individually but also which combinations produce convergent or divergent outcomes. This approach allows the synthesis to move beyond omnibus heterogeneity statistics toward actionable characterization of the parameter space.

The scope of this work is defined by explicit boundaries and non-goals. The analysis addresses lifespan as the primary outcome; healthspan, disease-specific endpoints, and mechanistic biomarkers are excluded unless they are reported as co-primary outcomes alongside lifespan in included studies. Only studies employing rapamycin per se or its close structural analogs (e.g., everolimus, temsirolimus) at doses explicitly calibrated to mTORC1 inhibition are included; dietary or genetic mTOR manipulations (e.g., *S6K1* knockout, dietary restriction models) are outside the present scope, though they bear mechanistic relevance [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor]. The synthesis does not aim to adjudicate between competing mechanistic hypotheses—autophagy induction, senolytic activity, immune modulation, or translation control—as primary drivers of lifespan extension; such adjudication would require integration of molecular endpoint data beyond the scope of the present corpus. Furthermore, this work does not constitute a risk–benefit assessment for clinical use of rapamycin in humans; the evidence streams, outcome definitions, and dose–toxicity profiles relevant to clinical translation differ substantively from those characterizing preclinical lifespan studies [CIT:mannick-2018-human]. Rather, the goal is to provide the most comprehensive quantitative characterization to date of how rapamycin's lifespan effects vary across the design parameters that preclinical researchers and translational planners can actually control. By specifying moderator tests and tension-matrix cells in advance, the analysis also produces a formal evidence-gap map that can guide the design of future experiments to fill cells where current data are sparse or equivocal [CIT:swindell-2017-meta, bitto-2016-itp].
