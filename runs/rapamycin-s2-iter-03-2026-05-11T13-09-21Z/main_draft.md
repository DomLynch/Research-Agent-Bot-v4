## Methods

The systematic review and meta-analysis protocol is registered and will be reported according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) statement [CIT:page-2020-prisma|method-citation]. A comprehensive electronic search strategy is designed to query [PLACEHOLDER:databases] from [PLACEHOLDER:date-range]. The search syntax will combine terms for the intervention (rapamycin, sirolimus, and their synonyms) with terms for the model (murine, mouse, mice) and the outcome (lifespan, longevity, survival), using controlled vocabulary and free-text fields [PLACEHOLDER:query-terms]. All searches will be documented in a full strategy appendix.

Eligibility criteria follow the PECO framework for preclinical animal studies. The population (P) is restricted to mice (*Mus musculus*). The intervention/exposure (E) in the primary pooled corpus includes only rapamycin (also known as sirolimus) administered as a lifespan-extending intervention. The comparator (C) is any control condition (vehicle, untreated, or standard diet). The primary outcome (O) is the measure of lifespan, reported as mean lifespan, median lifespan, survival curve data, or hazard ratios. Studies using translational-only mTOR inhibitors such as everolimus, RTB101, or other rapalogs are excluded from the primary pooled analysis but are pre-specified for inclusion in a separate translational evidence synthesis layer. Only primary research studies using in vivo murine models are eligible; in vitro or purely computational studies are excluded.

Data extraction will be performed independently by two reviewers using a pre-piloted form. Disagreements will be resolved by consensus or consultation with a third reviewer. Extracted variables include study identifiers, murine model characteristics (strain, genetic background, sex), intervention details (dose [mg/kg], route, frequency, age at treatment start, duration), control characteristics, and outcome data. Lifespan data will be extracted in their original reported units (e.g., days, weeks). Where means and standard deviations are not directly reported, they will be estimated from medians, ranges, or survival curves using validated methods, with the conversion assumptions documented and subjected to sensitivity analysis. For studies reporting multiple lifespan-related outcomes, the primary effect measure is pre-specified as the ratio of means (treatment vs. control) for mean lifespan.

Moderator coding is pre-specified to investigate sources of heterogeneity. The moderators are: [MODERATOR_P:strain] (e.g., C57BL/6, other inbred, hybrid), [MODERATOR_P:sex] (male, female, mixed), [MODERATOR_P:dose] (continuous in original mg/kg units, with post-hoc categorization for visual displays only), [MODERATOR_P:age_at_start] (continuous in weeks), and [MODERATOR_P:treatment_duration] (continuous in weeks or as a proportion of control lifespan). Each moderator is chosen based on biological plausibility and prior narrative reviews [CIT:arriola-apelo-2016-review|narrative-review].

Risk-of-bias assessment for the included animal intervention studies will be conducted using the SYRCLE risk of bias tool, which is designed for in vivo animal experiments [CIT:hooijmans-2014-syrcle|method-citation]. Each study will be assessed across domains including selection, performance, detection, attrition, and reporting. Two reviewers will independently assess each domain; a third reviewer will arbitrate discordant ratings.

Statistical synthesis will employ a multi-level mixed-effects meta-regression model to account for the nested structure of the data (e.g., multiple experiments within a publication). The random-effects structure will include a random intercept for study-ID to account for between-study heterogeneity. The fixed-effects predictors will be the pre-specified moderators (strain, sex, dose, age at start, treatment duration). The model will be estimated using a restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimator with the Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman adjustment for confidence intervals, which provides more reliable inference under heterogeneity with a small number of studies [CIT:hartung-knapp|method-citation]. Degrees of freedom will be calculated using the Kenward-Roger approximation. All analyses will be conducted in R using the `metafor` package [CIT:viechtbauer-2010-metafor|method-citation].

Sensitivity analyses will include leave-one-out analysis to assess the influence of individual studies, and diagnostic measures such as Cook's distance and DFFITS to identify influential outliers. Publication bias will be evaluated visually using funnel plots and statistically using Egger's regression test for asymmetry [CIT:egger-1997-funnel|method-citation]. Heterogeneity will be quantified with the I² statistic [CIT:higgins-2003-i2|method-citation]. A separate translational sensitivity analysis will map the evidence for everolimus and RTB101 on lifespan or relevant healthspan metrics in mice without pooling it with the primary rapamycin/sirolimus corpus, providing a narrative summary of effect directions and study characteristics.

A tension-matrix construction will be used to synthesize moderator interactions. The matrix will be defined by the categorical levels of two key moderators (e.g., strain and sex), with each cell representing a unique combination. The pooled effect estimate and prediction interval for each cell will be computed from the meta-regression model. Cells with fewer than three contributing studies will be flagged as sparse, indicating low confidence in the estimate. This matrix is designed to visually identify interactions where treatment effects diverge, guiding interpretation of generalizability and informing recommendations for future study designs to test specific hypotheses.
