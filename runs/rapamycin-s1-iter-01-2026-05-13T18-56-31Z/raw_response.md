=== TITLE ===
Scoping Review of Rapamycin Effects on Murine Lifespan Across Dose and Timing Regimens

=== ABSTRACT ===
To what extent does the effect of rapamycin on murine lifespan vary across different dosing schedules, concentrations, and initiation ages? Prior syntheses have identified general lifespan extension [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis], yet uncertainty remains regarding the optimal timing of intervention and the interaction between dose and strain [CIT:tyshkovskiy-2025-vertebrate-meta|prior-meta-analysis]. This scoping review is implemented through a reproducible evidence-contract pipeline to map the existing evidence landscape.

The pipeline will perform a multi-source retrieval sweep, screening [N_SCREENED] records to identify [N_ACCEPTED] eligible studies across [K_STUDIES] unique murine cohorts. Eligibility is adjudicated by a single LLM judge against a deterministic rule-triage pre-pass and a universal evidence-contract gate requiring parsed-text adequacy and endpoint coverage.

The planned analysis will utilize a narrative synthesis to map the distribution of effects, as the current pipeline design defers quantitative pooling until a sufficient parseable pool of effects is established. The review is designed to interrogate whether the magnitude of lifespan extension is modified by sex, mouse strain, dose, or the age at which treatment commences. Specifically, the analysis will examine if late-life initiation yields comparable benefits to early-life intervention and how dose-response curves differ across genetic backgrounds.

The resulting map of evidence is designed to identify specific gaps in the murine literature, particularly regarding the intersection of dose and timing. Future studies employing standardized, multi-arm dose-escalation protocols across diverse mouse strains would strengthen the findings by providing the high-resolution data necessary to transition from a scoping narrative to a formal quantitative synthesis.

=== INTRODUCTION ===
The modulation of the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway represents a primary axis for the pharmacological extension of murine lifespan [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor|mechanism-review] [CIT:saxton-2017-mtor|mechanism-review]. Rapamycin, a potent inhibitor of mTOR complex 1 (mTORC1), has demonstrated the capacity to increase lifespan in mice across various genetic backgrounds [CIT:strong-2008-itp|primary-study] [CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin|primary-study]. The significance of this research question lies in the high degree of variability observed in the magnitude of these effects, which suggests that the efficacy of rapamycin is contingent upon specific biological and temporal parameters [CIT:bitto-2016-rapamycin|primary-study] [CIT:miller-2011-rapamycin|primary-study]. Understanding these parameters is essential for refining the administration of mTOR inhibitors to maximize longevity while minimizing potential adverse effects [CIT:arriola-apelo-2016-review|narrative-review].

The landscape of prior syntheses has established a foundation for understanding rapamycin, yet specific gaps persist. For instance, Fok et al. (2023) provided a meta-analysis that quantified the general effect of rapamycin on murine lifespan [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis], while Swindell et al. (2017) explored the consistency of these effects across different study designs [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis]. More recently, Tyshkovskiy et al. (2025) expanded the scope to include a broader range of vertebrate models, providing a high-level overview of mTOR inhibition [CIT:tyshkovskiy-2025-vertebrate-meta|prior-meta-analysis]. Despite these contributions, prior syntheses have not provided a fully powered, pre-specified joint moderator framework that simultaneously evaluates the interaction between dose, timing of initiation, and mouse strain. Most existing reviews treat these variables as independent moderators or provide narrative summaries that do not systematically map the evidence-contract adequacy of the underlying primary studies [CIT:arriola-apelo-2016-review|narrative-review].

The present work extends prior syntheses by implementing a strict evidence-contract pipeline designed to stress-test the reliability of reported lifespan extensions. Unlike previous reviews that may rely on manual extraction of summary statistics, this work utilizes an automated pipeline to audit the raw evidence quotes within each paper, ensuring that the reported endpoints and interventions are explicitly affirmed in the text. This approach allows for a more rigorous mapping of the evidence landscape, specifically regarding the "translational-only" interventions such as everolimus or various rapalogs [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus|clinical-trial] [CIT:mannick-2018-rtb101|clinical-trial], which are often conflated with rapamycin in broader reviews but may possess distinct pharmacokinetic profiles. By isolating rapamycin and sirolimus as the primary interventions, this scoping review refines the focus to the most potent and well-studied mTORC1 inhibitors in mice.

A critical gap in the current literature is the lack of a systematic map regarding the timing of intervention. While it is known that rapamycin can extend lifespan when started in adulthood [CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin|primary-study], the relative efficacy of early-life versus late-life initiation remains under-explored in a synthesized format. Furthermore, the interaction between dose and strain is often reported anecdotally; for example, some strains may exhibit higher sensitivity to low-dose rapamycin than others [CIT:bitto-2016-rapamycin|primary-study]. The present work is designed to map these interactions, providing a structured overview of how the "dose-timing-strain" triad influences the primary endpoint of lifespan. This systematic mapping is a necessary precursor to any future quantitative synthesis that seeks to estimate a universal dose-response curve for mTOR inhibition in mice.

The scope of this review is strictly limited to murine models, specifically focusing on mouse lifespan. To maintain scope consistency, the analysis excludes other rodent species, such as rats, to avoid the confounding effects of species-specific mTOR regulation. The primary interventions eligible for the core analysis are rapamycin and sirolimus. While translational analogs like everolimus, RTB101, and other rapalogs are recognized in the literature [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus|clinical-trial] [CIT:mannick-2018-rtb101|clinical-trial], they are excluded from the primary pooled analysis and retained only for a translational sensitivity layer or discussion. This boundary is essential because the structural modifications in rapalogs can alter their affinity for FKBP12 and their subsequent inhibition of mTORC2, potentially leading to different lifespan outcomes compared to rapamycin [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor|mechanism-review].

Explicit non-goals of this study include the performance of a formal meta-analysis or the quantification of heterogeneity, as the current iteration is a scoping review designed to map the evidence rather than pool effects. The pipeline is designed for quantitative synthesis, but such capabilities are deferred until the corpus accrues a sufficient number of contract-passing effects per metric family. Additionally, this work does not perform per-domain expert risk-of-bias adjudication using specific frameworks; instead, it focuses on the adequacy of the evidence quotes and the adherence to reporting standards such as the ARRIVE guidelines [CIT:percie-du-sert-2020-arrive|method-citation]. By focusing on the mapping of the evidence landscape, this work provides a transparent audit of what is known and, more importantly, what remains unknown about the temporal and dosage requirements for rapamycin-induced lifespan extension in mice.
