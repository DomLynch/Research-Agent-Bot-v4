# Frontier review — rapamycin

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-15T19-07-34Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The divergent efficacy of rapamycin across genetic backgrounds—60% lifespan extension with transient high-dose (8 mg/kg/day i.p.) in C57BL/6 mice versus 14-26% effects with chronic lower doses in UM-HET3 mice—suggests that regimen optimization, not just mTOR inhibition, is a critical yet underexplored lever for anti-aging translation, potentially mediated by strain-specific metabolic or immune adaptations.

## Already known — do not publish

- Rapamycin is an mTOR inhibitor that extends lifespan in mice
- Rapamycin improves immune responses in the elderly
- mTOR pathway modulation is a conserved aging mechanism

## Tensions / contradictions

- Fact 1 (C57BL/6, 8 mg/kg/day i.p. for 3 months, 60% lifespan extension) vs. Fact 3 (UM-HET3, 14 ppm in feed chronic, 14% mortality reduction): conflicting outcomes due to strain, route, and dosing regimen
- Facts 5 and 6 (UM-HET3, 42 ppm, sex-specific 23% male vs. 26% female lifespan extension) indicate sex-dependent efficacy, but Fact 3 (UM-HET3, 14 ppm) shows female-focused effects without male data at lower doses
- Fact 4 (RAD001/everolimus in humans, 20% vaccine response improvement) uses a derivative, not rapamycin, raising translational validity for lifespan effects

## Evidence gaps

- No direct comparative studies of transient vs. chronic rapamycin dosing in the same mouse strain (e.g., C57BL/6 or UM-HET3)
- Lack of dose-response data in UM-HET3 mice between 14 ppm and 42 ppm to identify optimal anti-aging thresholds
- Human trials focusing solely on immune function (RAD001) without lifespan endpoints or sex-stratified analyses for rapamycin itself
- Underexploration of rapamycin's effects in aged or disease-prone subpopulations beyond standard mouse models

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- Heterogeneity in mouse strains (C57BL/6 vs. UM-HET3) and dosing routes (i.p. vs. feed) confounds direct comparisons, limiting meta-analytic reliability.
- Human evidence relies on RAD001 (everolimus), not rapamycin, and measures only immune function, not lifespan, undermining translational claims.
- The superseded fact (2) indicates methodological disputes, raising concerns about data quality in the evidence pool.

## Suggested next extractions

- Rapamycin doses in the 1-10 mg/kg/day range across different mouse strains (e.g., BALB/c, transgenic models) for lifespan studies
- Human clinical trials with rapamycin focusing on aging biomarkers, frailty, or mortality endpoints, especially those with sex-specific analyses
- Data on side effects and safety profiles of transient rapamycin interventions in mice and humans, including immunosuppression risks
