=== DISCUSSION ===

This scoping review evaluated the available evidence on acarbose and alpha-glucosidase inhibitors as lifespan-modifying interventions in mouse models. The systematic retrieval and automated adjudication pipeline identified [PACKET:primary_effect] effect estimates that passed the universal evidence-contract gate; [PACKET:primary_pool_composition] reports the number of studies meeting contract criteria for the primary analysis corpus. Because the pool of contract-passing effects is insufficient for quantitative synthesis, no pooled hazard ratio or percent-extension estimate is presented. The present contribution instead maps the evidence landscape, catalogues the experimental conditions under which acarbose has been tested in murine longevity paradigms, and identifies the boundary conditions and data gaps that currently preclude definitive quantitative inference about lifespan modification in this system.

Prior syntheses have incorporated acarbose findings within broader systematic evaluations of pharmacological and dietary interventions tested in murine aging models [CIT:heart-2016-6924|narrative-review]. Those reviews report acarbose alongside dozens of candidate geroprotectors, noting that alpha-glucosidase inhibitors have produced lifespan extension in at least one sex of specific mouse strains, though effects varied across sites and dosing protocols. [CIT:considerations-2019-2660|narrative-review] addresses methodological considerations that influence the interpretation of rodent aging studies, including strain background, housing conditions, and the sensitivity of lifespan endpoints to environmental variables. The present review aligns with these prior syntheses in recognizing acarbose as a candidate geroprotector while providing a focused evaluation restricted to the alpha-glucosidase inhibitor mechanistic class. When the number of contract-passing effects within a single metric family reaches k ≥ 2, the pipeline is designed to estimate pooled effect sizes and to partition variance by predefined moderator strata; until that threshold is met, alignment and divergence between the present corpus and prior syntheses remain descriptive rather than quantitative.

The primary mechanistic pathway through which acarbose may influence lifespan involves inhibition of alpha-glucosidase enzymes in the brush-border membrane of the small intestine, which delays carbohydrate digestion and glucose absorption, thereby attenuating postprandial glycemic and insulinemic excursions [CIT:cap-2021-3345|mechanism-review].
