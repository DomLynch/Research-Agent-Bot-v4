# Frontier review — NAD

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-15T05-58-27Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The data expose a paradoxical age-inversion in NAD+ precursor efficacy—where short-term NR shows no metabolic effect in young men but modulates neuroinflammatory markers in older adults—while simultaneously positioning NAD+ as a severity-dependent biomarker in alcoholic liver disease, bridging metabolic, inflammatory, and neurodegenerative pathways in a context-specific manner.

## Already known — do not publish

- Oral NR supplementation increases NAD+ levels in NEVs and decreases Aβ42, pJNK, and pERK1/2 in healthy older adults (Fact 3)
- NAD+ concentration is depressed in ArLD liver tissue compared to normal (Fact 2)
- Hippocampal NAD+ levels decrease after 3 months of STZ-induced diabetes in rats (Fact 19)

## Tensions / contradictions

- Fact 9 (1-week NR, no metabolic change in young men, The Journal of Physiology, 2021) vs. Fact 3 (NR modulates insulin and neuroinflammatory pathways in older adults, Aging Cell, 2022) highlights an age-dependent efficacy gap
- Fact 14 and 15 (NAD+ concentration differs with steatohepatitis severity, Hepatology Communications, 2020) vs. Fact 10 and 11 (NAD+ correlates with myeloperoxidase and bilirubin) suggest conflicting roles in inflammation vs. disease progression
- Fact 22 (NAMPT protein decreases with NR in obese men, The Journal of Physiology, 2019) vs. Fact 3 (NR increases NAD+ in older adults) implies potential negative feedback mechanisms in metabolic syndrome

## Evidence gaps

- Dose-response relationships for NR/NMN supplementation across age and sex subgroups, beyond fixed doses like 500 mg twice daily
- NAD+ dynamics in liver disease beyond ArLD, such as non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, with longitudinal tracking
- Sex-specific mechanistic studies linking plasma NAD+/NADH ratios (Fact 1 and 6) to tissue-level outcomes in humans

## Paper theses

### #1 — opportunity 88 · `meta-analysis-standard`

**Thesis:** Age-Inverted Efficacy of NAD+ Precursors: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Supplementation Trials in Young vs. Older Adults

- novelty 75 / evidence_strength 65 / reviewer_risk 55
- **Why publishable:** Conflicting outcomes between short-term NR studies in young men (e.g., Fact 9) and benefits in older adults (e.g., Fact 3) provide a clear publishable angle to resolve through quantitative synthesis, informing personalized aging interventions.


## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- Heterogeneity in intervention protocols (e.g., NR doses from 1000 mg/day to 500 mg twice daily) and outcome measures (metabolomic vs. functional) across studies limits comparability
- Overreliance on rodent models (e.g., Fact 4, 19) for human-relevant conclusions, especially for hippocampal NAD+ effects
- Small sample sizes in key trials (e.g., n=8 in Fact 9) reduce statistical power and generalizability

## Suggested next extractions

- Harvest data on NR/NMN supplementation in middle-aged adults (40-60 years) to bridge the young-old divide
- Extract studies measuring NAD+ levels in paired liver tissue and plasma from patients with varying steatosis grades
- Investigate sex-stratified responses to NAD+ precursors in clinical trials, building on Fact 1 and 6
