# Scoping Review of Rapamycin Effects on Lifespan in Murine Models

## Abstract

To what extent does the effect of rapamycin on lifespan vary across different mouse strains, sexes, and dosing regimens? While prior syntheses have quantified general lifespan extensions [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis], uncertainty remains regarding the interaction between dosing timing and genetic background, and the degree to which these effects are consistent across diverse murine populations. This scoping review is implemented through a reproducible evidence-contract pipeline. The pipeline will perform a multi-source retrieval sweep, with eligibility adjudicated by a single LLM judge against a deterministic rule-triage pre-pass, resulting in [N_SCREENED] records screened and [N_ACCEPTED] records accepted across [K_STUDIES] studies.

The planned analysis employs a narrative synthesis of extracted effects, as the current pipeline design defers quantitative pooling until a sufficient number of contract-passing effects per metric family are accrued. The analysis is designed to map the distribution of lifespan outcomes and partition variance by moderator strata. Specifically, the review will interrogate whether the magnitude of lifespan extension is modified by sex, strain, or the age of treatment initiation. The design also specifies a translational sensitivity layer to distinguish the effects of rapamycin from those of other rapalogs. The resulting synthesis is designed to identify specific gaps in the current murine evidence base, which would inform the design of future longitudinal studies to determine if the observed lifespan extensions are robust across all common laboratory mouse backgrounds or limited to specific genetic clusters.

## Introduction

The modulation of the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway represents a primary axis for the pharmacological extension of lifespan in murine models [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor|mechanism-review] [CIT:saxton-2017-mtor|mechanism-review]. Rapamycin, a potent inhibitor of mTORC1, has demonstrated the capacity to increase lifespan in mice when administered at various ages and doses [CIT:strong-2008-itp|primary-study] [CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin|primary-study]. The significance of this research question lies in the potential for rapamycin to serve as a blueprint for geroscience interventions; however, the variability in response across different murine cohorts suggests that the efficacy of the drug may be contingent upon baseline genetic and physiological states [CIT:bitto-2016-rapamycin|primary-study] [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin|primary-study]. Understanding the precise boundary conditions under which rapamycin extends lifespan is essential for translating these findings from controlled laboratory environments to broader biological contexts.

The landscape of prior syntheses has established a foundation for understanding these effects, yet specific gaps persist. For instance, the meta-analysis by Swindell et al. (2017) [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis] provided an early quantitative aggregation of rapamycin's impact on lifespan, while Fok et al. (2023) [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis] further refined the estimate of lifespan extension across various mouse models. More recently, Tyshkovskiy et al. (2025) [CIT:tyshkovskiy-2025-vertebrate-meta|prior-meta-analysis] expanded the scope to include a broader range of vertebrate species, providing a high-level view of mTOR inhibition. Despite these contributions, prior syntheses have not provided a fully powered, pre-specified joint moderator framework that simultaneously accounts for the interaction of sex, strain, and dosing timing within the murine subset. Many existing reviews rely on narrative summaries of a few high-profile studies [CIT:arriola-apelo-2016-review|narrative-review], which may overlook smaller studies or those with null results, potentially introducing publication bias into the perceived efficacy of the intervention.

The present work is designed to extend and stress-test these prior syntheses by applying a strict evidence-contract pipeline to the available murine literature. Rather than simply aggregating effect sizes, this scoping review focuses on the mapping of evidence adequacy and the identification of systematic gaps in the reporting of lifespan data. By utilizing a deterministic rule-triage and a universal evidence-contract gate, this analysis aims to refine the corpus of "contract-passing" studies, ensuring that only records with sufficient parsed-text adequacy and explicit endpoint coverage are included [CIT:page-2020-prisma|method-citation]. This approach allows for a more rigorous assessment of whether the reported lifespan extensions are consistent across different mouse strains or if the effects are driven by a small number of highly responsive genetic backgrounds [CIT:miller-2011-rapamycin|primary-study]. Furthermore, by explicitly separating rapamycin from other rapalogs such as everolimus or RTB101, this work provides a cleaner signal of the primary intervention's effect, whereas prior reviews have occasionally conflated these agents in their broader discussions of mTOR inhibition [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus|clinical-trial] [CIT:mannick-2018-rtb101|clinical-trial].

The scope of this review is strictly limited to the effect of rapamycin and sirolimus on the lifespan of mice. To maintain scope consistency, the analysis excludes other mammalian or vertebrate species, focusing exclusively on murine models to minimize inter-species noise. A critical non-goal of this study is the quantification of heterogeneity or the pooling of effects; because the current iteration is a scoping review, the pipeline defers quantitative synthesis—such as inverse-variance random-effects pooling—until the corpus accrues a sufficient number of contract-passing effects per metric family. Additionally, this work does not perform a formal risk-of-bias adjudication using a specific framework, as such expert-level adjudication is deferred in the current pipeline iteration. The analysis will not attempt to model the effects of rapalogs like everolimus within the primary pooled analysis, treating them instead as a translational sensitivity layer to be discussed qualitatively.

By focusing on the intersection of dose, timing, and strain, this review is designed to identify whether the "lifespan extension" narrative is monolithic or fragmented. For example, the timing of intervention—whether rapamycin is administered in adulthood or late-life—may fundamentally alter the lifespan trajectory [CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin|primary-study]. Prior work has suggested that the mTOR pathway's role in aging is context-dependent [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor|mechanism-review], yet the specific interaction between the age of onset and the genetic strain of the mouse remains under-explored in a systematic fashion. This review will therefore map these variables to determine if certain strains are more resilient or susceptible to the lifespan-extending effects of rapamycin. Through this systematic mapping, the work aims to provide a more nuanced understanding of the pharmacological landscape of mTOR inhibition in mice, moving beyond simple average effect sizes to a more granular understanding of biological variance.
