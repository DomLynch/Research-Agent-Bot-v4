## The lens

The paradoxical associations of telomere length with cancer risk—where short telomeres drastically elevate breast cancer risk in case-control studies (OR 15.5) but only marginally in prospective cohorts (OR 1.58), while long telomeres both increase breast cancer risk and protect against melanoma—expose how study design and biological context can invert biomarker interpretations, challenging simplistic telomere-centric models of disease.

## Already known — do not publish

- Telomere length is a biomarker of biological aging
- Lifestyle factors like exercise and diet influence telomere length
- Genetic heritability contributes significantly to telomere length variation

## Tensions / contradictions

- Contradictory odds ratios for breast cancer: shortest telomere quartile shows OR 15.5 in SEARCH case-control (fact 6907) versus OR 1.58 in EPIC-Norfolk prospective study (fact 6908)
- Directional inconsistency: short telomeres increase melanoma risk (OR 0.43 protective in fact 46707) but longer telomeres are associated with increased overall cancer risk (OR 1.086 in fact 3475)
- Folate deficiency elongates telomeres in vitro (26% increase in fact 20102), yet its role in human health remains unclear compared to interventions like psychosocial counseling that increase telomere length in cancer survivors (fact 21712)

## Evidence gaps

- Dose-response relationships for telomere length changes in endurance athletes across varying exercise intensities
- Telomere length dynamics in early-life stress across species, linking avian models (fact 4351) to human childhood interventions
- Mediation analyses of telomere length in genetic risk pathways for cancers beyond bladder cancer, such as breast or melanoma, given the 80% heritability (fact 13457)
