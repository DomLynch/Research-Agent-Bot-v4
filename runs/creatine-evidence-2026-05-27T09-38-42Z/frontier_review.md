# Frontier review — creatine

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-27T09-38-42Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The deuterated-creatine dilution method, a key tool for quantifying whole-body creatine kinetics, shows non-trivial errors in tracer distribution (2-10%) and retention (0-9%), challenging its precision in clinical and research settings.

## Already known — do not publish

- Creatine supplementation enhances athletic performance and muscle mass
- Long-term creatine use is generally safe and well-tolerated

## Tensions / contradictions

- The variability in non-muscle creatine distribution (2-10%) versus urinary isotope losses (0-9%) suggests methodological inconsistencies that could skew creatine store estimates
- Limited data from a single methodological study (2022) raises questions about generalizability across different populations

## Evidence gaps

- Lack of investigation into how distribution and retention losses vary with age, sex, muscle mass, or health conditions like sarcopenia
- No comparative analysis of deuterated-creatine dilution against other methods (e.g., MRI) in diverse human subgroups

## Paper theses

### #1 — opportunity 22 · `evidence-gap`

**Thesis:** Reassessing the Deuterated-Creatine Dilution Method: Addressing Tracer Variability for Accurate Whole-Body Kinetics

- novelty 75 / evidence_strength 25 / reviewer_risk 85
- **Why publishable:** This thesis is publishable as it highlights a critical methodological gap in creatine research, where current measurement inaccuracies could invalidate findings on supplementation efficacy or metabolic disorders, warranting a focused review or pilot study.


## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- The evidence is too narrow, relying solely on two facts from a single 2022 study, which may not capture the full scope of methodological issues or real-world applications
- Without data from multiple studies or validation against gold-standard methods, the thesis risks being speculative rather than empirically grounded

## Suggested next extractions

- Harvest deuterated-creatine dilution studies in older adults, post-menopausal females, or patients with muscle wasting diseases to quantify distribution/retention variability
- Extract data comparing deuterated-creatine dilution with alternative creatine measurement techniques (e.g., biopsies or spectroscopy) across different doses and supplementation protocols
