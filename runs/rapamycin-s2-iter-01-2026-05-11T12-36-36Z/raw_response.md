=== METHODS ===

A systematic literature search will be conducted across [PLACEHOLDER:databases], including at minimum PubMed, Web of Science, Scopus, and Embase, following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 guidelines [CIT:page-2020-prisma|method-citation]. The search will cover publications from [PLACEHOLDER:date-range] to capture the full span of rapamycin lifespan studies in murine models. Search strings will combine controlled vocabulary and free-text terms for the intervention (rapamycin, sirolimus, mTOR inhibitor) and outcome (lifespan, longevity, survival, mortality) paired with species filters restricting to mouse and rodent studies [PLACEHOLDER:query-terms]. The exact Boolean query architecture will be documented in a supplementary protocol. Searches will be rerun within 30 days of final analysis to capture late-breaking records. All retrieved records will be imported into a reference manager for deduplication prior to screening.

Studies will be eligible if they meet pre-specified Population–Intervention–Comparator–Outcome criteria. The population comprises mouse or other rodent strains receiving rapamycin (or its derivative everolimus) as a lifespan intervention administered via any route, dose, or schedule. Comparators include vehicle-treated, ad libitum-fed, or genetically matched control groups. The primary outcome is median or maximum lifespan reported as a survival metric amenable to effect-size extraction. Both randomized and non-randomized experimental designs will be included. Studies will be excluded if they report only surrogate endpoints without any survival data, if the intervention confounds rapamycin with another compound in the same arm, or if the publication exists only as an abstract without sufficient data for extraction. Prior meta-analyses of murine mTOR-pathway interventions have applied comparable scope boundaries [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis]; the present synthesis extends these by incorporating more recent records and an expanded moderator set. [PLACEHOLDER:protocol-registration]

Data will be extracted independently by two reviewers using a standardized electronic form piloted on a random 10% subset of included studies. Extracted variables encompass study identifiers, mouse strain (including genetic background and transgenic status), biological sex, rapamycin dose (mg/kg or mg/kg/day equivalent), route of administration (dietary, gavage, injection), treatment initiation age, treatment duration, cohort size at entry, median lifespan, maximum lifespan, survival-curve digitization coordinates, and hazard ratios where reported. Disagreements between extractors will be resolved through discussion; if consensus is not reached, a third senior reviewer will arbitrate. Where survival data are presented only in graphical form, digitization software will be used and the extracted values will be cross-validated by a second extractor [PLACEHOLDER:digitization-protocol]. Authors of primary studies will be contacted when critical data are missing or ambiguous.

The following moderators are pre-specified on the basis of biological plausibility identified in mechanism reviews of the mTOR pathway [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor|mechanism-review] [CIT:saxton-2017-mtor|mechanism-review] and empirical variation across existing primary studies [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin|primary-study] [CIT:strong-2008-itp|primary-study]. [MODERATOR_P:strain] — mouse genetic background (levels: C57BL/6, other inbred, hybrid, genetically heterogeneous, transgenic). Rationale: strain background modulates baseline mTOR signaling activity and drug metabolism. [MODERATOR_P:sex] — biological sex of the cohort (levels: male, female, mixed). Rationale: sex-dependent differences in lifespan response to mTOR inhibition have been observed [CIT:miller-2011-rapamycin|primary-study]. [MODERATOR_P:dose] — rapamycin dose category (levels: low ≤4 mg/kg, intermediate >4–8 mg/kg, high >8 mg/kg, dose-escalation). Rationale: dose–response relationships are central to therapeutic translation. [MODERATOR_P:route] — route of administration (levels: dietary, oral gavage, intraperitoneal injection). Rationale: pharmacokinetic profiles differ across routes. [MODERATOR_P:initiation-age] — age at treatment start (levels: young <12 months, middle 12–18 months, old >18 months). Rationale: intervention timing may determine effect magnitude. [MODERATOR_P:treatment-duration] — duration of treatment (levels: short ≤6 months, long >6 months, lifelong). Rationale: duration mediates cumulative drug exposure and potential compensatory adaptation.

Risk of bias will be assessed using the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (RoB 2) [CIT:sterne-2019-rob2|method-citation] for randomized controlled experiments and the Risk of Bias in Non-randomized Studies of Interventions (ROBINS-I) tool [CIT:sterne-2016-robins-i|method-citation] for non-randomized designs. Each domain — randomization, allocation concealment, blinding, incomplete outcome data, and selective reporting — will be rated as low, some concerns, or high risk. Overall evidence certainty will be evaluated using the GRADE framework [CIT:schunemann-grade|method-citation], which downgrades for risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias. Two reviewers will independently assess each study; discrepancies will be resolved through discussion or, if needed, a third adjudicator. Risk-of-bias ratings will be incorporated into sensitivity analyses by excluding studies rated high risk and by conducting subgroup comparisons across bias strata.

Effect sizes will be computed as log-transformed hazard ratios or median lifespan ratios depending on data availability, following conventions established in prior murine lifespan meta-analyses [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:tyshkovskiy-2025-vertebrate-meta|prior-meta-analysis]. The primary synthesis will employ a multi-level mixed-effects meta-regression fitted via restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation, with random intercepts specified at two levels: study identifier and outcome-within-study (to account for multiple dose groups or sex-specific arms nested within a single publication). Fixed-effect predictors will include the pre-specified moderators. The variance estimator will incorporate the Hartung–Knapp–Sidik–Jonkman adjustment for confidence intervals [CIT:hartung-knapp|method-citation], which provides more conservative coverage in small-study settings. Models will be fitted in R using the metafor package [CIT:viechtbauer-2010-metafor|method-citation]. Degrees-of-freedom adjustments will follow the Kenward–Roger method where applicable. Multivariate Wald tests will evaluate each moderator's omnibus significance, and moderator effect estimates with confidence intervals will be reported in supplementary tables with full model specifications.

A suite of sensitivity analyses will evaluate the robustness of the primary findings. Leave-one-out analysis will sequentially remove each study to assess the influence of individual data points on the pooled estimate. Influence diagnostics, including Cook's distance and DFBETAS at the study level, will flag disproportionately influential records. Publication bias will be assessed visually via funnel plots of effect size against standard error and statistically via Egger's regression test for asymmetry [CIT:egger-1997-funnel|method-citation]. The trim-and-fill algorithm will impute potentially missing studies to estimate the adjusted pooled effect under a publication-bias scenario. Heterogeneity will be quantified using the I² statistic [CIT:higgins-2003-i2|method-citation] and the τ² between-study variance component, with prediction intervals computed to convey the expected range of true effects across study populations. Cumulative meta-analysis ordered by publication year will assess temporal stability of the effect estimate.

A tension matrix will be constructed to visualize how combinations of moderators jointly shape the estimated lifespan effect. Matrix cells are defined by the Cartesian product of two focal moderators — for example, dose × initiation-age or sex × strain — with each cell representing a unique combination level. Cell values will reflect the model-estimated marginal effect derived from the full multi-level meta-regression. Cells populated by fewer than [PLACEHOLDER:min-k] independent effect sizes will be flagged with a visual indicator to communicate inferential fragility. Two-moderator matrices will be generated for each pair among the six pre-specified moderators, yielding [PLACEHOLDER:n-plots] plots in the supplementary materials. These matrices are designed to identify regions of concordance, where multiple moderators converge on a consistent effect, and regions of tension, where moderator combinations produce contradictory estimates. The concordance and tension patterns will feed back into the interpretation of which moderator configurations are best supported and will shape recommendations for future experimental designs that address gaps or contradictions.
