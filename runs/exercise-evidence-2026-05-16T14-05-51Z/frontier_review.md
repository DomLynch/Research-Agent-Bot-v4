# Frontier review — exercise

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-16T14-05-51Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

Exploring the differential modulation of angiogenic and anabolic pathways by exercise across metabolic states and disease contexts, revealing mechanistic targets for precision interventions in high-risk populations such as obese postmenopausal women and cancer survivors.

## Already known — do not publish

- Generic exercise-health improvement narratives
- Broad claims of exercise reducing inflammation or mortality
- Oversimplified exercise-muscle synthesis relationships

## Tensions / contradictions

- Contrast in protein synthetic response to exercise between healthy young men (fact 1-2) and obese rats (fact 14), indicating obesity-induced anabolic resistance.
- Variation in inflammatory marker reductions with exercise alone versus exercise+metformin in cancer survivors (facts 5-8), suggesting drug-exercise interactions.

## Evidence gaps

- Limited data on exercise dose and timing effects on angiogenic factors like VEGF and PEDF in obese postmenopausal women.
- Underexplored synergies between exercise and nutrient interventions (e.g., leucine) in human models of metabolic dysfunction.
- Scarce evidence on exercise adaptations in elderly with comorbidities like hypertension or peripheral artery disease.

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- Translational validity concerns due to species differences in protein synthesis studies (e.g., rats vs. humans).
- Confounding variables in diet+exercise interventions may obscure exercise-specific effects on biomarkers.
- Small sample sizes in cited studies (e.g., n=16 in fact 1) limit generalizability and statistical power.
