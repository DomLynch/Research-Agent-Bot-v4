# Frontier review — rapamycin

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-16T15-18-29Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The evidence reveals that rapamycin's lifespan effects in mice are critically modulated by an interaction between dose regimen, administration duration, and sex, with transient high-dose protocols showing disproportionate efficacy in certain models—a triad not yet synthesized for translational guidance.

## Already known — do not publish

- Rapamycin extends lifespan in mice
- mTOR inhibition promotes longevity

## Tensions / contradictions

- Transient i.p. rapamycin (8 mg/kg/day for 3 months) in C57BL/6 mice yields ~60% lifespan extension (fact 1) versus chronic low-dose feed (14 ppm) in UM-HET3 mice reducing 90th-percentile mortality by only 14% (fact 3), highlighting regimen and strain variability.
- Male mice show 52% lifespan extension from transient rapamycin (fact 2) but only 23% from chronic high-dose feed (fact 4), suggesting dose or administration differences.
- Female responses vary from 14% mortality reduction with low-dose feed (fact 3) to 26% lifespan extension with high-dose feed (fact 5), indicating sex-specific dose thresholds.

## Evidence gaps

- No studies directly compare transient vs. chronic rapamycin in the same mouse strain and sex to isolate regimen effects.
- Dose-response relationships for rapamycin lifespan effects are not systematically characterized across genetically distinct mouse models (e.g., C57BL/6 vs. UM-HET3).
- Effects of rapamycin on lifespan in aged-outbred or diversity-outbred mice, which better mimic human genetic variability, are underexplored.

## Paper theses

### #1 — opportunity 78 · `evidence-gap`

**Thesis:** The Dose-Regimen-Sex Triad in Rapamycin Lifespan Studies: Identifying Critical Variables for Translational Geroscience

- novelty 85 / evidence_strength 65 / reviewer_risk 70
- **Why publishable:** This thesis synthesizes conflicting lifespan data across rapamycin regimens and sexes to identify key variables (dose, regimen, sex) that must be optimized for human translation, addressing a critical gap in current geroscience literature where such interactions are often overlooked.


## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- Direct comparisons are confounded by differences in mouse strains (C57BL/6 vs. UM-HET3) and administration methods (i.p. injection vs. feed encapsulation), limiting causal inference.
- The evidence pool is too narrow (only 5 studies) for robust meta-analysis, and the thesis may overinterpret associative patterns.
- Lack of mechanistic data on how dose, regimen, and sex interact at the molecular level (e.g., via mTOR pathway modulation) weakens translational claims.

## Suggested next extractions

- Dose-response curves of rapamycin in female C57BL/6 mice to isolate sex effects from strain differences.
- Human clinical trials of rapamycin analogs (e.g., RAD001) for age-related immune decline, as hinted by influenza vaccine response studies in the elderly.
- Comparative analysis of transient vs. chronic rapamycin in UM-HET3 mice with biomarker endpoints (e.g., autophagy, inflammation) to elucidate regimen-specific mechanisms.
