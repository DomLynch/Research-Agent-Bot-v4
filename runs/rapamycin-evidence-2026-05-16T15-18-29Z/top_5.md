## #1 — score 76 · lifespan

- **Value:** 60.0%
- **Population:** middle-aged C57BL/6 mice (20 months at start)
- **Intervention:** transient rapamycin (8 mg/kg/day i.p.) for 3 months
- **Source:** *Transient rapamycin treatment can increase lifespan and healthspan in middle-aged mice* — eLife (2016)
  · DOI: `10.7554/eLife.16351`
- **Validator:** source-cross-check-claude-2026-05-14
- **Same-trial supporting numerics:** 52.0% (3 months of rapamycin extended median lifespan by 52% in mal)

- **Why it matters:** This indicates rapamycin could be a viable late-life intervention for extending human lifespan, as middle-aged mice mimic human aging stages.
- **Caution:** The study used a single inbred mouse strain (C57BL/6), so findings may not apply to genetically diverse populations or humans.
- **Next question:** Does rapamycin administration starting in older mice beyond 20 months yield similar lifespan extensions?

## #3 — score 56 · lifespan

- **Value:** 14.0%
- **Population:** female heterogeneous-stock mice (UM-HET3); 4-site NIA Interventions Testing Program cohort
- **Intervention:** encapsulated rapamycin in feed (~14 ppm); started at 600 days of age
- **Source:** *Rapamycin fed late in life extends lifespan in genetically heterogeneous mice* — Nature (2009)
  · DOI: `10.1038/nature08221`
- **Validator:** source-cross-check-user-2026-05-14

- **Why it matters:** Reducing 90th-percentile mortality by 14% suggests rapamycin may compress late-life morbidity and improve healthspan in females.
- **Caution:** The reduction was observed only in females at 14 ppm, with results from a multi-site study, but applicability to other doses or genders is uncertain.
- **Next question:** Can rapamycin lower mortality across different percentiles or in mixed-sex populations to enhance overall longevity?
