## The lens

The absence of direct evidence linking cold exposure parameters (e.g., temperature, duration) to brown fat metrics (e.g., volume, activity via PET-CT) in human cohorts prevents a publishable synthesis, as available data is fragmented across unrelated domains like environmental mortality and dietary fat metabolism.

## Already known — do not publish

- Cold exposure activates brown adipose tissue in rodent models
- Brown fat contributes to energy expenditure and glucose homeostasis

## Tensions / contradictions

- Contrasting findings from animal studies (e.g., high-fat diet effects on liver proteins) vs. human exposome research on cold-related mortality, lacking intersection on brown fat
- Discrepancy between acute cold exposure studies in controlled settings and population-level cold-attributable mortality data, with no brown fat mediation analysis

## Evidence gaps

- Human studies quantifying brown fat activation from graded cold exposure (e.g., 16-19°C vs. 20-23°C) in obese vs. lean subgroups
- Long-term cold exposure trials measuring brown fat changes alongside metabolic outcomes like liver fat reduction, which is hinted at in diet studies but not linked
