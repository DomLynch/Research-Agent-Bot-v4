# Frontier review — rapamycin

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-27T05-30-47Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The sexually dimorphic and dose-dependent longevity effects of rapamycin in mice underscore a complex pharmacology where transient versus continuous mTOR inhibition interacts with genetic background and sex-specific aging pathways, challenging uniform anti-aging assumptions.

## Already known — do not publish

- Rapamycin extends lifespan in laboratory mice
- mTOR inhibition is a key mechanism in aging and longevity
- Rapamycin is a common geroprotector in preclinical studies

## Tensions / contradictions

- Bitto 2016's transient rapamycin in C57BL/6 mice (60% extension) contrasts with ITP's continuous feeding in UM-HET3 mice showing 14-26% extension, suggesting strain and regimen dependencies.
- Within ITP studies, high-dose rapamycin (42 ppm) yields greater median lifespan extension in females (26%) than males (23%), indicating sex-specific dose-response relationships.

## Evidence gaps

- Effects of rapamycin on lifespan in young or aged-out mice beyond middle age, or in non-murine models like primates.
- Long-term healthspan outcomes such as cognitive function, muscle integrity, or metabolic health alongside lifespan metrics.
- Mechanistic studies on the role of sex hormones or X-linked genes in rapamycin's longevity effects.

## Paper theses

### #1 — opportunity 100 · `scoping-review`

**Thesis:** Sex and Regimen Dependencies in Rapamycin's Longevity Effects: A Scoping Review of Preclinical Evidence

- novelty 75 / evidence_strength 60 / reviewer_risk 40
- **Why publishable:** This review synthesizes disparate murine studies to highlight critical variables—sex, dose, and treatment timing—that modulate rapamycin's efficacy, providing a framework for targeted gerotherapeutic strategies.


## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- The evidence pool is limited to four mouse studies with heterogeneous designs (e.g., different strains, doses, endpoints), limiting direct comparison and robustness.
- Human translational relevance is speculative, as no human lifespan data are cited, and rapamycin's side effects (e.g., immunosuppression) may offset benefits.
- The focus on median lifespan extension may overlook heterogeneity in mortality risk, as seen in Harrison 2009's 90th-percentile analysis.

## Suggested next extractions

- Rapamycin effects on age-related diseases such as bladder cancer in models with TSC1 mutations, as hinted by alpha data.
- Impact of rapamycin analogs like RAD001 on immune function and vaccine response in elderly human cohorts.
- Dose-response studies with lower rapamycin doses (e.g., <14 ppm) in different mouse strains to define therapeutic windows.
