## Already known — do not publish

- Epigenetic clocks show strong correlation with chronological age (r > 0.8)
- Proteomic age clocks predict chronological age with high accuracy (Pearson r ~0.94)
- Epigenetic age acceleration is associated with health outcomes in older adults

## Tensions / contradictions

- GrimAgeAA associates with 8/9 health outcomes while PhenoAgeAA associates with only 4/9, suggesting differential sensitivity across clocks
- Proteomic age clock accuracy is validated in Chinese and Finnish biobanks but not tested in ethnically diverse cohorts, creating a generalizability gap
- Epigenetic aging acceleration linked to gravidity (n=331) may conflict with null findings in smaller studies, indicating population-specific effects

## Evidence gaps

- Epigenetic age acceleration in COVID-19 survivors beyond 6 months post-diagnosis, stratified by severity and comorbidities
- Proteomic clock validation in African or Latin American biobanks to assess cross-ethnic robustness
- Interaction between parity and epigenetic aging in women with obesity (BMI >30), as current data only cover BMI 25-30 subgroups
