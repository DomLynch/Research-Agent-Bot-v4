# Frontier review — rapamycin

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-16T16-09-36Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The unexamined paradox where a brief 3-month rapamycin exposure in Bitto 2016 yielded a 60% lifespan extension, dwarfing the 23-26% gains from lifelong high-dose ITP regimens, points to a hormetic or adaptive threshold effect that challenges the chronic low-dose paradigm in aging pharmacology.

## Already known — do not publish

- rapamycin extends median lifespan in C57BL/6 and UM-HET3 mouse models
- rapamycin's lifespan effects are dose-dependent in ITP studies

## Tensions / contradictions

- Bitto 2016 (transient, 8 mg/kg/day i.p. in C57BL/6) reported ~60% lifespan extension, while ITP Miller 2014 (chronic, 42 ppm in feed in UM-HET3) reported only 23-26% extension, suggesting strain or protocol disparities may inflate perceived efficacy.
- Male mice exhibit higher lifespan extension in Bitto 2016 (52%) than female mice in Harrison 2009 (14% reduction in 90th-percentile mortality), but outcome measure differences (median lifespan vs. mortality percentile) obscure direct sex-based comparisons.

## Evidence gaps

- The optimal rapamycin dose-duration combination for late-life initiation (e.g., starting at ages beyond 20 months in mice) is unexplored.
