**Snapshot:** 2026-05-17T06-54-30Z

## The lens

The sole evidence fact establishes that MRS can detect <5% changes in brain creatine among other metabolites in a 20-subject cohort, highlighting methodological sensitivity but exposing a critical gap: this precision lacks linkage to clinical relevance or intervention effects, rendering it insufficient for standalone publishable theses without broader evidence.

## Already known — do not publish

- Creatine is a metabolite measurable via brain MRS techniques
- Small sample sizes in MRS studies can detect minimal metabolic shifts

## Evidence gaps

- No data on creatine variability in specific neurological conditions (e.g., neurodegenerative diseases) from the provided evidence
- Lack of dose-response or longitudinal data for creatine interventions affecting MRS measurements

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- The evidence pool consists of a single study with 20 subjects, limiting statistical power and generalizability for any thesis on clinical applications
- Without comparative or interventional data, the detectability of creatine changes cannot be tied to health outcomes or therapeutic efficacy

## Suggested next extractions

- Extract studies on MRS-detected creatine changes in Alzheimer's disease or mild cognitive impairment populations
- Harvest data from creatine supplementation trials involving brain MRS measurements with multiple time points or higher subject numbers
