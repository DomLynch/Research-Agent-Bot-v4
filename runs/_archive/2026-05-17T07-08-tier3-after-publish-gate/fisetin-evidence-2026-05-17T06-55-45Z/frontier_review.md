# Frontier review — fisetin

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-17T06-55-45Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

Fisetin's significant tumor growth inhibition at 30 mg/kg in vivo contrasts sharply with its µg/mL-range antimicrobial efficacy in vitro, suggesting that its bioactivity is highly context-dependent and potentially mediated by divergent or shared pathways that have not been systematically compared.

## Already known — do not publish

- fisetin inhibits tumor growth by 35.7% in nude mice at 30 mg/kg
- fisetin and myricetin inhibit S. mutans growth by 81% and 86% at 8 µg/mL

## Tensions / contradictions

- Divergent effective concentrations: 30 mg/kg (intraperitoneal, in vivo) for tumor inhibition vs. 8 µg/mL (in vitro) for bacterial inhibition, raising questions about bioavailability and target specificity.
- Model disparity: the use of a nude mouse xenograft model for oncology versus a planktonic bacterial culture for microbiology limits direct mechanistic comparison.

## Evidence gaps

- No studies have directly compared fisetin's anti-proliferative mechanisms across tumor and microbial models using standardized doses or endpoints.
- The lack of dose-response data bridging in vivo animal models (e.g., 30 mg/kg) and in vitro effective concentrations (e.g., 8 µg/mL) obscures translational potential.

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- The evidence pool comprises only two studies with different models and endpoints, making it premature to draw cross-disciplinary conclusions.
- Variations in intervention delivery (intraperitoneal vs. in vitro) and measurement outcomes (tumor volume vs. growth inhibition) hinder direct comparison and meta-analysis.
