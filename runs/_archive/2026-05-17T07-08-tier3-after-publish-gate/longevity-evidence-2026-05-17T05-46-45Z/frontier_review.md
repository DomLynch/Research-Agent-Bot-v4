## The lens

Longevity mechanisms exhibit domain-specific critical thresholds – the 16.5% moisture breakpoint in rice seed viability and the discrete 30% mortality reduction from genetic enrichment in human offspring – suggesting that longevity is governed by nonlinear pivotal points rather than continuous gradients, a cross-biological insight with implications for aging research.

## Already known — do not publish

- Genetic factors influence human lifespan
- Environmental conditions affect seed storage longevity

## Tensions / contradictions

- The sharp 30% mortality reduction in offspring from long-lived families (fact_id=10028) implies a threshold effect, contrasting with potential additive genetic models from SNP studies hinted in the literature.
- The binary above/below 16.5% moisture threshold in rice seeds (fact_id=21067, 21068) shows a non-linear response absent in the human genetic data, highlighting divergent longevity mechanisms across kingdoms.

## Evidence gaps

- Cross-species analysis of threshold effects in longevity, specifically between mammals and plants under controlled stressors.
- Dose-response relationships for genetic enrichment in human subgroups beyond the Leiden Longevity Study cohort.
- Environmental triggers for longevity thresholds in seeds beyond moisture, such as temperature or light cycles.

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- The comparison between human and plant longevity is overly reductive, as it ignores mechanistic differences in aging processes.
- The evidence pool is limited to two studies, making the proposed threshold paradigm speculative and lacking in robust statistical support.
- The scoping review may conflate genetic and environmental factors without addressing confounding variables like population heterogeneity or seed accession variability.
