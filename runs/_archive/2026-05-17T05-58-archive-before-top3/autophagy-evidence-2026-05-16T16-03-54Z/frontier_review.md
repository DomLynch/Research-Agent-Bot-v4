## The lens

The juxtaposition of autophagy-related lncRNAs as poor prognostic indicators in lung adenocarcinoma with autophagy's cytoprotective role in glioma therapy resistance reveals a context-dependent paradox where autophagy machinery is co-opted for divergent cellular outcomes, suggesting that therapeutic strategies must be tailored to specific disease mechanisms and genetic backgrounds.

## Already known — do not publish

- Autophagy is a conserved cellular degradation process
- LncRNAs are regulators of gene expression in cancer
- Inhibition of autophagy can sensitize cells to anticancer agents

## Tensions / contradictions

- Between the cytoprotective role of autophagy in glioma cells (fact 8399) and the association of autophagy-related lncRNAs with worse survival in lung adenocarcinoma (facts 7110, 7112), implying that autophagy's impact on prognosis is not uniformly protective but may depend on lncRNA-mediated regulation.
- Between reduced autophagy flux in injured mouse muscles (fact 28274) and targeted selective autophagy of Mtb in macrophages (fact 5229), highlighting tissue-specific and pathogen-specific autophagy responses that may have conflicting implications for host defense versus repair.

## Evidence gaps

- The mechanism by which autophagy-related lncRNAs influence survival in lung adenocarcinoma, such as through modulation of autophagy flux or cross-talk with oncogenic pathways.
- The interplay between autophagy and immune response in bacterial infection versus tissue injury models, particularly how selective autophagy targeting differs from bulk flux changes.
