# Frontier review — mtor

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-15T16-23-34Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

mTOR's paradoxical role as a senescence modulator: inhibition can enhance immune response in the elderly (e.g., RAD001 in 65+ humans) yet exacerbate mortality in murine models of mTOR hypofunction, suggesting context-dependent therapeutic windows that challenge conventional pro-growth dogma. This duality is amplified by tissue-specific effects, such as mTOR suppression abolishing EMT in cervical cancer cells while being essential for EMT induction in the same system.

## Already known — do not publish

- mTOR is a central kinase in cell growth and metabolism
- mTOR hyperactivation promotes cancer progression
- mTOR inhibitors reduce tumor burden in preclinical models

## Tensions / contradictions

- RAD001 at 0.5 mg daily improved influenza vaccine response by ~20% in elderly humans (n=218, Science Translational Medicine 2014), but hypomorphic mTOR mice showed 40% mortality (Journal of Immunology 2013), indicating species-specific survival outcomes
- mTOR inhibition abolished TGF-β1-induced EMT in cervical cancer cells (Medical Science Monitor 2017), yet mTOR activation was required for TGF-β1-induced EMT initiation in the same model (Fact 10), revealing a biphasic regulatory mechanism
- Metformin decreased tumor burden by 72% via mTOR inhibition in NNK-treated A/J mice (Cancer Prevention Research 2010), while bladder cancer exhibits high mTOR activity in 70% of urothelial carcinomas (Cancers 2022), suggesting heterogeneous oncogenic dependencies

## Evidence gaps

- Dose-response relationships of RAD001 beyond 0.5 mg daily in non-elderly adults for vaccine enhancement
- Long-term immunological and survival impacts of mTOR inhibition in patients with chronic inflammation (e.g., CAD group with elevated IL-6/TNF-α, Clinical Science 2014)
- Comparative efficacy of ATP-competitive inhibitors (e.g., WYE-132 with IC50 0.19 nmol/L, Cancer Research 2010) versus allosteric inhibitors (e.g., RAD001) in Flcn-deficient tumors (Oncotarget 2015)

### #1 — opportunity 93 · `scoping-review`

**Thesis:** Context-Dependent mTOR Modulation: A Scoping Review of Paradoxical Outcomes in Immunosenescence and Oncogenesis

- novelty 80 / evidence_strength 70 / reviewer_risk 60
- **Why publishable:** This review synthesizes conflicting evidence across human, murine, and cellular models to identify biological and methodological determinants of mTOR's dual roles, addressing a critical gap in translational research for age-related and neoplastic diseases.


## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- Heterogeneity in study designs (e.g., mouse vs. human, cancer cell lines vs. primary tissues) limits direct comparison of mTOR inhibition effects
- Over-reliance on surrogate endpoints like phospho-S6K (Fact 14) without correlation to clinical outcomes such as overall survival
- Publication bias towards positive findings in mTOR inhibitor trials, potentially skewing scoping review conclusions

## Suggested next extractions

- Clinical trials of everolimus (RAD001) in elderly cohorts with infectious diseases beyond influenza, using vaccine response as a primary endpoint
- Dose-timing studies of mTOR inhibitors in cancer immunotherapy, focusing on combinatorial regimens with CDK4/6 inhibitors (e.g., palbociclib, Clinical Cancer Research 2017)
- Biomarker validation for mTOR activation, such as phospho-ribosomal protein S6, in women with LAM (Journal of Pathology Clinical Research 2020) to predict rapamycin response
