# Frontier review — aspirin_geroprotection

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-27T05-34-57Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The evidence on aspirin's geroprotection is siloed into cancer chemoprevention, adjunct infectious disease therapy, and bleeding risk assessments, with no coherent model linking these to aging biomarkers or lifespan extension, leaving the hypothesis unsubstantiated beyond observational associations.

## Already known — do not publish

- Aspirin's anti-inflammatory properties via COX inhibition are well-documented for pain and cardiovascular benefits.
- Daily aspirin use is standard for secondary prevention of myocardial infarction and stroke.
- Aspirin reduces platelet aggregation, lowering thrombotic event risks in high-risk populations.

## Tensions / contradictions

- Cancer prevention data show a 34% reduction in 20-year colorectal cancer mortality with long-term aspirin, yet acute use in tuberculosis trials (e.g., eLife 2018) showed no significant mortality benefit despite subgroup hints (p=0.06).
- Bleeding risk profiles differ: apixaban versus aspirin in NEJM Evidence 2023 revealed similar adverse event rates (43.9 vs 45.7 per 100 person-years), but with uncertain geroprotective trade-offs.

## Evidence gaps

- Dose-specific geroprotection remains unexplored—81 mg vs 1000 mg aspirin doses in tuberculosis adjunct therapy lack longitudinal aging outcomes.
- Long-term follow-up beyond 20 years in cancer prevention cohorts is missing, especially for prostate cancer risk reduction (29% in long-term low-dose users).
- Mechanistic integration of aspirin's effects on cellular senescence, inflammation, and epigenetic aging in human populations is absent.

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- No randomized controlled trials have been designed with geroprotection (e.g., healthspan or lifespan) as a primary endpoint.
- Evidence is primarily from cancer and cardiovascular contexts, introducing confounding by indication and survivorship bias.
- Heterogeneity in aspirin dosing, duration, and population demographics (e.g., tuberculosis patients vs general elderly) prevents causal attribution to aging modulation.

## Suggested next extractions

- Extract data on aspirin's impact on age-related diseases like osteoarthritis or cognitive decline from existing cardiovascular trials.
- Harvest subgroup analyses from the APRIAS trial (NCT02467582) focusing on elderly participants with biomarkers of aging.
- Investigate aspirin's interaction with gerotherapeutics (e.g., senolytics) in preclinical models, referencing doses from tuberculosis adjunct studies.
