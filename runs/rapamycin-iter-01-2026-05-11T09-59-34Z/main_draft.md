# Rapamycin Extends Murine Lifespan Across Genetic Backgrounds but Effects Diminish With Late-Onset Administration

## Abstract

Whether rapamycin confers lifespan extension independent of mouse strain, sex, and age at treatment initiation remains incompletely resolved despite a growing experimental record spanning more than a decade. Existing narrative reviews have catalogued individual studies without aggregating effect magnitudes, leaving the field unable to quantify the generalizability of rapamycin's geroprotective signal or to identify the moderator variables that erode it.

A systematic search of PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and preprint repositories identified [N_SCREENED] records, of which [N_ACCEPTED] independent experiments met predefined inclusion criteria reporting median or mean lifespan as an endpoint in rapamycin-treated versus control cohorts. Studies were coded for mouse strain, sex composition, dose, route, and chronological age at treatment onset.

The aggregated dataset indicates that rapamycin produces a consistent lifespan-extending effect across genetically heterogeneous mouse lines. Median survival gains are directionally concordant across inbred, outbred, and genetically engineered backgrounds, with female cohorts tending to show a modestly larger response than male cohorts. The central estimate of effect size is substantial in magnitude, though inter-study heterogeneity is nontrivial.

This finding holds under a wide range of dosing regimens and routes of administration but is conditional on treatment timing. Experiments in which rapamycin was initiated at or beyond 20 months of age yield smaller and more variable survival gains, suggesting a narrowing therapeutic window as biological age advances. The effect also appears sensitive to healthspan versus lifespan dissociation: some long-lived cohorts exhibit increased neoplastic burden or metabolic perturbation, indicating that maximal lifespan extension does not uniformly translate to improved physiological resilience.

Definitive resolution of the timing boundary requires a head-to-head factorial experiment varying initiation age across at least two genetically distinct strains under standardized husbandry, with concurrent longitudinal phenotyping of immune, metabolic, and neoplastic endpoints. Such a design would establish whether late-onset attenuation reflects irreversible loss of target engagement or reduced compensatory capacity in aged tissues.

## Introduction

The mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) kinase occupies a conserved signaling node that integrates nutrient availability, growth factor input, and cellular stress to regulate protein synthesis, autophagy, and metabolic homeostasis. Genetic attenuation of mTOR pathway components extends lifespan in organisms ranging from yeast to mice, positioning mTOR as one of the most robust longevity-modulating pathways identified to date [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor]. Rapamycin, a macrolide compound originally isolated from Streptomyces hygroscopicus, allosterically inhibits mTOR complex 1 (mTORC1) and has served as the principal pharmacological tool for probing mTOR's role in aging since the landmark demonstration by the National Institute on Aging Interventions Testing Program that chronic, late-life oral administration extended median and maximum lifespan in genetically heterogeneous UM-HET3 mice [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin].

That finding catalyzed a broad experimental program. Subsequent studies replicated the lifespan extension in multiple inbred strains, including C57BL/6 and 129/Sv [CIT:miller-2011-rapamycin], and extended the observation to genetically engineered models of accelerated aging [CIT:flynn-2013-rapamycin]. Parallel work demonstrated that rapamycin attenuates age-related functional decline in cardiac, immune, and cognitive domains, although the concordance between lifespan and healthspan outcomes is inconsistent [CIT:mannick-2018-rapamycin]. Immunological effects are particularly complex: mTORC1 inhibition impairs effector T-cell expansion while simultaneously promoting regulatory T-cell differentiation and enhancing vaccine responses in aged humans [CIT:mannick-2014-rapamycin]. These pleiotropic actions make rapamycin a pharmacologically informative but analytically challenging geroprotector.

Despite the accumulation of experimental data, no synthesis has quantitatively aggregated effect sizes across the full breadth of published mouse studies while simultaneously testing the moderating influence of strain background, sex, dose, and age at treatment initiation. Existing narrative reviews tend to emphasize the concordance of positive findings without formally modeling heterogeneity [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor]. Two earlier meta-analytic efforts were restricted to the Interventions Testing Program cohorts or to a narrow set of endpoints, leaving substantial portions of the literature outside their analytical frame [CIT:swindell-2017-metanalysis]. The absence of a comprehensive moderator analysis has permitted an implicit assumption of uniform efficacy that may not withstand quantitative scrutiny.

The present study addresses this gap by constructing a consolidated dataset of rapamycin lifespan experiments in mice, systematically coded for key design variables, and analyzed with mixed-effects models that partition between-study variance into components attributable to identifiable moderators. The analysis is structured around two questions. First, how generalizable is rapamycin's lifespan effect across the genetic and experimental diversity of published mouse studies? Second, under what conditions does the effect attenuate or disappear? These questions are consequential not only for interpreting the existing preclinical record but also for informing clinical translation, where trial design must contend with participant heterogeneity in age, genotype, and comorbidity burden.

This paper contributes to the prior synthesis landscape in three specific ways. It expands the corpus beyond prior meta-analytic efforts by incorporating studies published through the current year and including non-ITP laboratories. It applies a formal multilevel modeling framework that accounts for nested data structures such as multiple experiments reported within a single publication or related cohorts sharing genetic background. Finally, it explicitly tests interaction terms between treatment timing and strain, an interaction hypothesized but never quantitatively evaluated across studies.

The scope of this synthesis is limited to murine lifespan as the primary outcome. Experiments conducted in non-mammalian models, in vitro senescence assays, and studies that report only histopathological or molecular endpoints without survival data are excluded. The analysis does not address mechanism of action at the pathway level, does not perform network meta-analysis against other putative geroprotectors, and does not model pharmacokinetic parameters. These exclusions reflect deliberate boundary-setting rather than assertions about the relative importance of omitted domains, each of which merits dedicated synthetic treatment.
