## Methods

A systematic literature search will be conducted across [PLACEHOLDER:databases] for records published through [PLACEHOLDER:date-range]. Prior quantitative syntheses of rapamycin and lifespan have examined subsets of the available evidence [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:tyshkovskiy-2025-vertebrate-meta|prior-meta-analysis]; the present search is designed to capture the contemporary corpus including studies published after those reviews. The search strategy will combine intervention terms (rapamycin, sirolimus) with outcome terms (lifespan, longevity, survival, mortality) and organism terms (mouse, mice, murine), using Boolean operators adapted to each database's indexing conventions [PLACEHOLDER:query-terms]. Reporting will follow the PRISMA 2020 statement, including a flow diagram of records identified, screened, assessed for eligibility, and included [CIT:page-2020-prisma|method-citation].
Studies are eligible for the primary pooled analysis if they satisfy PICO criteria: the population is mice of any eligible age at treatment initiation, with age coded as a pre-specified moderator; the intervention is systemic rapamycin or sirolimus at any dose, route, or schedule; the comparator is an untreated or vehicle-treated control group; and the outcome is lifespan, reported as median or mean lifespan, survival curves, or hazard ratios with sufficient summary statistics for effect-size computation. Exclusion criteria are: in vitro studies without in vivo survival data, combination-intervention studies lacking a rapamycin-only arm, and studies whose full text cannot be obtained after author contact. Genetically modified mouse models receiving exogenous rapamycin will be included only when the design permits extraction of a rapamycin-versus-control contrast within the same genetic background; these studies will be coded separately and excluded in a pre-specified sensitivity analysis that examines robustness to genetic-background complexity [PLACEHOLDER:gm-sensitivity-protocol]. Everolimus, RTB101, and other rapalogs are excluded from the primary pooled lifespan analysis but retained in a separate translational evidence map (defined under Sensitivity Analyses). The two layers do not pool.

Two reviewers will independently extract data using a standardized, pilot-tested electronic form. Variables include: publication identifiers; mouse strain, sex, and age at treatment initiation; rapamycin dose, route, and schedule; control group type; sample size per group at study start; lifespan outcome measure; and summary statistics for effect-size computation (means, standard deviations, standard errors, confidence intervals, or raw survival data). All values will be extracted in their original reported units. Where dose is reported as dietary parts per million, the original value will be retained alongside a conversion to mg/kg/day computed under a stated body-weight and food-intake assumption [PLACEHOLDER:conversion-assumption]; the original-unit value serves as a precision-loss sensitivity check. Disagreements will be resolved by discussion, with a third reviewer adjudicating unresolved discrepancies.

Pre-specified study-level moderators will be coded to map sources of heterogeneity. [MODERATOR_P:mouse-strain] captures genetic background (e.g., C57BL/6, heterogeneous stock, other lines), as strain determines baseline lifespan and may modulate rapamycin response. [MODERATOR_P:sex] records male, female, or mixed-sex cohorts. [MODERATOR_P:dose] records the administered rapamycin dose in original units, treated as continuous where sample size permits; categorized dose levels will be derived from the observed distribution only for graphical display, not for primary modeling. [MODERATOR_P:age-at-initiation] records treatment start age, since the timing of rapamycin initiation substantially alters its lifespan effects [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin|primary-study]. [MODERATOR_P:treatment-schedule] distinguishes continuous from intermittent dosing. [MODERATOR_P:route] records administration method (diet, gavage, injection). Moderators with more than [PLACEHOLDER:missingness-threshold] missing data will be flagged and analyzed only in available-case subsets.

Risk of bias will be assessed using the SYRCLE tool for animal intervention studies, which evaluates sequence generation, baseline characteristics, allocation concealment, blinding, incomplete outcome data, and selective reporting [CIT:hooijmans-2014-syrcle|method-citation]. Each domain is rated as low, unclear, or high risk by two independent reviewers (third-reviewer arbitration as in extraction). Reporting completeness is evaluated against the ARRIVE 2.0 checklist [CIT:percie-du-sert-2020-arrive|method-citation]. Evidence-body certainty is contextualised using the GRADE framework [CIT:schunemann-grade|method-citation], adapted to the preclinical setting with interpretive caveats noted.

The primary analysis will estimate the pooled effect of rapamycin on mouse lifespan using a multi-level mixed-effects meta-regression. The primary effect-size metric is the log response ratio of median lifespan, defined as the natural logarithm of the median lifespan in rapamycin-treated animals divided by matched controls within the same study; mean lifespan will be substituted where median lifespan is unavailable and the substitution flagged in sensitivity analyses. Hazard ratios extracted from survival curves will be analysed in a pre-specified sensitivity model rather than pooled with the lifespan-ratio estimates. Random intercepts for study identifier will account for between-study heterogeneity; where studies report multiple independent treatment groups, a nested random effect for outcome within study will be specified [CIT:viechtbauer-2010-metafor|method-citation]. Between-study variance will be estimated via restricted maximum likelihood. Confidence intervals for pooled estimates and moderator coefficients will use the Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Jonkman correction for improved coverage with moderate study counts [CIT:hartung-knapp|method-citation]. Fixed-effects predictors will include the coded moderators, tested individually and in joint models. The analysis will be implemented in R using the metafor package [CIT:viechtbauer-2010-metafor|method-citation]. Model fit will be compared via likelihood-ratio tests and information criteria.

Robustness will be examined through pre-specified sensitivity analyses. Leave-one-out analysis will assess the influence of individual studies. Publication bias will be evaluated via funnel plots and Egger's regression test for asymmetry [CIT:egger-1997-funnel|method-citation], with trim-and-fill estimation of the adjusted pooled effect. Heterogeneity will be quantified using I² and its confidence interval [CIT:higgins-2003-i2|method-citation], supplemented by prediction intervals for the true effect in a new study. A translational evidence map will separately characterise rapalog evidence at aging-relevant endpoints — immune response, infection incidence, survival-adjacent outcomes, and validated healthspan measures — drawing on everolimus [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus|clinical-trial] and RTB101 [CIT:mannick-2018-rtb101|clinical-trial] trials and any available murine analog studies. This map is descriptive and is not pooled with the primary rapamycin/sirolimus lifespan corpus.

A tension matrix will map the joint influence of moderator pairs on the estimated lifespan effect. Prior syntheses have identified substantial heterogeneity across studies [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis], motivating this moderator-focused visualization. Pre-specified matrices will be generated for sex-by-strain, dose-by-age-at-initiation, and route-by-dose; additional exploratory matrices may be generated on the basis of joint-model results and biological plausibility and are labelled as exploratory [PLACEHOLDER:moderator-pair-selection]. For each cell, the pooled effect and confidence interval will be computed from the fitted meta-regression. Cells with fewer than [PLACEHOLDER:min-k-per-cell] contributing studies will be flagged as sparsely populated. The matrix is designed to reveal whether the lifespan effect is concentrated in particular moderator combinations or robust across settings. Sparsely populated cells will be highlighted as priorities for future primary research, providing a roadmap for targeted replication.
