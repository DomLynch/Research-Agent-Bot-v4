=== DISCUSSION ===

This meta-analysis was designed to estimate the effect of metformin on murine lifespan by synthesizing a pre-specified corpus of contract-passing primary studies. The current evidence base is limited; [PACKET:primary_effect] reports a k_studies value below the threshold for a fully powered, pre-specified joint moderator framework. Consequently, the pooled effect size is not reported here, as the analysis is not designed to yield a stable, generalizable estimate. The primary pool composition [PACKET:primary_pool_composition] indicates that the available studies do not yet provide sufficient statistical power or homogeneity to draw a definitive conclusion about the direction or magnitude of metformin's effect on lifespan in this model system. Prior syntheses have addressed related but distinct questions; the present work extends their scope by applying a pre-registered, contract-driven methodology to a murine-specific corpus focused on the primary endpoint of lifespan [CIT:chen-2022-metformin-meta|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:ivimey-cook-tyshkovskiy-2025-meta|prior-meta-analysis].

The position of this analysis relative to prior quantitative syntheses warrants careful consideration. Earlier meta-analyses have aggregated data across a broader range of models and endpoints, often including translational biguanides [CIT:chen-2022-metformin-meta|prior-meta-analysis]. The present corpus excludes buformin and phenformin from the primary pooled analysis to isolate the evidence for metformin itself. Where prior syntheses reported aggregate trends, the current analysis, constrained by its contract-passing corpus size, is not powered to confirm or contradict those specific numerical estimates. The divergence lies not in findings but in scope and methodological discipline: this synthesis applies a pre-specified gateway pipeline, sentinel-recall threshold, and separate pooling for distinct metric families, which prior works did not employ [CIT:ivimey-cook-tyshkovskiy-2025-meta|prior-meta-analysis]. Therefore, alignment or divergence in summary effects is not interpretable as a simple agreement or disagreement, but as a difference in the population of studies being summarized.

The mechanistic rationale for a metformin lifespan effect is drawn from extensive prior work, which this analysis does not test but uses to frame its hypothesis. Canonical reviews describe metformin's action via mitochondrial complex I inhibition, leading to decreased gluconeogenesis and altered cellular energy status, with downstream effects on nutrient-sensing pathways like AMPK and mTOR [CIT:foretz-viollet-2014-mechanism|mechanism-review]. Additional proposed mechanisms include modulation of the gut microbiome, reduction in oxidative stress, and induction of autophagy [CIT:madiraju-2014-metformin-mechanism|mechanism-review]. These pathways are theoretically linked to lifespan extension, but the evidence derives largely from cellular or metabolic endpoints, not direct lifespan quantification in murine models under the controlled conditions of the primary studies in this corpus.

Boundary conditions that may moderate any potential effect are currently underdetermined in the evidence base. Potential moderators include murine strain, sex, age at initiation, dose, route of administration, and pathogen-free status. The included studies vary on these dimensions, but the corpus is too small to permit a powered investigation of their influence. For instance, while some primary studies used specific inbred strains [CIT:martin-montalvo-2013-metformin|primary-study] [CIT:anisimov-2011-metformin|primary-study], the meta-analytic framework cannot yet partition variance by strain due to insufficient k. Similarly, sex-based differences in response, a common moderator in aging research, lack the statistical power for formal subgroup analysis within this corpus. The question of whether intervention timing (e.g., mid-life versus late-life initiation) alters efficacy remains equally open.

Translational considerations must be clearly separated from this murine-focused synthesis. Clinical trials, such as the TAME trial, are investigating metformin's potential to extend healthspan in humans [CIT:crowley-2017-metformin-clinical|clinical-trial] [CIT:barzilai-tame-2016-review|narrative-review]. These trials are informed by the preclinical data, but they address a distinct population and endpoint. Critically, translational biguanides (buformin, phenformin) are excluded from the primary analysis corpus and are retained only for a translational sensitivity layer or discussion. Therefore, no direct inference from this murine meta-analysis to human longevity outcomes is supported. The primary value of this work is to provide a calibrated, methodologically transparent assessment of the current preclinical evidence for metformin's effect on murine lifespan, highlighting the limitations of the existing data for answering the central research question.

=== LIMITATIONS ===

The primary limitation is the small corpus size, with [PACKET:primary_effect] indicating a low k_studies value for the pooled analysis, which inherently limits statistical power and the stability of any estimated effect. This scarcity is linked to the stringent pre-specified inclusion contract; the sentinel-recall gate [PACKET:sentinel_recall] confirmed that a key benchmark study was retrieved, but the overall yield of contract-passing studies was low. The analysis therefore cannot detect modest effects or reliably explore heterogeneity. The metric-family discipline—where 90th-percentile and median lifespan ratios are pooled separately—prevents cross-family inference and further reduces the effective k for any single analysis. While the pipeline employed automated risk-of-bias adjudication for consistency, it lacked explicit human arbitration, which may have misclassified study quality nuances. Documented manual full-text overrides [PACKET:study_selection] were performed solely for source recovery, not to alter eligibility, meaning some studies could not be retrieved for screening. The specific missing data are therefore: a larger corpus of contract-passing primary studies, a higher k for subgroup analyses, and continuous lifespan data from studies reporting only summary statistics.

=== CONCLUSION ===

Based on the current contract-passing corpus, the meta-analysis is not powered to support a definitive conclusion regarding the effect of metformin on murine lifespan. A robust conclusion would require more primary studies meeting the pre-specified contract and a repair of sentinel-recall gaps to ensure comprehensive evidence capture. The broader interpretive frame positions this work as a methodological template: it demonstrates a rigorous, pre-registered synthesis pipeline for aging interventions, whose output currently highlights the evidentiary limitations of the existing preclinical literature rather than establishing a biological effect.
