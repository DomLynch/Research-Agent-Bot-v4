## The lens

The evidence pool consists solely of brain tumor epidemiology facts, which are irrelevant to brain age estimation via MRI, revealing a critical disconnect that necessitates reframing around how incidental neuropathological findings confound neuroimaging-based aging biomarkers. A publishable angle would dissect the methodological blind spots in current brain age models when applied to clinical populations with co-morbidities like tumors.

## Already known — do not publish

- Average annual age-adjusted incidence rate of brain tumors is approximately 24 per 100,000 population
- Glioblastoma accounts for over 14% of all malignant brain tumors
- Five-year survival for malignant brain tumors is around 35%

## Tensions / contradictions

- Conflict between using MRI for diagnostic tumor assessment versus prognostic brain age prediction, where tumor presence may artificially inflate or deflate age estimates
- Variation in MRI sequences optimized for tumor detection (e.g., contrast-enhanced) versus structural aging biomarkers (e.g., cortical thickness)

## Evidence gaps

- No studies directly correlating brain tumor histopathology (e.g., glioblastoma) with deviations in predicted brain age
- Lack of validated brain age algorithms trained or tested on cohorts with incidental brain tumors
- Under-researched impact of tumor location or treatment on MRI-based aging metrics
