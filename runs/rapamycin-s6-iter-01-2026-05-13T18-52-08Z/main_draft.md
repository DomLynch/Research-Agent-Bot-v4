## Discussion

This scoping review sought to map the evidence regarding the effect of rapamycin and sirolimus on lifespan in mice. The identified corpus, comprising [PACKET:primary_pool_composition] records, provides a narrative basis for evaluating lifespan extension, though the absence of a parseable pool of effects ([PACKET:primary_effect]) precludes the calculation of a pooled estimate. The current evidence set allows for a descriptive mapping of study designs and reported outcomes, but it does not support a quantitative conclusion regarding a universal effect size for lifespan extension across the murine species.

The findings of this scoping review align with the general direction of prior syntheses, such as those conducted by Fok et al. [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis] and Swindell et al. [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis], which have previously quantified the impact of mTOR inhibition on longevity. While those works utilized different inclusion criteria and pooling methods, the present work extends the mapping of the literature by applying a strict evidence-contract gate to ensure that only records with explicit endpoint and intervention coverage are retained. Because the current iteration resulted in a narrative synthesis rather than a quantitative pool, the results are complementary to, rather than contradictory with, the estimates provided in prior meta-analyses [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:tyshkovskiy-2025-vertebrate-meta|prior-meta-analysis].

The biological plausibility of these observations is rooted in the inhibition of the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway. As detailed in mechanism reviews, rapamycin suppresses mTORC1, which modulates autophagy, protein synthesis, and cellular senescence [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor|mechanism-review] [CIT:saxton-2017-mtor|mechanism-review]. These pathways are central to the regulation of nutrient sensing and aging processes in mice, providing a theoretical framework for why mTOR inhibition is associated with lifespan alterations across various murine models.

Several boundary conditions remain underdetermined within the current corpus. The influence of mouse strain, sex, dosage levels, and the timing of administration (e.g., early-life versus late-life intervention) varies across the included studies, making it difficult to isolate the primary drivers of the observed effects. Furthermore, the impact of administration routes and the baseline pathogen status of the mice are not consistently reported, which may introduce variance in the reported lifespan outcomes. These factors represent critical gaps in the current evidence base that prevent a more granular understanding of rapamycin's efficacy.

Translational considerations are highlighted by clinical trials investigating rapalogs in humans, such as the use of everolimus [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus|clinical-trial] and RTB101 [CIT:mannick-2018-rtb101|clinical-trial]. It is important to explicitly state that translational interventions—including everolimus, RTB101, and other rapalogs—were excluded from the primary pooled analysis to maintain the scope of the murine lifespan endpoint. While these clinical data provide context for the potential utility of mTOR inhibition, they operate under different pharmacological and regulatory constraints than the primary murine corpus.

## Limitations

The primary limitation of this scoping review is the restricted size of the final corpus, which resulted in a narrative synthesis rather than a quantitative pool. The sentinel-recall gate [PACKET:sentinel_recall] indicates that some records may have been missed due to the limitations of automated retrieval, although SHA-256-hashed manual injections were used for known sentinels [PACKET:study_selection]. With [PACKET:primary_effect] studies meeting the strict evidence-contract for effect extraction, the power to detect consistent trends is limited, and the absence of a pool means that no statistical inference regarding the magnitude of lifespan extension was performed. Additionally, the pipeline maintains strict metric-family discipline; for example, 90th-percentile lifespan ratios and median lifespan ratios are treated as distinct families and are not cross-pooled, which prevents the aggregation of data across different reporting standards. Risk-of-bias adjudication was performed by a single LLM judge without human arbitration, and the lack of dual independent extraction may introduce systematic errors in data parsing. Finally, the reliance on automated evidence-contract gates means that studies with insufficient character counts or missing endpoint quotes were demoted, potentially excluding relevant but poorly reported data.

## Conclusion

The current corpus provides a narrative mapping of rapamycin's effect on mouse lifespan, but the lack of a parseable pool of effects precludes a quantitative conclusion on the magnitude of extension. This conclusion would be modified if a larger number of contract-passing studies were identified or if sentinel repair increased the number of records meeting the evidence-contract floor. Within the broader interpretive frame, these results highlight the necessity of standardized reporting for lifespan endpoints to enable robust quantitative synthesis of mTOR-inhibiting interventions in murine models.
