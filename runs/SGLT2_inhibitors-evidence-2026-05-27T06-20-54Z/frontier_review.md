# Frontier review — SGLT2_inhibitors

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-27T06-20-54Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The cerebrovascular protection afforded by SGLT2 inhibitors appears to be comparator-specific, with a clinically significant divergence between effects when benchmarked against DPP-4 inhibitors versus broader non-SGLT2 inhibitor classes, suggesting a mechanism not fully explained by glycemic control. Concurrently, the paradox of increased hypotension risk with non-selective agents underscores a need to dissect pharmacological heterogeneity within the SGLT2 inhibitor class itself.

## Already known — do not publish

- SGLT2 inhibitors lower HbA1c by 0.6-0.8% without increasing hypoglycemia risk.
- SGLT2 inhibitors reduce the risk of serious heart failure events by 25-40%.

## Tensions / contradictions

- Fact 182560 (stroke HR 0.83 vs. non-SGLT2 inhibitors) versus Fact 186175 (hypotension OR 1.87 with non-selective SGLT2 inhibitors) creates an efficacy-safety paradox requiring mechanistic explanation.
- Fact 139392 (20% CV death/HF reduction in non-diabetic HF patients) exists alongside predominantly type 2 diabetes-focused stroke and HF data, indicating a potential knowledge gap in therapeutic generalization.

## Evidence gaps

- Cerebrovascular outcomes in patient subgroups with both type 2 diabetes and hypertension, where hypotension risk may negate benefits.
- Dose-specific hypotension risk stratification for non-selective SGLT2 inhibitors like canagliflozin versus selective agents like empagliflozin.
- Long-term neurological outcomes (e.g., dementia, cognitive decline) in SGLT2 inhibitor users, given the alpha hints on brain penetration.

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- The stroke reduction comparison (Facts 182560 vs 182559) may confound by unmeasured differences in baseline cardiovascular risk or concurrent medication use between comparator arms.
- Hypotension risk (Fact 186175) is reported only for non-selective SGLT2 inhibitors; generalizing this to the entire class is unsupported without data on selective agents.
- The heart failure evidence for non-diabetic patients (Fact 139392) relies on a single journal publication; broader replication is needed to confirm external validity.

## Suggested next extractions

- Neurological endpoints (e.g., cognitive function, dementia incidence) in SGLT2 inhibitor clinical trials, focusing on brain-penetrant agents.
- Head-to-head trials comparing selective (e.g., empagliflozin) versus non-selective SGLT2 inhibitors on hypotension and stroke outcomes.
