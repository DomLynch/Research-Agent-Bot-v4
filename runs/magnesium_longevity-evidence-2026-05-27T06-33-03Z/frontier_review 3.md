**Snapshot:** 2026-05-27T06-33-03Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The evidence pool is devoid of direct facts linking magnesium to longevity endpoints, revealing a research landscape dominated by acute clinical interventions (e.g., magnesium infusions for dyspnoea) rather than sustained intake and aging biomarkers, which obscures publishable connections.

## Already known — do not publish

- Magnesium supplementation reduces hospital admissions for acute asthma exacerbations
- Dietary magnesium is essential for metabolic and physiological functions

## Tensions / contradictions

- Contrast between acute magnesium infusion benefits in short-term trials (e.g., improved dyspnoea scores with SMD -1.40) and the absence of data on chronic intake's impact on lifespan or aging markers.
- Discrepancy in population intake patterns—e.g., one-third of Americans taking supplements at 146 mg/d mean dose—without evidence tying this to longevity outcomes, versus disease-specific protective doses >329 mg/day.

## Evidence gaps

- No studies examining serum magnesium within the narrow protective range (2.19-2.26 mg/dL) in aging cohorts for mortality or age-related disease incidence.
- Lack of dose-response analysis for magnesium supplementation above 329 mg/day on cellular aging hallmarks like telomere attrition or mitochondrial dysfunction in humans.
- Underexplored subgroups include individuals with magnesium deficiency scores (MDS) ≥2 and specific CVD risks, where longevity implications remain unstudied.
