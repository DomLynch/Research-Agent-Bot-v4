## Discussion

The present meta-analytic synthesis addresses the question of whether acarbose, an alpha-glucosidase inhibitor, extends murine lifespan under controlled experimental conditions. The pipeline is designed to estimate a pooled effect within the primary corpus and to map heterogeneity across documented moderators. When [PACKET:primary_effect] indicates k_studies ≥ 2 within a single metric family, the pipeline will produce an inverse-variance random-effects pooled estimate with Hartung-Knapp–Knapp–Hartung adjustment [CIT:hartung-knapp|method-citation] to protect against small-study variance inflation. The composition of the contract-passing pool — [PACKET:primary_pool_composition] — will determine whether the corpus contains sufficient endpoint and intervention coverage for inference. At present, the analysis has not yet run; accordingly, no aggregate effect direction, magnitude, or confidence interval is asserted here. The discussion is structured to contextualize what the pipeline is designed to extract and to situate the planned findings within the existing literature.

Prior syntheses have examined caloric-restriction mimetic pharmacology in rodent models with varying scope and methodological rigor. Existing meta-analytic work on murine lifespan interventions has addressed heterogeneous pharmacological classes [CIT:heart-2016-6924|narrative-review], while narrative reviews have catalogued acarbose among candidates for glucose-mediated lifespan modulation without quantitative pooling [CIT:considerations-2019-2660|narrative-review]. The present pipeline extends this literature by applying a uniform evidence-contract gate — requiring parsed-text adequacy, minimum character count, and at least two non-title evidence quotes affirming the declared study system, intervention, comparator, and endpoint — before effect extraction. This contract-first design is intended to reduce extraction ambiguity that can arise when eligibility criteria are applied post hoc. Alignment or divergence between the present pooled estimate and prior syntheses will depend on the degree of overlap in included studies and the stringency of the evidence-contract filtering. Where prior work has drawn on broader intervention categories or less restrictive text-adequacy thresholds, the present corpus may capture a narrower but more internally consistent subset.

The mechanistic rationale for acarbose's potential lifespan effects rests on postprandial glucose suppression and downstream modulation of insulin and IGF-1 signaling pathways [CIT:cap-2021-3345|mechanism-review]. Alpha-glucosidase inhibition delays carbohydrate digestion in the proximal intestine, flattening postprandial glycemic excursions. In murine models, chronic glucose restriction has been associated with attenuated mTOR signaling, enhanced autophagy, and reduced oxidative damage — pathways implicated in the aging process. However, the present analysis does not treat mechanism as an endpoint; rather, mechanism-informed moderators (dose, dietary composition, route of administration) are specified as covariates whose joint effects the pipeline is designed to quantify once contract-passing effects accumulate to k ≥ threshold per stratum.

Boundary conditions that may moderate the acarbose–lifespan association remain underdetermined in the present iteration. The pipeline records strain, sex, dose, timing of intervention initiation, route, and pathogen status as structured covariates, but the contracted corpus may not yield sufficient k per moderator stratum to support partitioned inference. Strain-specific metabolic phenotypes — for example, differences between C57BL/6 substrains and other commonly used murine lines — represent a plausible source of heterogeneity that the pipeline is designed to test but cannot resolve when strata are sparsely populated. Sex as a moderator is of particular translational interest, yet prior experimental work has not uniformly reported sex-stratified survival data [CIT:considerations-2019-2660|narrative-review]. Dose–response estimation is constrained by the limited range of dosing regimens represented in the eligible literature. The pipeline's tension-matrix capability, which cross-tabulates moderator levels to visualize interaction patterns, is a designed-for feature deferred until the corpus accrues sufficient within-family contract-passing effects.

Translational considerations are pertinent because acarbose has been tested in human clinical populations, including the Study on the Prediction of Atherosclerosis Risk by Acarbose [CIT:enhancement-2022-2064|clinical-trial]. That trial assessed cardiovascular endpoints rather than lifespan as a primary outcome, and its relevance to the present murine synthesis is indirect. The primary corpus analyzed here is restricted to mouse lifespan studies; human clinical-trial data are not pooled with preclinical survival effects, and no cross-species effect-size extrapolation is attempted. Translational interventions in human cohorts are not in the primary corpus and are cited here solely for mechanistic and contextual framing.

## Limitations

The present synthesis is subject to several specific limitations inherent in the pipeline's design and in the contracted corpus it has identified. The retrieval sweep — a single multi-source pass across the databases specified in the topic pack — is subject to a sentinel-recall gate whose result is reported as [PACKET:sentinel_recall]; this metric quantifies the fraction of known sentinel records (identified a priori from prior narrative reviews) that the automated retrieval recovered, and any shortfall indicates a non-trivial probability that eligible studies were missed rather than excluded on merit. The k_studies value reported in [PACKET:primary_effect] constrains the inferential power of the pooled estimate: at k = 2, the random-effects model yields wide confidence intervals and minimal ability to detect between-study heterogeneity; the I² statistic [CIT:higgins-2003-i2|method-citation] and its associated prediction interval are unstable at small k, and publication-bias diagnostics such as funnel-plot asymmetry tests [CIT:egger-1997-funnel|method-citation] are not interpretable below approximately ten studies. The pipeline's metric-family discipline — pooling contract-passing effects separately for 90th-percentile lifespan ratios, median lifespan ratios, and maximum lifespan ratios without cross-family inference — prevents synthesis of studies that report incompatible summary statistics, which may further reduce k in any single family. Risk-of-bias adjudication is performed by a single LLM judge operating on automated text extraction rather than domain-expert human evaluation against the method-citation framework appropriate to the study designs represented [CIT:hooijmans-2014-syrcle|method-citation]; the pipeline does not currently execute a structured risk-of-bias tool but instead relies on evidence-contract compliance as a proxy for data integrity. Manual full-text injection via SHA-256-hashed retrieval — documented in [PACKET:study_selection] — constitutes source recovery for sentinel records that the automated sweep cannot access, not an override of eligibility criteria. These injections are logged and auditable but introduce a heterogeneous text-quality pathway that may differ from routinely retrieved records in parsing fidelity.

## Conclusion

The contracted corpus, as assessed by the present pipeline, provides a quantitative foundation for estimating the pooled effect of acarbose on murine lifespan within the metric families for which k_studies ≥ 2 contract-passing effects are available; the precision and generalizability of that estimate are calibrated by the specific k achieved [PACKET:primary_effect]. The conclusion would meaningfully change if additional contract-passing studies were incorporated — whether through sentinel-recall repair, expansion of the retrieval source list, or relaxation of the evidence-contract gate to accommodate records with partial text availability — or if the corpus accrued sufficient moderator-stratum cells to partition heterogeneity by strain, sex, dose, or timing. The broader interpretive frame positions acarbose within a class of glucose-modulating interventions whose lifespan effects in murine models are mechanistically plausible but empirically heterogeneous, and whose translational relevance to human aging remains an open question that preclinical synthesis alone cannot resolve.
