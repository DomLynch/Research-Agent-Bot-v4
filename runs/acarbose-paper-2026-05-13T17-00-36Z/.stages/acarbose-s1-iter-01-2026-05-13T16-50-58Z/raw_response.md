=== TITLE ===
Quantitative Synthesis of Acarbose Effects on Murine Lifespan Across Diverse Experimental Parameters

=== ABSTRACT ===
To what extent does acarbose consistently extend lifespan in mouse models across varying strains, dosages, and dietary contexts? Prior syntheses have identified general lifespan extensions associated with alpha-glucosidase inhibition [CIT:cap-2021-3345|mechanism-review], yet uncertainty remains regarding the magnitude of these effects when partitioned by specific murine genetic backgrounds and administration timings [CIT:considerations-2019-2660|narrative-review]. This study is implemented through a reproducible evidence-contract pipeline to quantify these variances.

Following a deterministic rule-triage and a universal evidence-contract gate, [N_ACCEPTED] papers will be included across [K_STUDIES] distinct experiments. Effect extraction is performed by a single LLM pass per paper, with a Researka Tier-2 canonical-fact cross-check overlay for numeric precision.

The planned analysis is designed to estimate the pooled effect size using an inverse-variance random-effects model. Variance partitioning will be conducted via mixed-effects models to test the influence of moderators, including sex, strain, and dose. A tension-matrix rendering is designed-for this pipeline and will be executed if the corpus accrues sufficient moderator-stratum cells. The analysis specifically interrogates whether the lifespan extension is contingent upon the baseline caloric density of the diet or the age of onset of acarbose administration.

The resulting synthesis is designed to map the boundary conditions of alpha-glucosidase inhibition. Findings will indicate whether future research should prioritize high-dose titration in specific genetic backgrounds or if the effect is robust across the murine species, thereby refining the target parameters for subsequent longevity interventions.

=== INTRODUCTION ===
The modulation of glucose absorption via alpha-glucosidase inhibition represents a significant axis for extending lifespan in murine models [CIT:cap-2021-3345|mechanism-review]. Acarbose, a pseudo-trisaccharide that inhibits the enzymes responsible for digesting complex carbohydrates, is hypothesized to extend lifespan by altering the glycemic profile and potentially inducing a caloric restriction-like state [CIT:heart-2016-6924|narrative-review]. The significance of this research question lies in the potential for acarbose to serve as a scalable pharmacological mimetic of dietary restriction, provided its effects are consistent across diverse biological contexts [CIT:cap-2021-3345|mechanism-review]. However, the degree of this consistency remains an open empirical question, as the interaction between drug dosage and host genetics often dictates the magnitude of the longevity response [CIT:considerations-2019-2660|narrative-review].

The prior synthesis landscape has provided foundational insights into the role of carbohydrate restriction. For instance, early narrative reviews have detailed the biochemical pathways through which acarbose influences insulin sensitivity and glucose homeostasis in mice [CIT:heart-2016-6924|narrative-review]. These works established that alpha-glucosidase inhibitors can shift the metabolic profile toward longevity-associated phenotypes [CIT:cap-2021-3345|mechanism-review]. Despite these contributions, existing syntheses have not provided a fully powered, pre-specified joint moderator framework to isolate the effects of strain and sex on the lifespan extension produced by acarbose [CIT:considerations-2019-2660|narrative-review]. While the general direction of the effect is often reported as positive, the variance in reported lifespan increases—ranging from modest to substantial—suggests that the effect size is highly sensitive to experimental parameters [CIT:cap-2021-3345|mechanism-review].

The present work extends prior syntheses by implementing a strict evidence-contract pipeline to minimize extraction bias and ensure that only records meeting a high threshold of evidence adequacy are pooled [CIT:page-2020-prisma|method-citation]. Unlike previous narrative summaries that may aggregate findings qualitatively, this analysis is designed to quantify the heterogeneity of acarbose's effect on lifespan using a random-effects framework [CIT:higgins-2003-i2|method-citation]. By applying a Researka Tier-2 canonical-fact cross-check, this study refines the precision of extracted effect sizes, specifically targeting the percent-change numerics that often vary across primary reports [CIT:percie-du-sert-2020-arrive|method-citation]. This approach allows for a more rigorous stress-test of the hypothesis that acarbose provides a universal lifespan benefit regardless of the murine strain used [CIT:considerations-2019-2660|narrative-review].

A critical gap in the current literature is the lack of a systematic evaluation of dose-response curves across different murine ages at the start of treatment [CIT:cap-2021-3345|mechanism-review]. Prior work has often focused on a single "optimal" dose or a specific age of onset, leaving the broader parameter space unexplored [CIT:heart-2016-6924|narrative-review]. The planned analysis will partition variance by dose and timing to determine if the lifespan extension is a linear function of dosage or if there is a saturation point beyond which no further benefit is observed [CIT:considerations-2019-2660|narrative-review]. Furthermore, the interaction between acarbose and the baseline caloric content of the diet remains poorly quantified in pooled analyses [CIT:cap-2021-3345|mechanism-review]. This study is designed to estimate whether the efficacy of acarbose is dependent on a high-carbohydrate diet or if it maintains potency under standard chow conditions [CIT:heart-2016-6924|narrative-review].

To maintain a precise scope, this synthesis focuses exclusively on the primary endpoint of lifespan in mice, mice, and murine models [CIT:considerations-2019-2660|narrative-review]. The analysis is restricted to acarbose and other alpha-glucosidase inhibitors that share a similar mechanism of action [CIT:cap-2021-3345|mechanism-review]. Explicit non-goals of this study include the evaluation of secondary healthspan markers, such as grip strength or cognitive function, unless they are directly tied to the primary lifespan endpoint [CIT:heart-2016-6924|narrative-review]. Additionally, this work does not aim to perform a risk-of-bias adjudication using a specific framework; such an analysis is deferred in favor of the evidence-contract gate, which ensures that only papers with sufficient parsed-text adequacy and endpoint coverage are included [CIT:schunemann-grade|method-citation].

The planned analysis will utilize an inverse-variance random-effects pool to synthesize the data [CIT:viechtbauer-2010-metafor|method-citation]. To address potential heterogeneity, the study will employ the Hartung-Knapp-Sidik-Xing-Zhou adjustment for the variance of the random-effects model [CIT:hartung-knapp|method-citation]. While the pipeline is designed for advanced diagnostics, such as leave-one-out analysis and publication-bias regression via funnel-plot inspection [CIT:egger-1997-funnel|method-citation], these capabilities are deferred until the corpus accrues a sufficient number of contract-passing effects per metric family. By adhering to the PRISMA guidelines for reporting [CIT:page-2020-prisma|method-citation], this work aims to provide a calibrated estimate of the lifespan-extending potential of acarbose, thereby clarifying the role of alpha-glucosidase inhibition in the broader landscape of murine longevity research [CIT:cap-2021-3345|mechanism-review].
