# Frontier review — rapamycin

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-14T16-22-02Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The sex-dependent dose-response relationship in rapamycin lifespan extension shows a paradoxical direction reversal: at standard ITP dose (14 ppm, UM-HET3), males benefit more (14% vs 9%), but at 3× dose (42 ppm), females overtake males (26% vs 23%), implying a dose-dependent sexual dimorphism threshold likely mediated by estrogen-mTOR crosstalk that has never been formally modeled or mechanistically interrogated. Separately, the 2016 eLife transient 3-month C57BL/6 protocol achieving ~60% extension dwarfs all continuous ITP feeding results, but the strain confound (inbred vs. heterogeneous stock) makes direct dosing-regimen comparison uninterpretable without additional controls—a gap that itself frames a publishable call for targeted experiments.

## Already known — do not publish

- rapamycin extends lifespan in mice
- rapamycin inhibits mTOR
- rapamycin has immunomodulatory effects
- everolimus/rapamycin are related compounds
- the NIA Interventions Testing Program tests longevity interventions

## Tensions / contradictions

- Standard ITP dose (14 ppm, Nature 2009): male advantage (14% vs 9%). At 3× dose (42 ppm, Aging Cell 2014): female advantage (26% vs 23%). The sex×dose interaction reverses sign, contradicting any simple model of uniform male-biased rapamycin sensitivity.
- Transient rapamycin (3 months, 8 mg/kg/day i.p.) in C57BL/6 mice (eLife 2016) yields ~60% extension versus continuous feeding at 42 ppm in UM-HET3 yielding only 23–26% (Aging Cell 2014). The magnitude gap is enormous but uninterpretable: inbred strain, parenteral route, and acute dosing regimen are all confounded.
- RAD001 at sub-immunosuppressive doses improved influenza vaccine response ~20% in elderly humans (Sci Transl Med 2014), contradicting the canonical framing that mTOR inhibition suppresses immune function—a paradox not reconciled with the longevity-improvement hypothesis in any integrated analysis.

## Evidence gaps

- No study has directly tested dose-dependent sex differences within a single strain and protocol—both ITP doses used different publication cohorts without formal interaction testing.
- Transient/intermittent rapamycin dosing has never been tested in UM-HET3 heterogeneous-stock mice, preventing any conclusion about whether the 2016 eLife C57BL/6 results are protocol-driven or strain-driven.
- No published analysis maps the 14→42 ppm dose-response curve at finer granularity in either sex; only two dose levels exist in the ITP literature.
- The RAD001 human immune-enhancement finding has never been followed by a rapamycin lifespan or healthspan pilot in elderly humans at the sub-immunosuppressive doses shown to benefit immunity.
- Female-specific mechanisms (estrogen receptor–mTOR crosstalk, differential autophagy flux) at high vs. low rapamycin doses are entirely unexplored.

### #3 — opportunity 54 · `scoping-review`

- novelty 68 / evidence_strength 40 / reviewer_risk 50
- **Why publishable:** The RAD001 0.5 mg daily dose improving vaccine response by 20% in elderly humans (Sci Transl Med 2014) sits at the intersection of immune rejuvenation and longevity pharmacology, but no review has connected this clinical result to the murine lifespan dose-response to argue for a human healthspan trial at sub-immunosuppressive rapamycin doses.


## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- The sex×dose interaction is inferred from two separate ITP cohorts published 5 years apart—cohort effects, environmental drift, and laboratory-site variation (4 sites in ITP) could explain the apparent reversal without invoking biology.
- C57BL/6 is inbred and short-lived relative to UM-HET3; the 60% extension may reflect strain-specific pathology rescue (e.g., obesity, insulin resistance) rather than a universal dosing-regimen advantage.
- RAD001 (everolimus) has distinct pharmacokinetics from rapamycin (sirolimus); extrapolating immune-rejuvenation findings across compounds without head-to-head data is pharmacologically naïve.
- The 42 ppm ITP dose was tested at only one timepoint—without a full dose-response curve, the apparent nonlinearity could be artifactual.
- No lifespan data exist for sub-immunosuppressive rapamycin doses in any species, so the healthspan thesis relies entirely on extrapolation from the immune endpoint.

## Suggested next extractions

- Body weight and metabolic phenotyping data from ITP 14 ppm vs 42 ppm cohorts to test whether differential adiposity mediates the sex×dose interaction
- Rapamycin trough blood levels at 14 vs 42 ppm in male vs female UM-HET3 mice—if available in ITP supplementary data—to rule out pharmacokinetic sex differences
- Any published intermittent rapamycin dosing studies in UM-HET3 or other heterogeneous-stock mice (e.g., every-other-day, 3-month-on/3-month-off protocols)
- Dose-finding data from Mannick et al. 2014 RAD001 trial for rapamycin itself in elderly humans at 1 mg/day or lower
- Histopathology and tumor spectrum data from 42 ppm ITP cohorts by sex, to test whether cancer vs. non-cancer mortality drives the female advantage at high dose
- S6K1 and 4E-BP1 phosphorylation data (pharmacodynamic markers) from any rapamycin lifespan study broken out by sex
