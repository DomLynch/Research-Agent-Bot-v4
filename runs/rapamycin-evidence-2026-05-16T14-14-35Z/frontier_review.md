# Frontier review — rapamycin

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-16T14-14-35Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

Rapamycin's lifespan effects reveal a non-monotonic efficacy landscape where dosing kinetics—transient high-dose pulses versus chronic low-level exposure—appear more influential than cumulative mTOR inhibition, challenging conventional gerotherapeutic dosing assumptions.

## Already known — do not publish

- Rapamycin extends lifespan in mice
- Rapamycin inhibits mTORC1 signaling
- Chronic rapamycin treatment improves healthspan

## Tensions / contradictions

- Bitto 2016's transient high-dose rapamycin (8 mg/kg/day i.p.) induced ~60% remaining lifespan extension in middle-aged C57BL/6 mice, contrasting with Harrison 2009's chronic low-dose (14 ppm in feed) showing only 14% reduction in 90th-percentile mortality in female UM-HET3 mice.
- Miller 2014's dose-response data (42 ppm vs. 14 ppm) indicates greater lifespan extension at higher doses, but the relative efficacy compared to transient regimens remains unquantified across studies.

## Evidence gaps

- Effect of transient rapamycin treatment in female mice is not addressed in the evidence facts.
- Direct head-to-head comparison of transient versus chronic dosing regimens in isogenic mouse cohorts.
- Integration of healthspan metrics (e.g., cognitive function, frailty) alongside lifespan outcomes for different rapamycin dosing paradigms.
