## The lens

The evidence for urolithin A is fragmented across unrelated organ systems—melanogenesis in vitro, kidney injury in mice, and muscle strength in humans—without a unifying mechanistic insight, suggesting that the field is ripe for a meta-review to prioritize translational pathways over isolated effects.

## Already known — do not publish

- Urolithin A attenuates melanogenesis in B16 melanoma cells at 10 μM
- Urolithin A improves muscle strength by approximately 12% in middle-aged adults

## Tensions / contradictions

- The 63% mortality reduction in cisplatin-induced acute kidney injury mice using nanoparticle urolithin A (fact 135398) versus the 12% muscle strength improvement in humans (fact 165756) highlights a potential dose or formulation disconnect that undermines clinical relevance.
- In vitro melanogenesis inhibition at 10 μM (fact 85415) lacks corroboration in human skin studies, raising questions about bioavailability and translatability.

## Evidence gaps

- No human data on urolithin A's effects on kidney injury or skin pigmentation, limiting cross-disease therapeutic claims.
- Standardization of urolithin A doses and delivery systems (e.g., nanoparticles vs. supplements) across preclinical and clinical research is critically absent.
