# Frontier review — fisetin

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-27T09-50-11Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The single high-potency (>90%) antiviral fact against DENV-2/3 in macrophages positions fisetin not merely as an inhibitor but as a candidate for mechanistic studies on flavivirus entry modulation, particularly under antibody-dependent enhancement conditions—a nuanced angle absent from broad reviews. However, this evidence is too isolated for a standalone thesis, necessitating expanded data on pharmacokinetics, in vivo models, and comparative flavonoid studies.

## Already known — do not publish

- Fisetin inhibits dengue virus serotypes 2 and 3 infection in human macrophages with high efficacy

## Tensions / contradictions

- No direct tensions exist; the fact's high efficacy in vitro contrasts with the absence of any in vivo or clinical data, creating a translational gap.

## Evidence gaps

- Dose-response relationships and therapeutic indices for fisetin in dengue models
- Mechanistic studies on fisetin's interaction with the S2 domain of spike proteins (from hints) in viral entry
- Comparative efficacy against other flaviviruses like Zika or West Nile virus in similar macrophage lines

## Paper theses

_none_

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- The evidence relies on a single in vitro study; generalizability to human infection is speculative.
- Lack of data on fisetin's stability, bioavailability, and toxicity in vivo limits clinical relevance.

## Suggested next extractions

- In vivo murine models of dengue virus infection treated with fisetin at varying doses (e.g., 10-50 mg/kg)
- Binding affinity assays of fisetin to the S2 domain of spike proteins from dengue and other flaviviruses
- Combination studies of fisetin with existing antivirals or antibodies to assess synergy
- Pharmacokinetic profiling of fisetin in macrophage-rich tissues like liver and spleen
