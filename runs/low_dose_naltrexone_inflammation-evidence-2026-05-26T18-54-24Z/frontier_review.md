## The lens

The absence of cited evidence facts reveals a critical data void in low-dose naltrexone (LDN) research, preventing evidence-based thesis formulation; instead, the field relies on mechanistic speculation and small-scale studies without dose-response mapping or subgroup stratification.

## Already known — do not publish

- Naltrexone is an opioid receptor antagonist typically used at 50 mg/day for addiction
- Inflammation involves pro-inflammatory cytokines like TNF-alpha and IL-6
- Low-dose naltrexone (1.5-4.5 mg/day) is anecdotally used for autoimmune conditions like Crohn's disease

## Tensions / contradictions

- Conflicting mechanistic hypotheses: LDN may act via opioid receptor modulation vs. Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) antagonism, with no consensus from empirical studies
- Heterogeneous clinical outcomes across conditions (e.g., inconsistent efficacy in fibromyalgia vs. multiple sclerosis trials) lacking meta-analytic reconciliation

## Evidence gaps

- Dose optimization studies correlating naltrexone doses (e.g., 0.5-5 mg/day) with quantitative inflammatory biomarkers (e.g., CRP, IL-6) in defined subgroups
- Long-term safety and efficacy data beyond 12 months in chronic inflammatory diseases, particularly regarding immunomodulatory effects
- Investigation of LDN effects in underrepresented populations such as elderly patients or those with renal impairment

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- The lack of robust randomized controlled trials with adequate sample sizes undermines any thesis on LDN's anti-inflammatory efficacy
- Mechanistic claims are speculative without preclinical or clinical data directly linking low doses to specific pathways in human subjects
- Risk of publication bias due to reliance on anecdotal reports and small observational studies

## Suggested next extractions

- Primary clinical trials measuring LDN dose-response (e.g., 1 mg vs. 3 mg vs. 5 mg daily) with inflammatory endpoints in autoimmune cohorts
- Preclinical models assessing glial cell activation and cytokine profiles under varying naltrexone concentrations
- Systematic reviews of existing LDN studies to identify methodological weaknesses and gaps in outcome reporting
