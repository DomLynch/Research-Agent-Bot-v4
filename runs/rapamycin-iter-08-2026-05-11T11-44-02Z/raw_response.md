=== TITLE ===

Rapamycin and Murine Lifespan: Heterogeneity Across Sex, Strain, Dose, and Timing



=== ABSTRACT ===

How consistent is the effect of rapamycin on murine lifespan across variation in sex, genetic background, dose, and age at treatment initiation? Prior quantitative syntheses have each documented a positive pooled effect but reported divergent heterogeneity estimates, and neither jointly modeled all candidate moderators in a single framework [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis; CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis]. Narrative accounts have catalogued individual sources of heterogeneity without formal quantitative tests [CIT:arriola-apelo-2016-review|narrative-review], leaving the magnitude and direction of conditional effects across the joint moderator space unresolved.

The corpus will be assembled following PRISMA guidelines [CIT:page-2020-prisma|method-citation] through systematic database screening, yielding [N_SCREENED] records at the title-abstract stage, [N_ACCEPTED] after full-text inclusion assessment, and [K_STUDIES] independent effect sizes eligible for quantitative synthesis. Each study will be coded for genetic background, sex, drug formulation, dose, route of administration, treatment initiation age, and lifespan endpoint.

Planned analyses include multi-level mixed-effects meta-regression with restricted maximum-likelihood estimation to partition between-study variance. Moderator tests will evaluate whether effect magnitude varies by sex, strain (e.g., C57BL/6 versus genetically heterogeneous backgrounds), dose, route, and age at treatment start. A tension matrix will cross-tabulate moderator combinations to identify cells that remain sparsely populated in the existing evidence base [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis; CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis].

Pre-specified boundary conditions interrogate whether rapamycin's lifespan effect is conditional on sex-by-strain interactions [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin|primary-study; CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin|primary-study], dose-by-timing interactions [CIT:strong-2008-itp|primary-study], and whether findings from the Interventions Testing Program generalize to single-institution studies with smaller sample sizes [CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin|primary-study; CIT:bitto-2016-rapamycin|primary-study].

The planned analysis will identify moderator combinations that either strengthen or attenuate the lifespan effect, directing future primary studies toward the least-resolved cells of the tension matrix and informing translational decisions about human aging interventions [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus|clinical-trial; CIT:mannick-2018-rtb101|clinical-trial].



=== INTRODUCTION ===

The mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) signaling pathway occupies a central position in the biology of aging, integrating nutrient-sensing, growth-factor, and stress-response cues to regulate cell growth, autophagy, and proteostasis [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor|mechanism-review; CIT:saxton-2017-mtor|mechanism-review]. Rapamycin, a macrolide that allosterically inhibits mTOR complex 1, has emerged as the most widely replicated pharmacological intervention capable of extending murine lifespan [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin|primary-study; CIT:strong-2008-itp|primary-study]. Harrison et al. (2009) demonstrated that rapamycin administered late in life to genetically heterogeneous mice extended both median and maximum lifespan [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin|primary-study], and subsequent Interventions Testing Program (ITP) cohorts replicated the finding across multiple genetic backgrounds and both sexes [CIT:strong-2008-itp|primary-study; CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin|primary-study]. Individual laboratory studies have reported comparable lifespan extensions in various inbred strains and under alternative dosing regimens [CIT:bitto-2016-rapamycin|primary-study; CIT:miller-2011-rapamycin|primary-study]. These primary findings have generated a body of evidence of sufficient size for quantitative synthesis, yet the extent to which the rapamycin lifespan effect is moderated by sex, genetic background, dose, route, and age at treatment initiation has not been resolved to a degree that permits confident translational extrapolation [CIT:arriola-apelo-2016-review|narrative-review]. Mechanistic differences in mTOR pathway activity across tissues, sexes, and genetic backgrounds provide biological plausibility for such moderation [CIT:saxton-2017-mtor|mechanism-review; CIT:lamming-2013-mtor|mechanism-review], but empirical confirmation requires systematic quantitative evaluation across the available primary literature.

Two prior meta-analyses have addressed the rapamycin–lifespan relationship in rodent models and established the positive pooled effect as a robust finding [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis; CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis]. Swindell (2017) aggregated effect sizes from studies available through mid-2016, reporting a positive pooled estimate and identifying dose and sex as candidate moderators, though the analysis was constrained by the number of eligible studies and could not jointly model multiple covariates [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis]. Fok et al. (2023) extended the search window through 2022, confirmed the positive pooled effect, and explored a broader set of moderators; however, several moderator estimates carried wide confidence intervals due to sparse cell counts in crossed designs [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis]. Neither synthesis [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis; CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis] performed joint estimation of all primary moderators within a single multi-level model. Narrative reviews have catalogued biological and methodological sources of heterogeneity—including strain, sex, dose, treatment duration, and formulation—without conducting formal quantitative tests [CIT:arriola-apelo-2016-review|narrative-review]. Consequently, the magnitude and direction of conditional effects across the joint moderator space remain uncertain [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis; CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis], limiting both mechanistic inference and translational reasoning. The human clinical trials of everolimus [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus|clinical-trial] and RTB101 [CIT:mannick-2018-rtb101|clinical-trial] underscore the importance of resolving these moderating conditions, as dose and schedule decisions in human geroscience depend on which murine experimental conditions are most informative.

The present work positions itself as a methodological extension of the two prior quantitative syntheses [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis; CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis]. Three specific extensions are planned. First, the corpus will be updated to include primary studies published after the Fok et al. search date, incorporating recently completed ITP cohorts and single-institution studies [CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin|primary-study; CIT:bitto-2016-rapamycin|primary-study; CIT:miller-2011-rapamycin|primary-study]. Second, a multi-level mixed-effects model will jointly estimate the contributions of sex, strain (classified by genetic heterogeneity and specific inbred backgrounds), dosing regimen, route of administration, and age at treatment initiation to between-study heterogeneity. This joint specification addresses a limitation of prior moderator analyses [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis; CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis], which evaluated candidate moderators in isolation or pairwise combinations, leaving potential interaction effects unexamined. Third, a pre-specified tension matrix will cross-tabulate moderator levels to identify sparsely populated or unpopulated cells, quantifying structural gaps in the existing evidence base. The tension matrix will serve both as an analytical diagnostic—guiding model specification and interpretive caution—and as a roadmap for directing future primary research toward the most informative yet underrepresented experimental conditions.

The specific moderator hypotheses derive from patterns identified but incompletely resolved in prior work [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis; CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis]. Sex has been proposed as a moderator, with some ITP data suggesting a larger effect in females [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin|primary-study; CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin|primary-study], yet the generality of this pattern across non-ITP designs and alternative genetic backgrounds remains unclear [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis]. Genetic background constitutes a second candidate moderator: the ITP employs genetically heterogeneous mice to enhance external validity [CIT:strong-2008-itp|primary-study], whereas many single-laboratory studies use inbred strains such as C57BL/6, and the degree to which strain moderates effect magnitude has not been conclusively resolved [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis]. Treatment timing—whether rapamycin initiated early, mid-, or late in life produces equivalent lifespan effects—has been examined in limited direct comparisons [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin|primary-study; CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin|primary-study] but has not been synthesized across the full literature in a formal meta-regression framework. Dose–response relationships present an analogous gap: both prior meta-analyses identified dose as a candidate moderator but reached different conclusions about the direction of the relationship, likely owing to differences in operationalization and in the studies included [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis; CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis]. Route of administration (e.g., oral versus subcutaneous) and drug formulation (encapsulated versus dissolved) introduce additional sources of between-study variability [CIT:arriola-apelo-2016-review|narrative-review] that have not been formally tested as moderators in a meta-analytic framework.

The scope of this synthesis is restricted to murine lifespan as the primary endpoint, reflecting the dominance of mouse models in the rapamycin–aging literature and the comparability of lifespan as a cross-study metric. Non-lifespan outcomes—cardiac function, glucose metabolism, immune competence, tumor incidence—are excluded to maintain a tractable analytic frame. Interventions involving rapamycin analogs or derivatives in non-lifespan murine models are not included, nor are human clinical endpoints, though the trials of everolimus [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus|clinical-trial] and RTB101 [CIT:mannick-2018-rtb101|clinical-trial] are referenced for translational context. Mechanistic pathway analyses—downstream mTOR signaling nodes, autophagy markers, and cellular senescence readouts—fall outside the present scope but are noted where they bear on interpreting moderator patterns [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor|mechanism-review; CIT:saxton-2017-mtor|mechanism-review]. The analysis does not aim to adjudicate among competing biological explanations for rapamycin's lifespan effect; instead, it seeks to map the empirical conditions under which the effect is observed and to quantify residual heterogeneity conditional on measured moderators. By jointly modeling pre-specified moderators and constructing a tension matrix of underrepresented experimental conditions, this synthesis will extend prior work [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis; CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis] at a granularity that enables targeted predictions for future primary studies and informs translational reasoning about human aging interventions [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus|clinical-trial].
