# Frontier review — metformin

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-15T18-52-55Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The dissonance between metformin's robust anti-proliferative effects in rodent carcinogenesis models (e.g., 72% tumor reduction in NNK-treated A/J mice) and its inconsistent null findings in large human epidemiological cohorts (e.g., HR 0.90 for colorectal cancer) suggests that tissue-specific mTOR inhibition may be overridden by systemic factors like metabolic state or drug dosing, creating a translational gap worth dissecting through dose-stratified analyses.

## Already known — do not publish

- Metformin reduces HbA1c in type 2 diabetics with good adherence (fact 13)
- Metformin inhibits mTOR and decreases tumor burden in NNK-treated A/J mice (fact 1)
- Metformin has no association with colorectal cancer risk in some diabetic human cohorts (fact 8)

## Tensions / contradictions

- Fact 1 (72% tumor reduction in mice via intraperitoneal metformin) vs. fact 8 (no association with colorectal cancer in humans) highlights route and dose discrepancies.
- Fact 2 (metformin impaired lean mass gain during resistance training in older adults) vs. fact 21 (metformin plus exercise reduced inflammation in cancer survivors) implies context-dependent metabolic trade-offs.
- Fact 4 (metformin 250 mg/d reduced aberrant crypt foci in humans) vs. fact 17 (metformin 1000 mg twice daily for 12 weeks showed no effect on Hscore in obese adults) points to inconsistent efficacy in premalignant lesions.

## Evidence gaps

- Human trials evaluating metformin for lung cancer prevention in non-diabetic smokers, based on the A/J mouse model in fact 16.
- Long-term studies on metformin's effects on sarcopenia in aging populations beyond 14 weeks (fact 2).
- Dose-response relationships for metformin's anti-inflammatory effects in cancer survivors, as fact 23 shows null results for metformin alone but synergy with exercise.

## Paper theses

### #1 — opportunity 0 · `meta-analysis-standard`

**Thesis:** Metformin's Translational Failure in Colorectal Cancer: A Meta-Regression on Dose, Route, and Metabolic Baseline

- novelty 75 / evidence_strength 0 / reviewer_risk 70
- **Why publishable:** This meta-analysis would stratify by key variables (e.g., oral vs. intraperitoneal, diabetic vs. non-diabetic cohorts) to explain the null human findings in fact 8 against strong preclinical data (facts 1, 11), addressing a specific gap in translational oncology.


## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- High heterogeneity across studies in doses (250 mg/d to 150 mg/kg) and populations (rodents vs. humans) may confound meta-regression results.
- Potential confounding by diabetes status or concomitant medications in human studies like fact 8.
- Lack of mechanistic human data to support mTOR inhibition as the primary pathway in all contexts.

## Suggested next extractions

- Studies on metformin in KRAS-mutant lung cancer models or human trials with metformin in non-diabetic smokers.
- Long-term cohort data on metformin and muscle function in adults over 65.
- Randomized trials combining metformin with exercise for inflammatory biomarkers in cancer survivors, per facts 21-23.
