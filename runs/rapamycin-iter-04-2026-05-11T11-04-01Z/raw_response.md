=== TITLE ===

Heterogeneous Effects of Rapamycin on Longevity Across Strain, Sex, Dose, and Timing

=== ABSTRACT ===

Rapamycin, a mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) inhibitor, reliably extends lifespan in multiple mammalian models, yet the magnitude of this effect varies substantially across experimental conditions. Prior syntheses have established lifespan extension as a robust phenomenon in genetically heterogeneous mice [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin] and have catalogued variation across cohorts and protocols [CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin]. Existing meta-analyses have addressed circumscribed subsets of the evidence — focusing on Interventions Testing Program cohorts [CIT:avanesian-2010-meta] or on the broader mTOR pathway intervention landscape [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor] — but uncertainty persists regarding how dose magnitude, treatment initiation age, route of administration, and dosing schedule jointly moderate effect sizes. This synthesis addresses the question: to what extent do pre-specified experimental moderators account for between-study heterogeneity in rapamycin's effect on lifespan and age-related physiological outcomes in mammalian models?

Eligible studies must report survival endpoints or validated biomarkers of aging in mammalian models treated with rapamycin or rapalogs, with sufficient data for effect-size extraction and moderator coding.

The analysis will employ multi-level mixed-effects models to estimate aggregate effect magnitudes while partitioning variance across study-level and outcome-level random effects [MODEL:mixed-effects]. Moderator tests will examine the independent and interactive contributions of biological sex, genetic background, rapamycin dose, treatment initiation timing, and duration of exposure. A tension matrix will identify conditions under which the direction or magnitude of effects diverges. Sensitivity analyses will assess robustness to publication bias, outlier exclusion, and coding decisions.

The pre-specified boundary conditions include: does the lifespan effect differ between males and females, and does this sex-moderation depend on strain background? Does initiating treatment earlier in life produce larger effects than late-life initiation [MODERATOR_P:timing]? Do intermittent dosing regimens yield different effect magnitudes compared to continuous administration? How do route of administration and formulation interact with dose in shaping outcomes [MODERATOR_P:dose-by-route]?

The results will identify the most informative design configurations for a subsequent multi-site confirmatory study. If specific moderator combinations account for a substantial share of between-study variance, targeted replication under those conditions will provide the strongest test of the synthesis findings. If residual heterogeneity remains large, the findings will motivate mechanistic investigations to identify unmeasured sources of variation.

=== INTRODUCTION ===

The mTOR signaling pathway integrates nutrient, energy, and growth-factor cues to regulate cell growth, autophagy, and metabolism. Pharmacological inhibition of mTOR complex 1 (mTORC1) by rapamycin extends lifespan in yeast, worms, flies, and mice [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor], positioning this pathway as one of the most conserved modulators of aging across taxa. In 2009, the National Institute on Aging Interventions Testing Program (ITP) reported that rapamycin fed to genetically heterogeneous mice beginning at 270 days of age extended median lifespan by approximately 9 percent in females and 14 percent in males [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin]. This result was notable because it represented the first pharmacological intervention to extend lifespan in a mammalian model when initiated late in life, and it generated a sustained research program investigating dose, timing, sex, and strain dependencies [CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin]. Concurrent clinical interest emerged when Mannick et al. [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus] demonstrated that low-dose everolimus, a rapalog, improved immune function in elderly human volunteers, suggesting translational relevance beyond rodent models.

Several quantitative syntheses have aggregated subsets of this growing literature. Avanesian et al. [CIT:avanesian-2010-meta] conducted an early meta-analysis of rapamycin lifespan studies available at that time, confirming a positive aggregate effect but noting substantial between-study heterogeneity that the small number of included studies limited their ability to explain. Lamming et al. [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor] provided a narrative and semi-quantitative review of mTOR-pathway interventions, covering rapamycin, dietary restriction, and genetic models, and identified sex and strain as candidate moderators without formal interaction tests. More recent scoping reviews [CIT:arriolaapelo-2016-review; CIT:kennedy-2016-mtor] have synthesized mechanistic evidence — linking mTORC1 inhibition to autophagy, proteostasis, and senescence — while calling for systematic moderator analysis across the expanding empirical base. In parallel, meta-analyses of rapamycin in cancer [CIT:rapamycin-cancer-meta] and transplantation [CIT:rapamycin-transplant-meta] have addressed domain-specific clinical outcomes, but these syntheses do not examine aging-relevant endpoints or preclinical design moderators. Collectively, prior work establishes that rapamycin extends lifespan under a range of conditions but leaves unresolved how much of the observed heterogeneity is attributable to identifiable experimental features.

This uncertainty has practical consequences. The mTOR field faces a growing number of experimental configurations — varying in mouse strain (e.g., C57BL/6, HET3, BALB/c), sex, rapamycin dose (ranging from 1.5 to 42 mg/kg/day or parts per million equivalents), treatment initiation age (from early life to 20 months), dosing schedule (continuous versus intermittent), route (diet admixture versus gavage versus encapsulated formulation), and outcome measured (median lifespan, maximum lifespan, 90th-percentile survival, or composite healthspan indices). Without formal moderator analysis on a consolidated dataset, it is difficult to determine whether these factors account for the heterogeneity reported in individual studies [CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin] or whether unmeasured between-laboratory variation predominates. The present synthesis extends prior work in three respects. First, it consolidates effect sizes from the full span of published rapamycin lifespan studies through [DATE_CUTOFF], increasing statistical power for moderator detection relative to earlier meta-analyses that were constrained by a smaller evidence base. Second, it applies a multi-level mixed-effects framework [MODEL:multilevel-moderator] with pre-specified interaction terms for sex × strain × dose × timing, allowing simultaneous estimation of main effects and interactions rather than stratified subgroup comparisons. Third, it constructs a tension matrix that maps configurations in which moderator effects align versus diverge, providing a structured basis for designing confirmatory replication studies.

The planned analysis addresses five specific moderator questions. First, whether the lifespan effect of rapamycin is moderated by biological sex, as suggested by the original ITP report [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin] and by sex-dimorphic mTOR signaling observed in downstream pathway studies [CIT:sex-dimorphism-mtor]. Second, whether genetic background moderates the effect, given that most lifespan studies have used C57BL/6 or genetically heterogeneous (UM-HET3) stocks while other strains remain underrepresented [CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin]. Third, whether dose-response relationships are monotonic or non-linear, and whether dose-moderation depends on route and formulation. Fourth, whether treatment initiation timing — categorized as early life (weaning to 6 months), mid-life (6 to 12 months), or late life (>12 months) — moderates effect magnitude, as suggested by the finding that intermittent rapamycin treatment beginning at 20 months still extended lifespan [CIT:bitto-2016-rapamycin]. Fifth, whether continuous and intermittent dosing regimens produce comparable effects after accounting for cumulative exposure. These questions are pre-specified to limit researcher degrees of freedom during analysis.

The scope of this synthesis is limited to rapamycin and its close analogs (everolimus, temsirolimus) administered to mammalian models with survival or age-related phenotypic endpoints. The synthesis does not cover mTOR-pathway genetic manipulations (e.g., S6K1 knockouts, mTOR hypomorphic mice) or dietary restriction, both of which interact with the mTOR axis through partially distinct mechanisms [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor]. The synthesis does not address clinical outcomes in transplant or oncology populations, where competing risks and disease-specific pathways confound lifespan interpretation. By restricting the scope to pharmacological rapamycin/rapalog interventions with survival endpoints in mammalian models, the analysis achieves a coherent population, intervention, comparator, and outcome (PICO) framework while providing sufficient sample size for the planned moderator tests. The results will establish which design features most strongly shape the rapamycin-longevity association and will identify the conditions under which a confirmatory, pre-registered multi-site replication would provide the greatest evidential value.
