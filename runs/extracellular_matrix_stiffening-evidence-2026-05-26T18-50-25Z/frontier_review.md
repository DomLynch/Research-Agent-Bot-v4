## The lens

The convergence of proteomic aging clocks with biomechanical ECM stiffening represents an untested paradigm where matrix dynamics could serve as both a cause and consequence of systemic aging phenotypes, yet interdisciplinary evidence remains siloed.

## Already known — do not publish

- ECM stiffening increases with age in tissues like liver and lung.
- Fibroblasts in stiff matrices exhibit enhanced contractility and collagen deposition.

## Tensions / contradictions

- Proteomic age predictors (r=0.94) are validated in biobanks but ignore tissue-specific mechanical properties.
- In vitro glucose transitions in INS-1E cells reduce matrix superoxide, suggesting metabolic-ECM crosstalk absent in in vivo models.

## Evidence gaps

- Dose-response effects of matrix-modifying enzymes (e.g., LOX) on ECM stiffness in proteomic-phenotyped human tissues.
- Longitudinal studies correlating proteomic age acceleration with direct ECM modulus measurements in chronic diseases.
