## Beclin 1 as a bifunctional regulator of intestinal barrier integrity: scoping review of autophagy-independent endocytic roles

## Why this is surprising

Autophagy research uncovers a context-dependent therapeutic paradox: in intestinal epithelia, beclin 1 drives occludin endocytosis and barrier dysfunction independent of autophagy, while in cancer models, autophagy's cytoprotective role shifts to apoptosis after 48 hours of combinatorial treatment, highlighting temporal and mechanistic bifurcations that challenge uniform intervention strategies.

Known / obvious (do not republish): Autophagy is a catabolic process for cellular degradation; Beclin 1 is a key autophagy initiation protein; Autophagy modulation affects cancer cell survival

Real tension: In Caco-2 cells and mouse colon, beclin 1 activation increases occludin endocytosis and TJ permeability (facts 1, 12), but autophagy induction prevents this effect (fact 4), indicating a conflict between beclin 1's autophagy-dependent and independent roles.

## Evidence

- These findings show that beclin 1 plays a constitutive, autophagy-independent role in the regulation of intestinal TJ barrier function via endocytosis of occludin. **[1]** (American Journal of Physiology-Cell Physiology 2019)
- Perfusion of mouse colon with beclin 1 peptide caused an increase in colonic TJ permeability that was prevented by in vivo induction of autophagy. **[1]** (American Journal of Physiology-Cell Physiology 2019)
- Inhibition of autophagy by silencing of the key autophagy gene, beclin 1 or 3-MA, further increased the cytotoxicity of this combinatorial treatment, suggesting that autophagy plays a cytoprotective r **[1OR]** (Molecular Cancer Therapeutics 2011)
- beclin 1 siRNA transfection enhanced Caco-2 TJ barrier function **[1]** (American Journal of Physiology-Cell Physiology 2019)
