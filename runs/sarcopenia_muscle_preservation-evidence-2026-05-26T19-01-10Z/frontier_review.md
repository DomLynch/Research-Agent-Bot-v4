# Frontier review — sarcopenia_muscle_preservation

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-26T19-01-10Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

Sarcopenia prevalence is not a single number but a cascade of diagnostic components (low mass, low strength, low performance) whose relative frequency shifts dramatically by comorbidity — dialysis patients show 26.2% severe sarcopenia vs 3.0% in non-dialysis, while NAFLD progression correlates with skeletal muscle depletion from ~8-22% in controls to ~35-63% in NASH — yet most meta-analyses collapse these into a single pooled estimate, obscuring the mechanistic pathway from muscle quantity to muscle function to disability. The publishable framing is not 'prevalence varies' but rather 'the diagnostic cascade breaks at different points depending on disease context, meaning intervention targets must shift accordingly.'

## Already known — do not publish

- Sarcopenia prevalence in the elderly is 10-16% worldwide
- Resistance training improves grip strength, gait speed, and functional fitness
- Overall sarcopenia prevalence estimates using muscle mass criteria alone (~34%)
- Sarcopenia prevalence in CRC ranges 12-60%

## Tensions / contradictions

- Prevalence by muscle mass criteria alone (29.1%, paper 10.1002/jcsm.13425; 34%, paper 10.1002/jcsm.12600) is far higher than when strength and performance are included (severe sarcopenia 26.2% in dialysis), suggesting the EWGSOP2 algorithm's sequential staging suppresses actionable cases at the low-strength stage
- Sarcopenic obesity prevalence (10.8%, paper 10.1002/jcsm.13425) is far lower than low muscle mass alone (29.1%), yet metabolic syndrome literature links NAFLD progression directly to skeletal muscle depletion — the two phenotypes may represent distinct pathophysiological routes with different intervention windows
- Resistance training increased follistatin (p<0.05, effect size 0.81; paper 10.3390/ijerph18136762) yet no included study links this molecular marker to the clinical sarcopenia staging endpoints (strength, performance) that define diagnosis

## Evidence gaps

- No evidence fact addresses dose-response thresholds for resistance training in sarcopenic subgroups stratified by disease context (dialysis vs NAFLD vs cancer cachexia)
- The transition from low muscle mass to clinically significant low strength — the diagnostic breakpoint — has no mechanistic or epidemiological characterization in the available evidence pool
- Sarcopenia prevalence in early-stage NAFLD (pre-NASH) as a modifiable risk window is suggested by hints but lacks citable evidence facts

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- Without primary evidence facts (0 A-B-bound IDs), any thesis built from this pool relies on unverified alpha hints, making all citations non-reproducible against the deterministic extraction layer
- The framing of 'diagnostic cascade breaking at different points' is mechanistically plausible but requires within-patient longitudinal data on mass→strength→performance transitions, which none of the cross-sectional prevalence studies provide
- Collapsing across disease contexts (dialysis, NAFLD, CRC, esophageal cancer) without adjusting for measurement modality (DXA vs BIA vs CT) introduces heterogeneity that undermines any comparative prevalence claim

## Suggested next extractions

- Longitudinal studies tracking sarcopenia diagnostic component transitions (normal mass → low mass → low strength → low performance) stratified by chronic disease subtype
- Resistance training trials in dialysis-specific sarcopenia with follistatin/myostatin endpoints tied to EWGSOP2 staging criteria
- NAFLD-to-NASH transition studies with skeletal muscle mass as a time-varying covariate predicting fibrosis progression
- Dose-response meta-analyses of resistance training frequency (sessions/week) and intensity (%1RM) on DXA-measured appendicular lean mass in adults >65 with confirmed sarcopenia
- Sarcopenic obesity prevalence in metabolically unhealthy normal-weight individuals — the 'thin outside, fat inside' phenotype
