# Frontier review — longevity

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-15T19-38-00Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The coexistence of hormetic dose-response patterns across phylogenetically distant species—from GA3 in wasps to fibrates in C. elegans—suggests a universal stress-adaptation mechanism, while the stark contrast between strong genetic longevity signals (e.g., FOXO3 SNPs) and low population heritability in humans reveals a critical gap in understanding cohort-specific epigenetic modulation.

## Already known — do not publish

- Genetic factors influence lifespan variation in model organisms
- Environmental and dietary interventions can extend lifespan in short-lived species
- Heritability estimates for complex traits are context-dependent

## Tensions / contradictions

- In parasitoid wasps, GA3 increases longevity at 50-200 ppm but decreases at 2000 ppm (fact_id=60218), whereas in C. elegans, fibrates extend lifespan at 10 μM without reported high-dose harm (fact_id=60906), challenging the universality of hormetic dose thresholds across taxa.
- Heritability of human longevity is below 10% in historical cohorts (fact_id=2885), yet genetic enrichment in offspring reduces mortality by 30% (fact_id=10028), indicating that genetic signals may be masked or amplified by cohort-specific environmental or epigenetic factors.

## Evidence gaps

- Dose-response relationships for anti-aging compounds (e.g., oleuropein, fibrates) in mammalian models, particularly primates, where only correlative genetic data exist (fact_id=27050).
- The role of Nrf2-signaling regulators (Keap1, βTrCP) in longevity beyond rodent species (fact_id=3432, fact_id=3431), with no data on human or primate tissues.
- Epigenetic mechanisms (e.g., DNA methylation) underlying the low heritability of longevity in human cohorts, specifically in early 20th-century birth groups (fact_id=2885).

- novelty 90 / evidence_strength 50 / reviewer_risk 85

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- For the hormesis thesis: Pharmacokinetic differences across species may invalidate direct dose comparisons, and mechanisms like GA3 signaling in wasps may not translate to mammalian systems.
- For the heritability thesis: Low heritability in historical cohorts could reflect poor data quality or survivorship bias, and genetic enrichment studies may involve select families not representative of general populations.

## Suggested next extractions

- Dose-response data for Nrf2-pathway activators (e.g., sulforaphane) in primate cell cultures or mouse models with varying maximum lifespan.
- Epigenetic clocks and DNA methylation patterns in human cohorts with birth years from 1900-1950, correlated with longevity and genetic risk variants (e.g., FOXO3 SNPs from fact_id=5979).
