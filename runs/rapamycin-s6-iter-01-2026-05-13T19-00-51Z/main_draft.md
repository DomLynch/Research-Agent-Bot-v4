## Discussion

This scoping review investigates the murine lifespan response to rapamycin (sirolimus) intervention through an automated, pipeline-driven synthesis of the available corpus. The current iteration of the pipeline identifies [PACKET:sentinel_recall] sentinel studies from pre-existing meta-analyses [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:tyshkovskiy-2025-vertebrate-meta|prior-meta-analysis], with the contract-adjudicated corpus comprising [PACKET:study_selection] records. The planned primary analysis, specified for inverse-variance random-effects pooling, requires at least two contract-passing effects within a single metric family. The current corpus yields [PACKET:primary_effect] such effects for lifespan extension, a sample size [K_STUDIES] too small to support a quantitative synthesis. Therefore, this review cannot yet provide a pooled estimate of rapamycin's effect on murine lifespan. The evidence base currently documents the intervention but does not permit a statistical aggregation of its magnitude.

Prior quantitative syntheses have provided pooled estimates of rapamycin's effect on mouse lifespan [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis], with the latter including both rapamycin and other interventions. The present scoping review's protocol is designed to extend this work by applying a stringent, automated evidence-contract gate to standardize inclusion and a strict per-quote audit for alignment with the declared study system. This approach aims to refine the input corpus for future meta-analysis by ensuring extracted effects derive from primary data meeting a priori relevance criteria. Alignment with prior effect directions is anticipated [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis], but any divergence in effect magnitude would be interpretable only upon accrual of a larger contract-passing corpus.

The mechanistic rationale for rapamycin's lifespan effects centers on its inhibition of the mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1), a key nutrient-sensing pathway implicated in aging [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor|mechanism-review]. This inhibition modulates anabolic processes, autophagy, and cellular senescence [CIT:saxton-2017-mtor|mechanism-review]. The discussion herein treats these mechanisms as established biological context, not as a finding derived from the current corpus. The planned moderator analysis, designed to partition variance by factors like genetic background, is deferred until the corpus accrues sufficient effects.

Boundary conditions that may moderate rapamycin's effect—including murine strain, sex, dose regimen, timing of intervention onset, route of administration, and specific pathogen-free versus conventional housing status—are currently underdetermined in this corpus. The pipeline is specified to test these moderators via mixed-effects meta-regression, but execution is contingent on [K_STUDIES] sufficient for stable estimation. Translational considerations are informed by clinical trials of the rapalog everolimus in older adults [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus|clinical-trial] and the catalytic site mTOR inhibitor RTB101 [CIT:mannick-2018-rtb101|clinical-trial]. It is critical to note that these interventions are classified as translational-only and are explicitly excluded from the primary pooled analysis of rapamycin/sirolimus. Their inclusion in the discussion provides context on age-related immune and physiological endpoints in humans, not on murine lifespan.

## Limitations

This review is constrained by a small contract-passing corpus, yielding [PACKET:primary_effect] effect(s) for the primary lifespan endpoint, which precludes a pooled quantitative estimate. The sentinel-recall rate of [PACKET:sentinel_recall] indicates the automated retrieval captured [N_SCREENED] of the sentinel studies from prior meta-analyses [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis]; those not retrieved represent a specific gap in the automated sweep. The pipeline's metric-family discipline—pooling 90th-percentile ratios and median ratios separately, without cross-family inference—conserves statistical rigour but further segments an already small available effect set. Risk-of-bias adjudication was performed by the automated LLM judge against the scoping review's eligibility criteria, not by domain experts applying a formal appraisal framework like SYRCLE's [CIT:hooijmans-2014-syrcle|method-citation], which is the appropriate method-citation anchor for murine intervention studies; this remains a designed-for capability deferred to future iterations. Manual full-text injection was required for [PACKET:study_selection] records, a process of documented source recovery, not an override of eligibility rules.

## Conclusion

The current automated, contract-adjudicated corpus contains an insufficient number of effects ([PACKET:primary_effect]) to support a pooled quantitative estimate of rapamycin's effect on murine lifespan. This conclusion would change with the incorporation of additional contract-passing primary studies that survive the evidence-contract and per-quote audit gates, thereby increasing [K_STUDIES] within a single metric family to the pipeline's pooling threshold. The interpretive frame for this work positions it as a rigorously specified scoping review whose output defines the present evidence landscape and whose architecture is designed to generate a meta-analytic pool once a sufficient corpus is accrued.
