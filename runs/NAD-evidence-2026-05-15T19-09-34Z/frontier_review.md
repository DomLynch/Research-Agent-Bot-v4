# Frontier review — NAD

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-15T19-09-34Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The divergence in NAD+ responses between sex-stratified healthy cohorts and metabolically compromised populations reveals a critical oversight in supplementation trials, where baseline redox states and disease contexts dictate precursor efficacy more than dosage alone.

## Already known — do not publish

- NAD+ is a central coenzyme in cellular metabolism
- Alcohol-related liver disease reduces hepatic NAD+ concentrations
- NAD precursor supplementation can elevate tissue NAD+ levels in animal models

## Tensions / contradictions

- Fact 1 reports women have higher plasma NAD+/NADH ratios than men (1.33 vs 1.09), yet Fact 7 shows no metabolic impact from NR in young men, suggesting sex-specific baseline redox states may influence supplementation outcomes.
- Fact 16 demonstrates a 14% decrease in NAMPT protein with NR in obese, insulin-resistant men, while Fact 23 indicates NR in a combination therapy shortens COVID-19 recovery, highlighting context-dependent effects where metabolic syndrome alters NAD pathway responsiveness.
- Microbial engineering studies (Fact 6, 17) leverage NAD+ cofactor recycling for metabolite production yields up to 67%, but human liver disease data (Fact 12, 13) show NAD+ depression correlates with pathology severity, exposing a translational gap between biotechnological exploitation and therapeutic modulation.

## Evidence gaps

- No randomized controlled trials of NAD+ precursors (NR, NMN) specifically in women, despite sex differences in NAD metabolism (Fact 1).
- Absence of direct comparative studies on NR vs. NMN dosing in insulin-resistant human populations, where Fact 16 suggests altered Nampt expression.
- Underexplored therapeutic targeting of NAD+-associated enzymes (e.g., Nampt in ovarian cancer, Fact 10; NQO1 in pancreatic cancer, Fact 15) in clinical oncology settings.
- Lack of data on long-term NAD+ precursor supplementation (beyond 12 weeks, Fact 16) in age-related metabolic decline, with hippocampal NAD+ depletion noted in diabetic rats (Fact 14).

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- Plasma NAD+/NADH ratios (Fact 1) may not reflect tissue-specific NAD+ pools, limiting direct translation to supplementation protocols.
- The combination therapy in COVID-19 studies (Fact 23, 24) includes multiple agents (l-serine, N-acetyl-l-cysteine, NR, l-carnitine), confounding attribution of effects solely to NR.
- Microbial NAD engineering (Fact 6, 17) operates in simplified systems, and human physiological complexity may preclude direct analogies for therapeutic design.

## Suggested next extractions

- Clinical trials on NMN or NR in postmenopausal women with obesity, measuring both plasma NAD metabolome and insulin sensitivity.
- Longitudinal studies in non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) patients assessing NAD+ precursor effects on liver histology and NAD+ concentrations, using Hepatology Communications (2020) as a benchmark.
- Mechanistic analyses of Nampt expression modulation in ovarian serous adenocarcinoma (Fact 10) to inform cancer adjuvant therapies.
- Dose-optimization trials of NR in diabetic patients, monitoring hippocampal function via neuroimaging, inspired by rat model data (Fact 14).
