## Discussion

This scoping review assessed the available evidence on rapamycin’s effect on murine lifespan, a question central to geroscience. The automated pipeline identified a small corpus of contract-passing primary studies ([PACKET:primary_pool_composition]). With a k value below the threshold for statistical pooling, the synthesis remains narrative. The corpus currently provides qualitative support for a lifespan-extending effect of rapamycin, aligning directionally with prior syntheses [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:tyshkovskiy-2025-vertebrate-meta|prior-meta-analysis]. However, these earlier meta-analyses, which quantified pooled percent-change estimates, cannot be directly replicated or refined by the present work due to the insufficient number of studies passing the evidence-contract gate ([PACKET:primary_effect]). The divergence lies in precision, not direction: prior estimates of effect magnitude are based on a larger, aggregated evidence base.

The mechanistic rationale for rapamycin’s potential anti-aging action is well-established in the literature, centering on its inhibition of the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor|mechanism-review] [CIT:saxton-2017-mtor|mechanism-review]. mTOR integrates nutrient and growth factor signals to regulate cellular growth, autophagy, and metabolism. Chronic, low-dose rapamycin treatment may induce a pro-longevity metabolic state partly by enhancing autophagic clearance of damaged cellular components. This review does not present new mechanistic data but situates the lifespan outcome within this known biological framework.

Critical boundary conditions that moderate the lifespan effect—including murine genetic background, sex, administered dose, treatment timing (lifespan-long vs. late-life), route of administration, and pathogen-free facility status—remain underdetermined by the current corpus. The small study count precludes a robust investigation of these moderators through meta-regression, a designed-for capability of the pipeline deferred until k is sufficient. Strain and sex differences, suggested in some primary reports [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin|primary-study] [CIT:strong-2008-itp|primary-study], warrant systematic quantification in a larger evidence pool.

Translational considerations are informed by clinical trials of the related compounds everolimus and RTB101, which demonstrated improved immune function in older human adults [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus|clinical-trial] [CIT:mannick-2018-rtb101|clinical-trial]. It is essential to note that these translational interventions are excluded from the primary analysis corpus; they are retained only for contextual discussion. The primary corpus is restricted to murine rapamycin studies. Therefore, while translational interest exists, direct extrapolation of effect magnitude from mice to humans is not supported by this synthesis. The pipeline’s sentinel-recall gate ([PACKET:sentinel_recall]) also highlights that not all seminal studies from prior reviews could be retrieved or met the stringent evidence-contract criteria, indicating an area for corpus enrichment.

## Limitations

This analysis is constrained by a small primary corpus of k studies ([PACKET:primary_effect]), which limits inference to narrative synthesis and precludes quantitative pooling and moderator analysis. The pipeline’s strict evidence-contract gate and A-core audit, while enhancing specificity, likely contributed to this small k by excluding studies with insufficient or misaligned evidence quotes. The sentinel-recall analysis ([PACKET:sentinel_recall]) indicates a gap between the pipeline’s automated retrieval and all potentially relevant studies identified in prior work. Furthermore, metric-family discipline is enforced strictly; studies reporting distinct summary statistics (e.g., 90th-percentile lifespan vs. median lifespan) are not pooled, preventing cross-family inference. Risk-of-bias adjudication is automated without explicit human arbitration per domain-specific frameworks, a capability deferred for future implementation. Finally, the process included manual full-text injection for sentinels the automated sweep could not reach ([PACKET:study_selection]), representing a documented source recovery effort, not an eligibility override. The key missing datum is a statistically powerful effect estimate with quantified heterogeneity, which requires a larger pool of contract-compliant studies.

## Conclusion

The current corpus, assessed through a scoping-review pipeline, provides qualitative support for rapamycin extending murine lifespan but is insufficiently powered for a pooled quantitative estimate ([PACKET:primary_effect]). This conclusion would be strengthened by the incorporation of more primary studies that meet the evidence-contract standard, particularly those that can repair the identified sentinel-recall gaps. The work underscores the transition from narrative affirmation of an effect to precise, moderated quantification as the next critical step in synthesizing this evidence base.
