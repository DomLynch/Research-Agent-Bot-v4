## The lens

The coexistence of mTOR deficiency-induced immune mortality and mTOR-mediated tumor suppression highlights a paradoxical therapeutic dilemma where modulation must navigate between immunocompromise and oncogenic inhibition, a nuance often overlooked in monotherapeutic approaches.

## Already known — do not publish

- mTOR is a central regulator of cell growth and metabolism
- mTOR inhibitors like rapamycin are used in cancer and autoimmune diseases

## Tensions / contradictions

- Hypomorphic mTOR mice with 40% mortality (fact_id=15567) versus metformin's 72% tumor reduction via mTOR inhibition (fact_id=2848) in disparate models, suggesting context-dependent mTOR roles that complicate therapeutic strategies.

## Evidence gaps

- Dose-dependent effects of mTOR modulation on immune function in cancer models, as hinted by RAD001's vaccine response improvement
- Integration of mTOR mutation profiles (e.g., 33 hyperactivating mutations) with immune outcomes in clinical cohorts

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- The analysis rests on only two preclinical studies with different endpoints (survival vs. tumor burden), limiting direct comparability and mechanistic insight.
- Lack of human data or dose-response details reduces the translational relevance of the proposed evidence gap.

## Suggested next extractions
