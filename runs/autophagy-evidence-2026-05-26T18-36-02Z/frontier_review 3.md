## Already known — do not publish

- Autophagy is a conserved cellular degradation process
- Autophagy involves autophagosome formation and lysosomal fusion
- Autophagy is regulated by key sensors like mTOR and AMPK
- Autophagy dysregulation is linked to diseases such as cancer and neurodegeneration

## Tensions / contradictions

- Conflicting roles of autophagy in cancer: e.g., HIF-1α-induced autophagy promotes hepatocellular carcinoma malignancy, while berberine-induced autophagy suppresses gastric cancer growth
- Context-dependent outcomes in testicular damage: autophagy protects against CuSO4-induced damage but can mediate ferroptosis when inhibited
- Alcohol impairs autophagy flux in liver disease, yet autophagy activation in Schwann cells promotes pancreatic cancer nerve invasion

## Evidence gaps

- Specific dose-response and timing of autophagy modulators (e.g., ATO in atherosclerosis) in different animal models
- Comparative analysis of autophagy induction via AMPK-mTOR pathway versus PI3K/mTOR inhibitors across disease subtypes
- Role of autophagy in immune evasion through MHC-I degradation in pancreatic cancer microenvironment
