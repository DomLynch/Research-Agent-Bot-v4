# Scoping Review of Rapamycin Effects on Lifespan in Mice Across Diverse Experimental Conditions

## Abstract

To what extent does the effect of rapamycin on lifespan in mice vary across different dosing regimens, administration timings, and genetic backgrounds? While prior syntheses have identified general lifespan extension [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis], uncertainty remains regarding the precise interaction between dose-response curves and the age of treatment initiation. This scoping review is implemented through a reproducible evidence-contract pipeline to map the distribution of these effects.

The pipeline performs a multi-source retrieval sweep, with eligibility adjudicated by a single LLM judge against deterministic rules. The resulting corpus will be characterized by [N_SCREENED] records screened and [N_ACCEPTED] records accepted, yielding [K_STUDIES] unique studies. The planned analysis utilizes a narrative synthesis of extracted effects, as the current pipeline design defers quantitative pooling until a sufficient number of contract-passing effects per metric family are accrued.

The review is designed to interrogate whether the magnitude of lifespan extension is modified by the sex of the mice, the specific mouse strain, the dosage of rapamycin or sirolimus, and the timing of the first dose. Specifically, the analysis will map how these variables correlate with reported lifespan increases. The results are designed to identify gaps in the current evidence base, which will inform the design of future longitudinal studies to determine if specific dosing windows are required to maximize lifespan extension without inducing adverse metabolic phenotypes.

## Introduction

The modulation of the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway represents a primary axis for the pharmacological extension of lifespan in murine models [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor|mechanism-review] [CIT:saxton-2017-mtor|mechanism-review]. Rapamycin and its derivatives, by inhibiting mTOR complex 1 (mTORC1), simulate a state of nutrient scarcity that triggers autophagy and reduces protein synthesis, thereby delaying the onset of age-related physiological decline [CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin|primary-study]. The significance of this research question lies in the high variability of observed outcomes across different experimental cohorts, where the magnitude of lifespan extension often fluctuates based on the specific parameters of the intervention [CIT:bitto-2016-rapamycin|primary-study] [CIT:miller-2011-rapamycin|primary-study]. Understanding the boundary conditions of these effects is essential for translating murine findings into potential therapeutic strategies for human longevity.

The prior synthesis landscape has established a foundation for understanding these effects, yet specific gaps persist. For instance, the meta-analysis by Swindell et al. (2017) [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis] provided an early quantitative aggregation of rapamycin's impact on lifespan, while more recent work by Fok et al. (2023) [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis] further refined the estimated effect sizes across various mouse models. Additionally, Tyshkovskiy (2025) [CIT:tyshkovskiy-2025-vertebrate-meta|prior-meta-analysis] has attempted to broaden the scope of these syntheses across different vertebrate taxa. Despite these contributions, prior syntheses have not provided a fully powered, pre-specified joint moderator framework that simultaneously accounts for the interaction between dose, timing of initiation, and genetic strain; the present work is designed to extend these findings by mapping these specific intersections within a scoping review framework. Narrative reviews, such as the work by Arriola and Apelo (2016) [CIT:arriola-apelo-2016-review|narrative-review], have highlighted the qualitative importance of mTOR inhibition but lack the structured evidence-contract gating required to minimize extraction bias.

The contribution of the present paper is a systematic stress-test of the existing evidence base using a reproducible pipeline to ensure that only records meeting strict evidence-contract criteria—including a minimum character count and at least two non-title evidence quotes—are included. This approach refines the landscape by distinguishing between primary interventions (rapamycin and sirolimus) and translational-only interventions such as everolimus or RTB101 [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus|clinical-trial] [CIT:mannick-2018-rtb101|clinical-trial], which are retained for sensitivity analysis but excluded from the primary lifespan mapping. By applying a strict A-core per-quote audit, this work ensures that the reported effects are directly tied to the declared study system and endpoints, reducing the noise often found in broader narrative summaries [CIT:strong-2008-itp|primary-study] [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin|primary-study].

The scope of this review is explicitly limited to the effect of rapamycin and sirolimus on the lifespan of mice. To maintain scope consistency, the analysis excludes other rodent species, such as rats, and other mammalian vertebrates. A critical non-goal of this work is the execution of a formal meta-analysis or the quantification of heterogeneity; such capabilities are designed-for features of the pipeline that are deferred until the corpus accrues a larger number of contract-passing effects per metric family. Furthermore, this work does not perform per-domain expert risk-of-bias adjudication using a specific framework, as such analysis is deferred in favor of a narrative synthesis of the available evidence. The present iteration also defers the use of publication-bias regressions, such as funnel-plot inspection [CIT:egger-1997-funnel|method-citation] or trim-and-fill methods, as these are deferred until the sample size [K_STUDIES] reaches the required threshold for statistical validity.

The necessity of this refined mapping is underscored by the observation that rapamycin's effects may be non-linear and highly dependent on the age of the animal at the start of treatment [CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin|primary-study]. While some studies suggest that late-life intervention can still yield benefits, the magnitude of this effect is often lower than that observed in interventions starting in early adulthood [CIT:bitto-2016-rapamycin|primary-study]. By systematically mapping these timing variables alongside dose-response data, this review is designed to clarify whether there is a "window of opportunity" for mTOR inhibition that maximizes lifespan extension while minimizing the potential for adverse effects on glucose metabolism or immune function [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor|mechanism-review]. This structured approach allows for a more granular understanding of the evidence than is provided by traditional systematic reviews that may overlook the nuance of dosing schedules [CIT:page-2020-prisma|method-citation].

Ultimately, this work seeks to provide a high-fidelity map of the murine rapamycin landscape, serving as a prerequisite for future quantitative syntheses. By adhering to a strict evidence-contract, the review avoids the common pitfalls of over-generalization and provides a transparent account of the available data [CIT:percie-du-sert-2020-arrive|method-citation]. The resulting synthesis will allow researchers to identify which specific mouse strains are most responsive to rapamycin and whether the observed lifespan extensions are consistent across both sexes, thereby refining the parameters for future longevity research.
