# Frontier review — exercise

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-26T19-53-42Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

Exercise's therapeutic value is not a monolith but a condition-specific modifier: it demonstrates pharmaceutical-comparable efficacy in stroke prevention yet delivers inconsistent quality-of-life benefits post-cardiac valve surgery, pointing to hidden mechanistic divergences that demand precision prescription.

## Already known — do not publish

- Exercise reduces mortality in coronary heart disease
- Exercise improves bone density
- Exercise reduces cancer recurrence

## Tensions / contradictions

- Exercise shows dramatic odds ratios versus anticoagulants (0.09) and antiplatelets (0.10) in stroke (facts 149217, 149218), but its mortality benefit in coronary heart disease is marginal (RR 0.87, fact 74368), questioning differential potency across cardiovascular conditions.
- Exercise-based rehabilitation yields clear mortality reductions in coronary heart disease (fact 74368) but fails to significantly improve mortality or quality of life in heart valve surgery patients (facts 153892, 153897, 153894), highlighting a critical gap in post-surgical rehab guidelines.

## Evidence gaps

- Exercise effects in heart valve surgery are limited to two trials with 131-150 participants and wide confidence intervals, indicating a severe evidence deficit for this growing patient population.
- Dose-response relationships for exercise modality (e.g., impact vs. aerobic), intensity, and timing relative to surgery or disease onset are unspecified across the evidence pool.

## Paper theses

### #1 — opportunity 69 · `scoping-review`

**Thesis:** Cardiac Condition-Specific Efficacy of Exercise Rehabilitation: A Comparative Evidence Synthesis

- novelty 75 / evidence_strength 65 / reviewer_risk 70
- **Why publishable:** By systematically comparing exercise's mortality benefits in stable coronary heart disease with its ambiguous effects on quality of life post-valve surgery, this review exposes a critical guideline oversight and prioritizes research on tailored exercise protocols for distinct cardiac pathologies.


## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- The included populations (post-MI vs. post-valve surgery) are too clinically heterogeneous for direct comparison, risking misleading conclusions.
- Outcome measures differ fundamentally (all-cause mortality vs. SF-36 scores), preventing a coherent meta-analytic synthesis.

## Suggested next extractions

- Specific exercise types (e.g., high-intensity interval training vs. resistance) in valve surgery patients
- Long-term functional outcomes (>1 year) post-cardiac rehabilitation
- Subgroup analysis by age, sex, and ejection fraction in heart failure patients
