# Frontier review — creatine

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-14T19-25-30Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The co-occurrence of elevated urinary creatine riboside in adrenocortical carcinoma (ACC) and higher brain creatine levels linked to cognitive impairment suggests a shared, non-organ-specific metabolic dysregulation, while methodological sensitivities in neuroimaging and animal models hint at underappreciated variabilities in creatine's diagnostic utility.

## Already known — do not publish

- Creatine supplementation enhances exercise performance and muscle mass
- Creatine is a key substrate for ATP regeneration in high-energy tissues

## Tensions / contradictions

- The 2.1-fold increase in urinary creatine riboside in ACC (Clinical Cancer Research, 2017) contrasts with the <5% detectable threshold for brain creatine changes via NMR (NMR in Biomedicine, 2015), indicating potential over- or underestimation in biomarker applications across different biological compartments.
- Higher creatine levels associated with cognitive decline (Brain, 2022) may conflict with neuroprotective effects proposed in supplementation studies, though direct comparative data are absent.

## Evidence gaps

- No studies have examined creatine riboside levels in other adenocarcinomas (e.g., prostate, breast) or in precancerous lesions to assess specificity.
- The impact of niacin deficiency on creatine metabolism beyond the intestinal model (Aquaculture Nutrition, 2015) in humans or other organs remains unexplored, limiting translational insights.

## Paper theses

### #1 — opportunity 45 · `scoping-review`

**Thesis:** Creatine as a pleiotropic biomarker: integrating evidence from ACC, cognitive decline, and niacin deficiency models

- novelty 80 / evidence_strength 40 / reviewer_risk 70
- **Why publishable:** Synthesizes scattered findings across oncology, neurology, and nutrition to highlight creatine's role beyond energy metabolism, proposing a novel framework for cross-disease biomarker validation that addresses current literature fragmentation.


## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- Heterogeneity in study populations (e.g., ACC patients vs. cognitive impairment cohorts) and methodologies (urinary assays vs. NMR vs. animal models) precludes valid comparisons and risks overinterpretation.
- The lack of mechanistic or interventional studies linking creatine riboside in ACC to cognitive outcomes undermines causal claims, leaving the proposed dysregulation speculative.

## Suggested next extractions

- Prospective cohorts measuring urinary creatine riboside in other cancer types with neurological symptoms, such as glioblastoma.
- Human studies on niacin deficiency's effect on muscle and brain creatine kinase activity, using controlled dietary interventions.
