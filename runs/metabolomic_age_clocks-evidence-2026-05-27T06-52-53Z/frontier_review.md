# Frontier review — metabolomic_age_clocks

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-27T06-52-53Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The absence of dedicated metabolomic age clock datasets forces a strategic pivot: leveraging the robust predictive accuracy (r > 0.92) and disease associations of proteomic clocks in biobanks like China (n=3,977) and Finland (n=1,990) as a scaffold to design metabolomic equivalents. This cross-omics framing could reveal whether metabolomic signatures capture unique aging dimensions missed by proteins or epigenetics, especially in multimorbidity.

## Already known — do not publish

- Metabolomic age clocks can estimate chronological age
- Biological age acceleration is linked to chronic disease risk
- Epigenetic and proteomic clocks show high correlation with age (r > 0.8)

## Tensions / contradictions

- Proteomic age clock accuracy differs subtly between ethnic cohorts (Pearson r=0.92 in Chinese vs. 0.94 in Finnish biobanks), suggesting population-specific calibration needs
- Epigenetic clocks like GrimAge show deceleration in severe COVID-19 (beta = -0.24), contrasting with typical age acceleration, indicating context-dependent clock behavior

## Evidence gaps

- Metabolomic aging signatures in East Asian vs. European populations, replicating proteomic biobank comparisons
- Dose-response of environmental exposures (e.g., 0.03 mg/kg·bw·d) on metabolomic age, extending from epigenetic clock findings
- Metabolomic clock validation in specific subgroups like parous women, where gravidity accelerates epigenetic aging (n=331)
