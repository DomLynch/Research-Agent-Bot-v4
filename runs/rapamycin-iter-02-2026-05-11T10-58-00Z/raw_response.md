=== TITLE ===

Systematic Synthesis of Rapamycin Effects on Longevity Across Species, Doses, and Treatment Timing

=== ABSTRACT ===

Rapamycin inhibits mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) signaling and has emerged as one of the most replicated pharmacological interventions extending lifespan in model organisms. Prior meta-analytic work has established a positive overall effect of rapamycin on murine lifespan [CIT:harrison-2009-itp] and has catalogued heterogeneity across experimental paradigms [CIT:bitto-2016-midlife]; yet the magnitude, direction, and determinants of variation across species, dosing regimens, age-of-initiation, sex, and genetic background remain incompletely resolved. The present work addresses this gap through a pre-registered, multi-species systematic synthesis designed to partition observed heterogeneity along pre-specified biological and methodological dimensions.

A comprehensive literature search will be conducted across major biomedical databases, with screening counts reported as structured placeholders: [N_SCREENED] records identified, [N_ACCEPTED] meeting inclusion criteria, yielding [K_STUDIES] independent effect sizes. Eligible studies include any controlled experiment administering rapamycin (or a close structural analog) to a living organism with survival or lifespan as a primary endpoint. The analytic plan centers on multilevel mixed-effects meta-regression with crossed random effects for study and species [MODEL:mixed-effects-crossed]. Planned moderator tests include dose [MODERATOR_P:drug-dose], treatment initiation age [MODERATOR_P:onset-age], biological sex [MODERATOR_P:sex], genetic strain [MODERATOR_P:strain], and route of administration [MODERATOR_P:route]. A tension matrix will be specified to evaluate whether moderator effects are robust to model specification changes and outlier exclusion [MODEL:tension-matrix].

Pre-specified boundary conditions interrogate whether effects differ: (a) between short-lived and long-lived strains, (b) between chronic and intermittent dosing, (c) between early-life and late-life onset, and (d) across sex within genetically homogeneous cohorts. These questions are framed as hypotheses, not conclusions, because the planned analysis has not yet been executed. The results will determine whether rapamycin's longevity effect generalizes beyond canonical mouse models and whether particular experimental configurations account for disproportionate heterogeneity. Findings that survive robustness checks would motivate targeted replication studies; those that do not would direct resources toward underpowered moderator cells requiring additional primary data.

=== INTRODUCTION ===

The mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway integrates nutrient, growth factor, and stress signals to regulate cellular growth, autophagy, and metabolic homeorhesis. Pharmacological inhibition of mTOR complex 1 (mTORC1) by rapamycin (sirolimus) has been demonstrated to extend lifespan in multiple organisms, including yeast [CIT:kappe-yeast-rapamycin], worms [CIT:robida-stan-rapamycin], flies [CIT:bjedov-drosophila-rapamycin], and mice [CIT:harrison-2009-itp], positioning mTOR as a central node in the biology of aging. The translation of these findings toward human healthspan has generated substantial clinical interest [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus], yet the conditions under which rapamycin reliably produces longevity benefits—and the sources of inconsistency across studies—remain active areas of investigation.

The foundational mouse result reported by Harrison et al. [CIT:harrison-2009-itp], conducted as part of the Interventions Testing Program (ITP), showed that rapamycin administered at 14 mg/kg in feed beginning at 270 days of age extended median lifespan in both sexes of genetically heterogeneous UM-HET3 mice. This result was later extended by studies examining earlier-onset treatment [CIT:bitto-2016-midlife], dose-response relationships [CIT:arriola-apelo-2016-mTOR-review], and intermittent dosing schedules [CIT:arriola-apelo-2016-intermittent]. Synthesis efforts have estimated the pooled effect across murine studies [CIT:nadon-meta-analysis-rapamycin], and narrative reviews have catalogued the breadth of species in which mTOR inhibition produces some longevity signal [CIT:lamming-2013-mTOR-aging]. A prior quantitative synthesis identified a statistically positive overall effect but flagged substantial heterogeneity across study designs and reported outcomes [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin].

Despite these advances, several specific gaps motivate the present work. First, existing meta-analyses have focused predominantly on Mus musculus; the extent to which effect sizes converge or diverge across invertebrate and other mammalian models has not been subjected to formal multi-species quantitative comparison. Second, moderator analyses in prior syntheses were constrained by small cell sizes for key biological variables—particularly sex × strain × dose × initiation-age combinations—limiting statistical power to detect interaction effects [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin]. Third, publication bias diagnostics and sensitivity analyses (e.g., leave-one-out, robust variance estimation) have been applied inconsistently across prior work, leaving the robustness of the aggregate estimate uncertain [CIT:harrer-2021-meta-analysis-guide]. Fourth, clinical translation studies using rapamycin analogs such as everolimus [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus] and RTB101 [CIT:mannick-2018-rtb101] have not been integrated into the broader preclinical evidence base through a unified analytic framework. This paper addresses these gaps by conducting a pre-registered, multi-level synthesis that extends prior work in three ways: by broadening the species scope, by specifying a richer moderator structure with pre-specified interaction tests, and by applying a systematic robustness protocol (tension matrix) that evaluates the sensitivity of conclusions to analytic choices.

The scope of this synthesis is deliberately bounded. The primary endpoint is lifespan or survival, operationalized as either median or maximum lifespan extension, reported as a ratio or log-transformed effect relative to concurrent controls. Studies examining only surrogate endpoints—such as tumor incidence, body weight, or biomarker trajectories—without a survival or lifespan outcome are excluded. Similarly, the synthesis does not address the broader family of rapalogs outside the specific chemical entities rapamycin and everolimus unless a study provides a direct lifespan comparison for these compounds. These boundary conditions are specified to maintain construct validity and to avoid conflating mechanistic breadth with empirical depth. Non-goals include a comprehensive pharmacokinetic modeling of rapamycin bioavailability across species, a network meta-analysis comparing rapamycin to other lifespan-extending interventions, and an exhaustive review of mTOR signaling biology, all of which are addressed by other dedicated treatments [CIT:lamming-2013-mTOR-aging] [CIT:saxton-2017-mTOR-signaling].

The present synthesis is positioned as an extension and stress-test of prior quantitative work rather than an independent line of inquiry. Where previous meta-analyses estimated a single pooled effect or tested a limited moderator set [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin] [CIT:nadon-meta-analysis-rapamycin], this analysis will employ multilevel models with crossed random effects to account for the non-independence of multiple outcomes reported within and across publications. The analytic framework is specified to estimate between-study heterogeneity (τ²) and the proportion of variance attributable to pre-registered moderators [MODEL:R2-marginal], providing a structured decomposition of the factors that shape rapamycin's longevity effects. By pre-registering the full analysis pipeline—including inclusion criteria, coding protocol, effect size extraction rules, statistical models, and robustness checks—the present work aims to reduce researcher degrees of freedom and to produce results whose evidentiary weight can be assessed transparently [CIT:page-2020-prisma].
