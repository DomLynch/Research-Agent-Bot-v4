## The lens

The absence of A/B-bound evidence facts suggests a critical misalignment: while alpha hints indicate multi-organ aging's mortality gradient (e.g., 5-7 aged organs HR=4.5 from doi:10.1038/s41591-025-03798-1), no primary datasets with standardized biomarkers are available to derive causal mechanisms or intervention thresholds, rendering publishable theses speculative rather than evidence-based.

## Already known — do not publish

- Organ-specific aging clocks exist and correlate with disease risk
- Multi-organ aging increases mortality risk in a dose-dependent manner

## Tensions / contradictions

- Regional mortality spikes (e.g., Ohio Valley vs. New England in doi:10.1001/jama.2019.16932) lack direct linkage to organ-specific aging biomarkers, creating geographic confounders
- Contrast between brain aging's protective HR=0.26 (comparable to APOE2) and immune system aging's HR=0.58 from the same study (doi:10.1038/s41591-025-03798-1) implies organ heterogeneity not reconciled in mortality models

## Evidence gaps

- No data on how organ-specific aging clocks interact with environmental factors like those driving regional mortality disparities (e.g., Ohio Valley states)
- Underexplored subgroups include individuals with accelerated single-organ aging (nearly 20% of population per doi:10.1038/s41586-023-06802-1) but without multi-organ involvement
- Lack of dose-response or timing studies for interventions targeting organ aging, such as crystalloid fluid regimens (30 mL/kg from doi:10.1097/ccm.0000000000005337) in aging cohorts
