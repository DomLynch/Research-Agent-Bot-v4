**Snapshot:** 2026-05-16T11-41-29Z

## The lens

Autophagy's context-dependent modulation by lncRNAs and ubiquitin tagging reveals divergent prognostic and therapeutic implications across pathologies, challenging the notion of a uniform autophagic response. This framing shifts focus from generic autophagy activation to specific molecular arbitrators that could enable targeted interventions in cancer and infectious disease.

## Already known — do not publish

- Cytoprotective autophagy enhances cancer cell survival during therapy
- Selective autophagy targets intracellular pathogens like Mtb for clearance
- Autophagy-related lncRNAs serve as biomarkers in lung adenocarcinoma

## Tensions / contradictions

- The strong prognostic association of autophagy-related lncRNAs in lung adenocarcinoma (HR 1.215-1.256) [7110,7112] contrasts with the cytoprotective role of autophagy in glioma therapy resistance [8399], indicating cancer-type-specific autophagy roles that may inform tailored therapeutic strategies.
- Reduced autophagy flux (-26%) in traumatic muscle injury [28274] versus efficient ubiquitin-tagged autophagy of Mtb (30% targeting) in macrophages [5229] suggests tissue-specific autophagy efficiency that could be exploited for regenerative or antimicrobial purposes.

## Evidence gaps

- The mechanistic role of autophagy-related lncRNAs beyond lung adenocarcinoma, such as in other NSCLC subtypes or therapy-resistant cancers.
- Non-autophagic functions of beclin 1 in epithelial barrier integrity (e.g., tight junction regulation), inspired by alpha hints but unaddressed in evidence.
- Comparative analysis of autophagy flux dynamics across injury models (e.g., freeze vs. mechanical) and species (mice vs. human tissues).
