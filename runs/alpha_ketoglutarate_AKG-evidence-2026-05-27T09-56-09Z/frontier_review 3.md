# Frontier review — alpha_ketoglutarate_AKG

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-27T09-56-09Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The non-obvious angle is AKG's paradoxical role as both a TCA cycle fuel and a signaling molecule with dose-specific, species-dependent outcomes that may mask translational risks in human aging interventions.

## Already known — do not publish

- AKG serves as an intermediate in the citric acid cycle and influences amino acid metabolism

## Tensions / contradictions

- Drosophila studies report lifespan extension at 5 μM AKG (aging.102045), whereas murine studies use 2% AKG to increase muscle mass (srep26802) without longevity data, creating a disconnect between dose scaling and functional endpoints.

## Evidence gaps

- Lack of human clinical trials on AKG dosing (e.g., millimolar vs. dietary percentages) for age-related decline, and unexplored interactions with biomarkers like L-octanoylcarnitine (fphar.2020.600561).

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- The evidence is confined to preclinical models with inconsistent doses and species, lacking controlled human data to support any mechanistic or therapeutic thesis.
