# Frontier review — longevity

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-14T16-44-41Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The non-obvious convergence of genetic and regulatory mechanisms across taxa—from nematodes to ants—challenges the low heritability narrative in human longevity, suggesting that evolutionary trade-offs and conserved pathways like insulin/IGF-1 and Nrf2 may be masked by environmental buffering or epistatic interactions.

## Already known — do not publish

- Genetics and environment influence longevity
- Longevity varies across species
- Exercise and diet can extend lifespan

## Tensions / contradictions

- Between the low heritability of human longevity (<10% in fact 7) and strong genetic associations in model organisms (e.g., 41 FOXO3 SNPs in fact 4, daf-2 mutations in C. elegans in fact 11).
- Between interventions effective in invertebrates (e.g., 22.29% lifespan extension with OLE in C. elegans in fact 17) and the lack of translated evidence in mammals.
- Between ecological longevity data (e.g., honey-fed wasps in fact 20) and medical-focused human studies, raising questions about universal vs. species-specific mechanisms.

## Evidence gaps

- Dose-response relationships and optimal timing for interventions like fibrates (fact 19) or calcium (fact 6) in mammalian models.
- The interaction between pathogen resistance genes (84% overlap in fact 8) and lifespan in non-model vertebrates or humans.
- Role of structural traits (e.g., pastern angles in horses in fact 9) in longevity across taxa, beyond traditional genetic pathways.

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- Inclusion of non-mammalian data (e.g., rose flowers in fact 6, wasps in fact 20) may not be directly relevant to human longevity interventions.
- Heterogeneity in endpoints (e.g., post-capture longevity in fact 1 vs. human mortality risk in fact 14) limits cross-study comparability.
- Potential bias toward positive results in model organism studies (e.g., C. elegans in facts 11, 17) may inflate effect sizes in meta-analyses.

## Suggested next extractions

- Human cohort studies with genetic data on FOXO3 and Nrf2 pathways, focusing on dose-response to environmental factors like diet or activity.
- Intervention studies in mammals (e.g., mice or primates) with standardized doses for compounds like OLE or fibrates, linked to molecular markers.
- Longitudinal data on structural traits (e.g., pastern angles) in livestock or companion animals to explore biomechanical longevity determinants.
