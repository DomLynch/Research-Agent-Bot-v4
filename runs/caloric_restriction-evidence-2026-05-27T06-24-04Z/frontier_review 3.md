# Frontier review — caloric_restriction

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-27T06-24-04Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

The efficacy of caloric restriction is not a monolithic phenomenon but is critically modulated by ancillary interventions such as resistance training, circadian feeding alignment, and baseline metabolic state, revealing a complex interplay that challenges simplistic dose-response assumptions.

## Already known — do not publish

- Caloric restriction extends lifespan in male C57BL/6J mice (fact 161504)
- Time-restricted feeding combined with caloric restriction induces weight loss in overweight/obese individuals (fact 183811)

## Tensions / contradictions

- In obese elderly, caloric restriction alone reduces lean body mass, but adding resistance training mitigates 93.5% of this loss (fact 100758) versus a non-significant trend in strength/LBM ratio (fact 100761)
- 30% caloric restriction in young male mice improves glucose tolerance (fact 162990), while moderate caloric restriction in human type 2 diabetes patients yields only a 6.9% remission rate (fact 185351), highlighting species or dose disparities

## Evidence gaps

- The interaction between circadian alignment and caloric restriction in human populations, beyond murine models (fact 161503)
- The differential effects of caloric restriction on lean body mass in non-obese adults (fact 146839) versus obese subgroups
- The dose-threshold for type 2 diabetes remission with caloric restriction, as only moderate reductions are characterized (fact 185351)

## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- Overreliance on murine models for human health implications, as seen in lifespan studies (facts 161503, 161504)
- Limited sample sizes and heterogeneity in human trials, such as the 2-year study in non-obese adults (fact 146839)
