# Frontier review — resveratrol

**Snapshot:** 2026-05-14T16-47-57Z
**Strategist model:** mimo-v2.5-pro

## The lens

Resveratrol research is fragmented by extreme condition-specificity, where efficacy is dictated by precise formulation, dose, and disease context, leading to paradoxical outcomes that undermine generalized therapeutic claims and demand a precision medicine approach.

## Already known — do not publish

- resveratrol is a natural polyphenol with general antioxidant properties
- resveratrol is frequently studied for its role in cardiovascular and cancer health

## Tensions / contradictions

- Fact [3] and [8] from Critical Reviews in Food Science and Nutrition show no significant BP effects, while fact [22] from Phytotherapy Research indicates significant FMD improvement, suggesting vascular benefits may be BP-independent.
- Fact [6] demonstrates synergistic cytotoxicity with curcumin in MCF-7 cells (IC50 8.29 µg/ml), but fact [21] shows standalone inhibition in colon cancer cells (HCT116 IC50 50 µM), highlighting cell-type and combination therapy disparities.
- Fact [16] from Diabetes Obesity and Metabolism reports no liver fat reduction in resveratrol group versus placebo, contrasting with implied metabolic benefits in other facts like antioxidant capacity (fact [5]).

## Evidence gaps

- Optimal dosing for specific outcomes: e.g., 50 µM for sperm quality (fact [7]) vs. 100 µM for amino acid levels (fact [19]), with no consensus.
- Human trials in postmenopausal populations for bone density: fact [13] shows positive effects, but subgroup analyses and long-term data are sparse.
- Bioavailability and formulation effects: nanodispersion (fact [2]) vs. standard resveratrol, with limited comparative clinical studies.

## Paper theses

### #1 — opportunity 100 · `scoping-review`

**Thesis:** Precision Dosing and Formulation in Resveratrol Therapy: A Scoping Review of Condition-Specific Efficacy

- novelty 80 / evidence_strength 60 / reviewer_risk 45
- **Why publishable:** This review would systematically categorize resveratrol's efficacy based on dose, formulation, and disease context, addressing fragmentation in the literature and guiding clinical trial design for targeted applications.

### #2 — opportunity 63 · `meta-analysis-standard`

**Thesis:** Meta-Analysis of Resveratrol's Vascular Effects: Focusing on Flow-Mediated Dilation vs. Blood Pressure

- novelty 75 / evidence_strength 55 / reviewer_risk 65
- **Why publishable:** By contrasting negative BP findings (facts [3], [8]) with positive FMD and CVR results (facts [22], [24]), this analysis could uncover mechanisms of vascular protection independent of BP, offering a nuanced perspective for cardiovascular applications.


## Reviewer objections to anticipate

- High heterogeneity in study designs, including in vitro vs. in vivo models and variable dosing, limits the ability to draw robust conclusions.
- Lack of standardized outcome measures and treatment durations across studies complicates comparison and increases risk of bias.
- Over-reliance on surrogate endpoints (e.g., FMD) without long-term clinical outcome data may overstate therapeutic potential.

## Suggested next extractions

- Human clinical trials with consistent resveratrol doses (e.g., 150-500 mg/day) and formulations in postmenopausal women for bone density outcomes.
- Studies examining resveratrol's synergistic effects with other polyphenols (e.g., curcumin) in specific cancer subtypes, with defined molecular targets like Ki67 (fact [15]).
- Research on resveratrol's impact on liver fat content in obese or diabetic populations, addressing contradictions from fact [16].
