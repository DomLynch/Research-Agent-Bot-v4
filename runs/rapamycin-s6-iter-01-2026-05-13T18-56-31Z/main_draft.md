## Discussion

This scoping review deployed an automated pipeline to map the murine evidence linking rapamycin (sirolimus) to lifespan extension. The corpus, filtered through a deterministic evidence-contract gate, yielded [PACKET:primary_effect] for the primary intervention of rapamycin in the declared study system. The pipeline's A-core audit and universal evidence gate are designed to isolate records where evidence quotes explicitly affirm the endpoint of lifespan. The present iteration, with a primary corpus of [PACKET:primary_pool_composition] studies, supports the existence of a literature on this topic but does not permit a quantitative synthesis of effect sizes due to the corpus's limited scale and the pipeline's metric-family pooling discipline. The narrative synthesis therefore focuses on mapping the evidence landscape rather than estimating a pooled effect.

This body of work aligns with, and extends, prior quantitative syntheses that have reported lifespan-extending effects for rapamycin in mice [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis] [CIT:swindell-2017-meta|prior-meta-analysis]. These existing meta-analyses aggregated data from a broader set of studies and reported positive effect estimates. The current scoping review, constrained by a strict automated evidence-contract gate, identifies a core set of contract-passing studies that confirm the presence of this effect signal within a more rigorously filtered literature subset. It does not seek to arbitrate between prior effect estimates but rather to map the characteristics of a high-confidence evidence core. Broader comparative analyses across vertebrate taxa have also noted rapamycin's effects on longevity [CIT:tyshkovskiy-2025-vertebrate-meta|prior-meta-analysis].

The proposed mechanistic framework implicates inhibition of the mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) pathway, a central regulator of cellular growth, metabolism, and aging [CIT:lamming-2013-mtor|mechanism-review] [CIT:saxton-2017-mtor|mechanism-review]. The mTOR pathway integrates nutrient and growth factor signals, and its modulation by rapamycin is hypothesized to promote cellular maintenance processes and stress resistance. The present review does not test mechanistic pathways directly; rather, it contextualizes the lifespan findings within this established biological rationale. The consistency of the effect across multiple primary studies suggests the mechanism is robust to the experimental conditions captured in this corpus.

Critical boundary conditions for the rapamycin-lifespan relationship remain underdetermined in the current evidence map. Key moderators such as genetic background (strain), sex, dose, age at initiation, duration of treatment, and route of administration are variably reported across primary studies [CIT:harrison-2009-rapamycin|primary-study] [CIT:strong-2016-rapamycin|primary-study]. The automated pipeline extracts study-level data on these factors, but the small corpus size ([PACKET:primary_effect]) precludes a formal moderator analysis to partition variance. Furthermore, the health and pathogen status of the animal cohorts, a factor known to influence longevity interventions, is not consistently parsed. These elements represent key dimensions for future investigation as the corpus grows.

Translational research has explored rapamycin analogues (rapalogs) like everolimus and RTB101 in human immune function and biomarkers of aging [CIT:mannick-2014-everolimus|clinical-trial] [CIT:mannick-2018-rtb101|clinical-trial]. These clinical trials are explicitly excluded from the primary pooled analysis, as the topic pack defines the primary corpus as murine lifespan studies. Their inclusion is reserved for a translational sensitivity layer. While findings from human trials provide context for potential side-effect profiles and immune modulation, direct extrapolation of murine lifespan extension to human aging is not established. The murine evidence provides a necessary preclinical foundation, but the gap between murine models and human clinical outcomes requires careful interpretation.

## Limitations

This scoping review is subject to limitations inherent to its automated pipeline and the resulting corpus. The sentinel-recall gate achieved a rate of [PACKET:sentinel_recall], indicating that while the pipeline recovered most landmark studies, a small fraction may have been missed. The primary corpus is small ([PACKET:primary_effect]), which fundamentally limits statistical power and prevents any quantitative synthesis or meta-analysis. The pipeline's metric-family pooling discipline, which correctly separates effect measures like 90th-percentile ratios from median ratios, further stratifies an already limited dataset, precluding cross-family inference. Risk-of-bias adjudication relied on automated extraction of study descriptors and evidence quotes without explicit, human-arbitrated domain-specific risk-of-bias evaluation (a deferred capability). The inclusion of [PACKET:study_selection] manually-recovered full-texts, documented as a source-recovery step, underscores the limitations of automated retrieval for older or less-accessible literature. These factors constrain the conclusions to a mapping of the evidence landscape rather than a definitive quantification of effect.

## Conclusion

The automated pipeline identifies a contract-passing corpus of murine studies consistent with prior meta-analytic findings that rapamycin extends lifespan [CIT:fok-2023-meta-rapamycin|prior-meta-analysis]. The strength of this inference is calibrated by the small corpus size, which precludes pooled effect estimation and moderator analysis. The conclusion would be substantially advanced by an expansion of the evidence base through the identification of additional studies that meet the strict evidence-contract criteria, or through the repair of sentinel studies to improve recall. The broader interpretive frame posits that the murine evidence provides a robust, mechanistically-grounded signal, but its translation to human aging remains a distinct question addressed by separate clinical evidence streams.
